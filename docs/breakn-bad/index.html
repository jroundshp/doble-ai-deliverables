Revision 2 · what changed and why the score moved

The first version of this report, dated 14 August, compared Break'n Bad against Layton Sports Cards, The Clubhouse, Triple Diamond and the card shops holding map listings in Rochester and South Bend. That comparison was not like for like, and it penalised Break'n Bad on local signals that none of its actual peers maintain either. The digital score moves from 3.4 to 3.8 on that re-weighting alone, almost all of it in the Google Business Profile category.

Nothing else improved. Every technical finding below was re-fetched live on 15 August 2026 and every one still stands, including the blank pages, the empty collections, the mismatched collection titles and the fictional phone number. The peer correction makes the case sharper, not softer: the businesses Break'n Bad is genuinely level with have all built the thing it is missing.

The Core Gap — The Site Launched Before It Was Finished, and Nobody Came Back

The foundation is genuinely good. Shopify hosting answers in 55 milliseconds, the security headers are correct, www and the .myshopify.com address both redirect properly, the mobile viewport allows pinch-to-zoom, and every AI crawler we tested (GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Googlebot) receives a clean 200. Nothing is blocking anybody. There are also five genuinely well-chosen blog articles already written, on exactly the questions a new collector types.

What sits on top of that foundation is unfinished. 22 of the site's 26 pages render the same 15 words, byte for byte: the "Join Our Lives" platform block. That includes the refund policy, the shipping policy, the privacy policy, the terms of service, the FAQ, the testimonials page, the card breaking glossary and the live schedule. The text for those pages exists inside Shopify, because we can read fragments of it in the auto-generated meta descriptions, but the page template never puts it on the screen. A customer who clicks "refund policy" before a $20 buy-in sees nothing at all.

Alongside that: every one of the eight products is Sold Out, so the shop cannot take money. All seven collections are empty, and three of them carry the wrong name entirely, so /collections/nfl is headed "What We Break" and /collections/mlb is headed "Contact." The published phone number on every page is +1 (555) 010-2026, which is inside the range reserved for fiction. The homepage headline is still selling a drawing at a convention that ended on 2 August. The Instagram, X and TikTok icons in the footer link to href="", and the copyright line reads "[2026]" with the brackets still in it.

None of this is a rebuild. It is a template fix, an inventory setting and a launch checklist that was never run.

01 Business Snapshot
What It Is
A live sports card breaking business, founded 2021, streaming daily across three platforms: Fanatics Live (fanatics.live/shops/breakn-bad), Whatnot (whatnot.com/user/breaknbad) and eBay Live (ebay.com/str/breaknsupplies). The signature offer is a $20 flat rate break with free shipping. Six breakers are named on the team page: Alex, Sam, Isaiah, Michael, Jaeger and Grady.
Where It Is, According to Its Own Site
Three different answers. The About page says "just outside of Rochester, Minnesota" and mentions an expansion to Notre Dame in South Bend, Indiana. Every product page says "Ships within 24 hrs from South Bend, IN." The Collector's Club sign-up page says South Bend. The contact page gives no address at all, and no hours. There is no single place on the site where a customer can learn where this business actually is.
The Platform Dependency
All three revenue channels are rented. Breaks are sold on Fanatics Live, Whatnot and eBay Live, and the breaknbad.com store sells only merchandise: eight items, all currently unbuyable. There is not one break, spot or slot purchasable on the owned domain. If a platform changes its fee structure, its algorithm or its terms, there is no direct channel to fall back on.
The Site Itself
Shopify, on a custom theme (bnb-base.css), Archivo Black and Inter, black-and-orange, and it looks sharp. The build quality of what renders is good. The problem is coverage: only four page templates actually do anything, and 22 pages fall through to a default that displays nothing the page was written to say.
Content Already Written
Five real articles, and the topic selection is smart: What is Card Breaking, A Complete Beginner's Guide, Fanatics Live vs Whatnot, Which Platform Should You Use, How Instant Rips Work, How to Submit Cards for Grading and What Are Live Sports Card Breaks. These are the exact questions a first-time buyer asks. They are also scattered across four separate blog sections, with the grading article reachable at five different URLs.
Measurement in Place
Effectively none. No GA4, no Google Tag Manager, no Meta pixel, no email or SMS platform tag. Only Shopify's built-in analytics is running. For a business whose entire model is moving an audience from a rented live stream to an owned property, there is currently no way to see whether anyone makes that trip.
02 Competitive Set

The right comparison for Break'n Bad is not the card shop on the other side of Rochester. It is the other national streaming breakers who rip all day on Whatnot and Fanatics Live and ship nationwide: WeTheHobby, Swish Breaks, Backyard Breaks and Blez Sports Cards. Measured against that set, the picture changes completely, and it changes in Break'n Bad's favour on the hard part.

Level-Setting: This Is Not a Small Business

Break'n Bad is a Whatnot Premier Shop with 58,500 reviews and 524,100 items sold. Against the four breakers named above, that is second of five on review count and second of five on items sold. More reviews than Swish Breaks, more reviews than Backyard Breaks, more items sold than WeTheHobby. This is a top-tier operation by the only metric the hobby actually keeps score with.

Which makes the rest of this report a different document than it first appears. The problem is not that Break'n Bad is small and needs to grow. The problem is that a business with half a million completed transactions owns a website that cannot complete one.

How this peer set was chosen

The four breakers above were selected on a single criterion: they compete with Break'n Bad for the same viewer at the same hour on the same platforms. All five stream daily on Whatnot or Fanatics Live or both, all five ship nationally, and all five sit in the same order of magnitude on transaction volume. Revision 1 of this report used a set drawn from whoever live search happened to return, which produced full-service retail shops and regional operations alongside a business several times their size. Search results are a measure of who ranks, not of who competes.

Deliberately excluded: local card shops in either city, and the platforms themselves. Both appear further down where they are relevant, neither belongs in a like-for-like comparison.

03 Where They Stand

Break'n Bad has the two assets that are hardest to manufacture: a live audience and a brand with a voice. Five years of streams, six breakers, a memorable name and a $20 price point that is genuinely easy to say out loud. What it does not have is anywhere for that audience to land. The website is the one asset it owns outright, and right now it is the weakest thing in the operation.

Real Strengths
Fast, secure, correctly-configured Shopify hosting with a 55ms response. A clean, distinctive visual brand. Presence on all three major breaking platforms. A clear, repeatable offer at $20 flat rate with free shipping. Five well-targeted articles already written. Every AI crawler is welcome and unblocked.
The Drag
A site that launched with placeholder content still in it and was never swept. A fictional phone number, a bracketed template token in the copyright, three dead social icons, and a homepage headline still promoting a convention that ended two weeks ago. Each one is small. Together they read as abandoned.
The Open Flank
Total dependence on rented platforms, with no owned channel that can transact, no email or SMS capture on the domain, and no analytics to measure the handoff. Three of the four breakers at this level already sell on their own domain, and the fourth is mid-rebuild. Break'n Bad has the second-largest transaction record in that group and the only storefront that cannot take an order.
The Finding That Explains The Rest

We pulled all 26 pages listed in the site's own sitemap, stripped the navigation and footer, and hashed what was left. 22 of them are identical. Not similar. The same 15 words, in the same order: "JOIN OUR LIVES / Watch on Fanatics / Watch on WhatNot / Watch on eBay Live." The refund policy, shipping policy, privacy policy, terms of service, FAQ, testimonials, card breaking glossary, live schedule and "What We Break" pages all return that identical block. We confirmed it twice, once with a raw fetch and once by rendering the pages in a real browser so that nothing loaded by JavaScript was missed.

The content is not lost. Shopify auto-generates a meta description from a page's body text, and those descriptions are still populated. The FAQ page's description begins "When will new group breaks be added? New group breaks (i.e. old favor..." and the shipping page's begins "Shipping for any of our breaks is ALWAYS free as a courtesy to all of...". That text is sitting in the Shopify admin right now. It reaches the <head> of the document and never reaches the screen. One page template is dropping the body.

The sharpest way to say it, measured on 15 August 2026: a working page on breaknbad.com has a 15-word body. A page that does not exist has a 12-word body, reading "404, the page you were looking for does not exist." Three words separate a live page on this site from a missing one. For comparison, fetched the same day: the FAQ at Backyard Breaks returns 4,251 words and its refund policy 222; WeTheHobby returns 1,044 and 474.

Separately, the Shopify-native policy URLs (/policies/refund-policy and the other three) all return 404, which means the policies were entered as custom pages rather than in Shopify's policy fields, and are therefore not attached to checkout either. A buyer has no way to read the terms of a $20 purchase before or during checkout.

04 Online Presence Audit
Hosting & InfrastructureA
Nothing to fix. Shopify on Cloudflare, HTTP/2, 55ms time to first byte, valid SSL with HSTS, a content security policy, X-Frame-Options: DENY, and correct 301s from both www.breaknbad.com and breakn-bad.myshopify.com to the apex domain. The 404 handler returns a real 404. This is the part of the stack that is doing its job.
Crawler AccessA
Checked deliberately, because a blocked crawler would explain the invisibility for free. It does not. GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot and Googlebot each receive a normal 200 with a full-size response identical to a browser's. robots.txt is Shopify's standard file and correctly declares the sitemap. Nothing is being kept out. The engines can read this site; there is just very little on it to read.
Page Templates & Content RenderingF
The headline failure. 22 of 26 pages render byte-identical bodies of 15 words. Only the homepage, About, The Team and Contact display real content. Every policy page, the FAQ, the testimonials, the glossary and the live schedule are blank to a human and blank to a crawler. Verified in a rendered browser, not just a raw fetch.
Store & PurchasabilityF
All eight products show "Sold Out." The store cannot take an order today. Beyond stock: six of the eight have a title tag that disagrees with the product name on the page (/products/bnb-sweatshirt is headed "BnB Archive Collection 1/100" but titled "BnB Sweatshirt"), three share the identical description "Break'n Bad cotton tee," and a crewneck sweatshirt is priced the same $35 as a t-shirt.
Collections & MerchandisingF
All seven collections contain zero products. Three carry the wrong name outright: /collections/nfl is headed "What We Break," /collections/mlb is headed "Contact," /collections/nba is headed "Testimonials," and those wrong names are in the title tags Google shows. A leftover Shopify demo collection, media-and-entertainment-example-products, is live and listed in the sitemap.
Structured Data / SchemaF
Zero application/ld+json blocks across every URL we fetched: homepage, all 8 products, all 7 collections, all 26 pages and every blog article. No Product markup means no price, availability or rating can appear in a Google result. No Organization or LocalBusiness means no machine has a structured record of who this business is. Both Shopify peers, Backyard Breaks and WeTheHobby, ship Organization and WebSite blocks on the homepage and carry schema through to their policy and FAQ pages. On the same platform, with the same theme controls available. This is a settings gap, not a development project.
Placeholder Content & Launch QAF
The site shipped with template filler still in place. The phone number on every page is +1 (555) 010-2026, wired into a live tel: link, and 555-01XX is the block reserved for fiction. The copyright line reads "[2026] // LIVE STREAMING MEMORABILIA", brackets included. The Instagram, X and TikTok footer icons are anchors with href="", so they reload the page. A launch checklist would have caught all three in ten minutes.
Homepage & TimelinessD
The <h1>, the first thing a search engine reads, is "ENTER IN A $2,000 CREDIT DRAWING" for the National Sports Card Convention, which ran 29 July to 2 August 2026 in Rosemont and finished twelve days ago. The page never states what the business does, where it is, when it streams or how a break works. The title tag reads "Break'n Bad – Break'n Bad" and there is no meta description, no Open Graph and no Twitter card, so every share of this URL renders as a bare link.
Information ArchitectureF
Duplicates everywhere. /pages/about-1 and /pages/about-us-1; /pages/the-team and /pages/the-team-1, which are byte-identical; three separate live pages (live-stream, live-streams, live-schedule). Nine theme utility pages are indexable, including set-password, profile-edit, edit-notifications, reward and a store-locator for a business with no store. Twenty of the 26 pages are unreachable from the navigation.
Blog StructureD
Good writing in a broken container. Five articles are split across four separate blog sections, three of which (how-to-submit-cards-for-grading, -1, -2, -3) are empty index pages duplicating an article's title. The grading article is reachable at five URLs competing with each other. None of the articles carries a written meta description, an author, a visible date or Article schema.
Google Business ProfileC−
Re-graded up from F on 15 August 2026. No profile exists in Rochester, Minnesota or South Bend, Indiana, and that is still true. But the peer check changes what it is worth: of the four national breakers Break'n Bad actually competes with, only WeTheHobby holds a Google Business Profile, and only because it operates a physical shop. A streaming breaker shipping nationwide is not losing revenue to a walk-in store. The one reason to file anyway is the name collision: WeTheHobby is headquartered in Rochester, New York and holds the 4.8-rated "sports card store" listing that a Rochester query surfaces.
Name, Address, Phone ConsistencyF
Three conflicting stories on one domain: About says Rochester, Minnesota, every product page says "ships from South Bend, IN," and the Contact page gives no address whatsoever. The only phone number published is fictional. The eBay storefront trades as "breaknsupplies" rather than Break'n Bad. Search engines confirm a business identity by matching these signals across sources, and there is nothing here to match.
AI & Search VisibilityF
Absent from every buyer-intent question tested: best breakers to buy into online, where to join a $20 flat rate break, who to trust for NFL team breaks, and card breakers near Rochester. Named instead: Fanatics Live, Whatnot, Layton Sports Cards, Best Card Breaks, Real Sportscards and Iron Lion Collectibles, none of which are peers at Break'n Bad's scale. A direct brand query does find the site, and returns the fictional phone number as the contact. Re-verified 15 August 2026.

The honest caveat, and it matters: of the four real peers, only Blez surfaced on these questions. WeTheHobby, Swish Breaks and Backyard Breaks are largely absent too. Search visibility is not where those breakers beat Break'n Bad, and anyone who tells you the fix here is an SEO retainer is selling the wrong thing. They beat it on owned checkout and on captured audience. Rank is the slow compounding play; the storefront is the one that pays this quarter. See the measurement note below.
Social FootprintD
Real accounts exist and the site mostly fails to link them. The footer offers Facebook (an un-vanitied profile.php?id= URL), YouTube and LinkedIn, which is an unusual choice for a hobby audience, while Instagram, X and TikTok are dead links. The Instagram account @break.n.bad does exist and is linked only from the eight product pages, which are the pages fewest visitors reach. TikTok Shop is discussed in the articles as a rival breaking venue and linked nowhere on the site.
Analytics & MeasurementF
No GA4, no Google Tag Manager, no Meta pixel, no Klaviyo or comparable email and SMS tag. Shopify's built-in analytics is the only instrumentation present. There is currently no way to answer the one question the business most needs answered: how many people leave a Whatnot stream and arrive here.
Collector's Club (Data Capture)C-
The single best strategic idea on the site, executed on the wrong domain. The QR code on the merch shirts leads to a sign-up collecting name, email, mobile, birthday and zip. It is hosted at collectors-club-henna.vercel.app, an auto-generated address that looks like a phishing link to a collector who has just met the brand, sits outside the site's own privacy policy, and passes none of its SEO value back to breaknbad.com.
Trust & ProofD
The proof on the page cannot be checked by a reader. Three About-page testimonials are credited to handles (@RIPPER_JAX99, @CARD_QUEEN_TX, @MIKE_THE_MINT) with no platform, link or date. Two team-page quotes are attributed to "Matthew" and "Chris," neither of whom is among the six breakers named directly above them. Meanwhile the real proof, the platform ratings and sales history on Fanatics Live and Whatnot, appears nowhere on the site.
Mobile & Page ExperienceB
Solid. The viewport is declared correctly and does not lock pinch-to-zoom, which is an accessibility failure common in this class of build. Fonts are preloaded, the homepage carries only nine images and every one has descriptive alt text. Marks off only because one breaker's photo is missing from the crew carousel.
How the AI Visibility Result Was Measured, and Its Limits

These findings come from running buyer-intent questions through live, AI-summarised web search and recording which businesses are named and which sources are cited, plus headless Google Maps queries for the profile check. That is solid evidence of what the engines currently surface. It is not the screenshot-verified sweep of ChatGPT, Perplexity, Grok and Google AI Overviews in logged-out sessions that we normally deliver: those four blocked automated access from our end today. Read every result here as "not visible from outside" rather than "does not exist."

Recommendation 01 asks the team to run the same five prompts by hand and screenshot the answers. It takes about ten minutes and settles the question with Break'n Bad's own evidence rather than ours. Those five prompts, re-run monthly, are also how you measure whether any of this work lands.

Best breakers to buy into online: Fanatics Live, Whatnot, Layton Sports Cards, Best Card Breaks, and a scattering of individual Fanatics shops. Where to join a $20 flat rate break: Whatnot, Fanatics Live, Cardlines, Iron Lion Collectibles. Read that list carefully, because the obvious conclusion is the wrong one. Break'n Bad is missing from all four answers, but so are WeTheHobby, Swish Breaks and Backyard Breaks, each of which has moved roughly half a million items. The names being returned are mostly platforms, aggregator articles and mid-size shops with tidy websites. What this proves is that nobody at the top of this category has solved search, which makes it an open lane rather than a deficit to catch up on. It also means the work below should be sequenced with the storefront first and the ranking second.

The pattern in what gets cited matters as much as who gets named. The recurring sources are Cardlines, Hall of Cards, Baseball America, Grumpy Dad Cards, Yelp, Facebook and the platforms' own help content. That gives the repair path two tracks. On-site: publish answers a machine can quote, starting with the blank pages. Off-site: get Break'n Bad present, named and correctly described inside the hobby publications and directories the engines already read. Unlinked brand mentions count here, not only links.

One further note worth acting on quickly. A brand-name query does return breaknbad.com, and the answer it generates repeats +1 (555) 010-2026 as the way to contact this business. The fictional number is now being propagated by machines, which is a different and worse problem from simply having it on a page.

05 Priority Recommendations
01 Do These Four Things Today, Before Anything Else
Four fixes, under an hour combined, each of which is currently costing money. One: replace +1 (555) 010-2026 with the real number, or remove the phone line entirely if the business does not take calls. It is on every page and inside a live tel: link, and AI answers are now repeating it. Two: mark the eight products back in stock, or hide the shop; a storefront that says Sold Out on every item reads as a closed business. Three: replace the homepage headline, which still promotes a drawing at a convention that ended on 2 August. Four: point the Instagram, X and TikTok footer icons at the real accounts, starting with instagram.com/break.n.bad, which exists and is currently linked only from the eight product pages.
02 Fix the One Page Template That Is Hiding 22 Pages
This is the highest-leverage item in the audit and it is a single template file, not 22 separate repairs. The default page template renders a fixed "Join Our Lives" block and never outputs the page's own body content. The text is not lost: it is in the Shopify admin and still reaching the meta description tag. Whoever built the theme needs to add the page content output back to that template, at which point the refund policy, shipping policy, privacy policy, terms, FAQ, glossary, testimonials and live schedule all reappear at once. While that is open, also move the four policies into Shopify's native policy fields under Settings, so they attach to checkout and the /policies/ URLs stop returning 404.
03 Run the AI Test Yourselves. Ten Minutes, and It Frames Everything Else
Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, Grok and a plain Google search for the AI Overview at the top. Use a logged-out or private window so your own history does not colour the result. Type what a collector types: "best sports card breakers to buy into online" · "where can I join a $20 flat rate sports card break" · "recommend a trustworthy breaker for NFL team breaks on Whatnot" · "card breakers near Rochester Minnesota" · and finally "what do you know about Break'n Bad sports cards". Screenshot each answer. Note who gets named and which sites get cited. That last prompt is the revealing one, because it tests whether the engines know this business exists under its own name and what they say about it. Re-run the same five every month; the change in those answers is the scoreboard for all the work below.
04 Sell Breaks on Your Own Domain
This is the strategic item, and it is the one that changes the business rather than the website. Every dollar currently runs through Fanatics Live, Whatnot or eBay, and the owned store sells only t-shirts. Backyard Breaks runs nine break formats as products on the same Shopify platform and keeps 79% of its catalogue in stock. WeTheHobby runs 27. Blez sells Pick Your Team spots with a live price and a working cart. Break'n Bad has moved more items than two of those three and sells none of it on its own domain. Start small: list the recurring formats as real products with a price, a date and a description, keep the live rip itself on whichever platform the audience prefers, and let collectors who find you through search buy a spot without leaving your site. That turns seven empty collections into the actual catalogue and gives every other recommendation here something to point at.
05 Rebuild the Collections and Repair the Product Records
The NFL collection is headed "What We Break," the MLB collection is headed "Contact," the NBA collection is headed "Testimonials," and none of the seven contains a single product. Rename all three to match their handles, fill them, and delete the leftover Shopify demo collection media-and-entertainment-example-products, which is live and in the sitemap. On the products themselves, six of eight have a title tag that contradicts the name on the page, and three tees share the identical one-line description. Give each product its own title, its own description and a real photograph. Ten items, maybe two hours, and it is the difference between a catalogue and a placeholder.
06 Add Structured Data, Which Currently Does Not Exist Anywhere
There is not one ld+json block on this site. Three additions, in order of payback. Organization in the theme layout: legal name, logo, the real phone, the real address, and sameAs links to the Fanatics, Whatnot, eBay, Instagram, YouTube and Facebook accounts, so machines can join the identity into one entity instead of several. Product on product pages with price and availability, which is what lets a Google result show a price. FAQPage on the FAQ once it renders again, which is the single strongest driver of being quoted by an AI assistant. Most Shopify themes ship with all of this; this one has had it removed or never had it.
07 Settle Where the Business Is, Then Claim It on Google
The site currently says Rochester, Minnesota on one page and South Bend, Indiana on another, and gives no address on the page built for exactly that. Decide which location is the customer-facing one, state it on the Contact page with a real address and hours, and make every other mention agree. Then create a Google Business Profile. Weight this correctly: of the four national peers, only WeTheHobby holds one, and only because it runs a retail floor. This is not the gap that is costing money. It is worth ten free minutes for one specific reason, which is that WeTheHobby is in Rochester, New York and owns the profile that "Rochester card breaks" returns. Break'n Bad is in Rochester, Minnesota and is invisible on that query. A service-area profile with no public address resolves the confusion. Do the same tidy-up on the eBay storefront, which trades as "breaknsupplies" and splits the brand in half.
08 Consolidate the Blog and Answer the Questions You Are Losing
The five existing articles are on the right topics; they are just buried. Merge the four blog sections into one, delete the three empty index pages duplicating an article's title, and pick one canonical URL for the grading guide, which currently exists at five addresses competing with one another. Give each article a written meta description, an author and a visible date. Then write against the miss list from Recommendation 03: how a $20 flat rate break actually works, what happens if my team hits nothing, when do cards ship after a break, how to pick a breaker you can trust. Those are the questions where the engines currently answer with someone else's page.
