The Core Gap: Your Website Describes a Different, Much Smaller Company

The foundation underneath is genuinely good, and in one respect it is the best in the peer group. Shopify serves this site faster than any competitor we measured: 32 milliseconds of server processing time, against 96 for Backyard Breaks, 207 for WeTheHobby and 231 for Layton Sports Cards. Both alternate hostnames redirect correctly in a single hop, the canonical tag is right, the mobile viewport allows pinch-to-zoom, every homepage image carries descriptive alt text, and GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Googlebot and OAI-SearchBot each receive a clean 200 with the full page. Nothing is blocking anybody.

What sits on top of that foundation is unfinished. We pulled all 26 pages in the site's own sitemap, stripped navigation and footer, and hashed what was left. Twenty-two of them are byte-for-byte identical: the same fifteen words, "JOIN OUR LIVES / Watch on Fanatics / Watch on WhatNot / Watch on eBay Live." That set includes the refund policy, the shipping policy, the privacy policy, the terms of service, the FAQ, the testimonials, the card breaking glossary, the live schedule and What We Break. A collector who clicks "refund policy" before a $20 buy-in sees nothing at all.

The text is not lost. Shopify builds a page's meta description from its body, and those descriptions are still populated. The FAQ page's begins "When will new group breaks be added? New group breaks (i.e. old favorites that we bring back, mixers, et al) are posted spontaneously throughout the..." and the refund page's begins "Once a live break has begun, there are no refunds. For all other merchandise, order cancellations and returns are subject to a 5% restocking fee...". That writing is sitting in the Shopify admin right now. It reaches the head of the document and never reaches the screen. One page template is dropping the body.

Around that sit the launch leftovers. The phone number in a live tel: link on every page is +1 (555) 010-2026, inside the block reserved for fiction. The first thing on the homepage is still a $2,000 drawing for the National Sports Card Convention, which ended on 2 August, sixteen days ago. A leftover Shopify demo collection is live and listed in the sitemap.

None of this is a rebuild. It is one template fix, a settings pass and a launch checklist that was never run.

01 Business Snapshot
Three Versions of the Same Company

Your media kit, your website and your live shows describe different businesses.

The kit tells a sponsor you are the #3 breaker on Fanatics Live. Your homepage does not contain the word "Fanatics." The kit calls the 100% Buyback Program a first-of-its-kind that no other breaker offers, and the word "buyback" does not appear on any page of breaknbad.com, though it is in the title of the shows you run. The kit lists the Shopify storefront as a sales channel. That storefront currently holds eight sold-out t-shirts and six empty collections.

The kit prints a full street address in South Bend. The website gives no address anywhere, while every product page reads "Ships within 24 hrs from South Bend, IN." We are not recommending you add an address or remove one. We are pointing out that three documents give three answers, and any one of them would be fine on its own.

The kit is also carrying a channel line that has moved on without it: "Primary revenue driver across Fanatics Live and Whatnot." If eBay Live has replaced Whatnot, that sentence is selling a channel you have stepped back from and leaving out one you are actively running. Worth checking the date on the file before the next partnership conversation.

One related question rather than a finding, because we cannot verify it from outside. The kit advertises 210,000+ total cross-platform following, and a large share of that looks like it sits on Whatnot. If that channel is no longer a priority, it is worth confirming what portion of the reach you are quoting to sponsors is on platforms you are currently running.

None of this is a writing problem. Each document was accurate when someone wrote it, and then the business moved faster than the paperwork. The cost is that a sponsor, a customer and an AI assistant researching you will each come away with a different answer about what you do and how big you are.

What It Is
One of the largest sports card breaking companies in the United States. Founded 2021 by Matthew Harfmann, and described in the company's own 2026 media kit as having grown from a dorm-room project into a 94+ employee operation headquartered in South Bend, Indiana, with $20M+ in lifetime revenue. Those two figures are self-reported and we have not verified them independently; the Fanatics Live ranking below is third-party. Live breaks stream on Fanatics Live (fanatics.live/shops/breakn-bad) and eBay Live, which replaced Whatnot as the second channel earlier this summer. #3 of all 148 sellers on Fanatics Live in 2025, ranked by total buyers, in a market where the top 25 breakers control 71.3% of everything. Third place out of 148, in the segment of the market that matters, is the correct frame for everything that follows.
How the Funnel Is Supposed to Work
Selling happens live, on Fanatics Live and eBay Live. That is the model and this report does not argue with it. The job of breaknbad.com and the social accounts is to find people and deliver them into a stream. That is the standard every finding here is measured against, and it is a fairer standard than the one an ordinary SEO audit would apply. The question is never "why does the website not sell more." It is "does the website send anyone to the show."

@break.n.bad carries roughly 16,000 followers across more than 1,500 posts, and it is doing most of the work the website is not. Two things follow from that, and both matter: the destination it points at is the single most valuable fix in this report (Section 03), and the website currently links back to it with a dead anchor.

Shopify on a custom theme (bnb-base.css), Archivo Black and Inter, black and orange, and it looks sharp. The build quality of what renders is good. The problem is coverage. Only four page templates do anything at all, and 22 pages fall through to a default that displays nothing the page was written to say.

Five real articles, and the topic selection is smart: What is Card Breaking, A Complete Beginner's Guide, Fanatics Live vs Whatnot, Which Platform Should You Use, How Instant Rips Work, How to Submit Cards for Grading and What Are Live Sports Card Breaks. When we raised these five articles with someone who works at the company, the response was that they were not aware the company had a blog. That is the clearest possible statement of the problem: if the people who work here cannot find these pages, a first-time buyer and a search crawler have no chance.
Measurement in Place
Effectively none. No GA4, no Google Tag Manager, no Meta pixel, no email or SMS tag anywhere in the page source. Shopify's built-in analytics is the only instrumentation running. The business does capture: an SMS list advertised on Instagram, and a Restock Rocket notify-me widget on every product page. What is missing is any way to measure it. The entire job of this website is to move people into a live show, and not one of those handoffs is instrumented. Nobody can currently answer how many people arrive at breaknbad.com and leave for a Fanatics Live or eBay Live stream, which is the single number this business would most benefit from knowing.

Why the Thin Site Costs You, Even Though You Sell in the Stream

Layton Sports Cards is not a competitor on Fanatics Live and this report is not suggesting you copy its business. It is useful for one narrow reason: it shows what a search engine sees when a domain is full rather than empty.

Layton publishes 7,122 URLs, including a results page for every completed break. Break'n Bad publishes 54, of which 22 render blank. When a collector types "is Break'n Bad legit" or "who has the best $20 breaks," the engines answer from whatever text exists. Layton has given them thousands of pages to quote. Break'n Bad, which is the bigger operation on the platform that matters, has given them almost nothing.

That is the cost of a thin site for a business that sells in the stream: not lost checkout revenue, lost discovery. The buyer you never reach is the one who was researching before they ever opened an app.

What They Are Doing Better
1. Their websites carry the business. VIP Rips built a purpose-made storefront. Layton publishes thousands of pages. Every one of them has more on its own domain than eight sold-out t-shirts. Break'n Bad's own store carries eight t-shirts and beanies, none of them purchasable.

2. Every one of them tells a machine who they are. Five of the six publish Organization and WebSite structured data. All six publish a meta description and social preview tags. Break'n Bad publishes none of the three, and is the only site of the seven with zero structured data anywhere.

3. They publish, and it compounds. Layton has 5,481 break-results pages. WeTheHobby runs a 75-episode podcast archive on its own domain. Backyard Breaks writes buyer-safety and beginner guides. Break'n Bad has five good articles and stopped.

4. Their homepages say what the business does. Layton's homepage carries 1,497 words of server-rendered text, Backyard's 736, WeTheHobby's 520. Break'n Bad's carries 174, and none of them state what a break is, when the streams run, or how to join one.

5. Backyard Breaks captures email on-site. Klaviyo is installed and running on their storefront. Break'n Bad's capture happens on Instagram and on a Vercel subdomain, both outside the property it owns.

6. VIP Rips built a purpose-made storefront. A custom front end on Vercel with a hardened content security policy, backed by Shopify checkout. It shows what the category looks like when someone decides the platform-based platforms are not enough.

The Honest Read on Search

Run the buyer-intent questions a collector actually types and the names that come back are Fanatics Live, Whatnot, Layton Sports Cards, Best Card Breaks, Cardlines and a scattering of individual shops. Break'n Bad is absent. But so are WeTheHobby, Swish Breaks and Backyard Breaks, each of which has moved close to half a million items.

The obvious conclusion is the wrong one. Nobody at the top of this category has solved search, with the partial exception of Layton, whose 7,122 pages are doing the work. This is an open lane rather than a deficit to catch up on, and it means the sequencing matters: put something on the domain worth finding first, then worry about ranking. Anyone who leads with an SEO retainer here is selling the wrong thing in the wrong order.

What gets cited matters as much as who gets named. The recurring sources are Cardlines, Hall of Cards, Baseball America, Sports Collectors Daily and the platforms' own help content. That gives the work two tracks: publish answers on the domain that a machine can quote, and get Break'n Bad named and correctly described inside the hobby publications the engines already read. 03 The Instagram Funnel, and Where It Ends

Instagram is the strongest owned-adjacent channel Break'n Bad has. It carries the audience, the offers, the SMS join and the crew's voice. Everything about the funnel is right up until the last step, and the last step is where this report's single most valuable finding sits.

The Link in the Bio Is Clean. Three of Its Nine Tiles Are Not.

We rendered linktr.ee/breaknbad in a browser on 18 August 2026. It carries nine tiles and all nine are yours: Fanatics Live first, then eBay, Whatnot, Instagram, TikTok, breaknbad.com, YouTube, Twitch and Twitter. No affiliate offers, no third-party brands. The account is on Linktree's free tier and is flagged eligible for their sponsorship programme, but none of it is switched on. Leave it that way.

What is worth fixing is where three of those nine tiles actually land. The TikTok tile points to tiktok.com/@breaknbad, which returns "Couldn't find this account." The YouTube and Twitch tiles point at channels the company confirms are dormant. So a third of the most valuable real estate you own, tapped at the exact moment of highest interest, sends people to a dead page or an empty one.

One ordering note. The only tile carrying a promotional hook is "Get $10 Off Your First Whatnot Purchase!!", and Whatnot is the channel you have stepped back from. The eBay Live tile, which is now doing that work, has no offer attached and sits second. The incentive is pointed at the wrong platform.

Ten minutes of tidying: remove or repoint the three dead tiles, move the offer onto the channel you want the traffic on, and consider whether breaknbad.com deserves to be sixth.

The One Bridge That Works Is Invisible to Machines

The homepage runs a live Instagram feed. It auto-populates as the team posts, and on the day we looked it was carrying the 2026 Topps Chrome Logofractor promo, the Monday giveaway, a Justin Herbert pull and the Sunday "Pulls of the Week" recap. This is the site's best feature and the one place the funnel genuinely reciprocates. A visitor landing on breaknbad.com sees current activity rather than a static page.

The problem is who can see it. The feed is built entirely in the browser after the page loads. Fetch breaknbad.com the way a crawler does and the homepage contains no Instagram content at all, not one reference. Render it in a browser and there are more than 250. Everything in that feed, every promotion and every pull, is invisible to Google, to Bing and to every AI assistant, because none of them execute the script that builds it.

So the most current, most persuasive material on the whole website is doing nothing whatsoever for findability. That is not an argument for removing it. It is an argument for writing the same information into the page itself, where a machine can read it: the current promotion in text, the live schedule in text, the recent hits in text, with the feed sitting alongside as the visual proof.

Underneath that feed sits the contradiction. The Instagram, X and TikTok icons in the footer are anchors with href="", verified in the rendered page, so a click reloads instead of going anywhere. The site has a working Instagram bridge on the homepage and three dead Instagram-and-friends links in the footer of that same page. Facebook, LinkedIn and a dormant YouTube channel are the only footer icons that lead anywhere.

One gap is real and unchanged. The eight product pages carry no link to Fanatics Live, eBay Live or Whatnot at all, which we checked page by page. A visitor deep in the merch catalogue has no route to a live rip from where they are standing.

The Store Is Mid-Transition, and the Relaunch Is the Moment

All eight products show Sold Out. An earlier draft of this report read that as deliberate scarcity. Break'n Bad has since told us it is temporary: new merchandise is coming and the whole approach to the shop is being reworked. That is the right answer, it is better than the one we guessed, and this section is written to be useful for the relaunch rather than to score the current state as a fault.

The single most valuable thing you can do is treat the relaunch as the moment to fix the records, not just the inventory. Everything in Recommendation 08 costs almost nothing while you are already in the product admin and costs real effort as a separate cleanup later: unique titles and descriptions per item, collections named to match what they hold, and the leftover Shopify demo collection deleted. Doing it at relaunch is roughly free. Doing it in six months is a project.

Two things worth fixing before the new stock lands. The only capture on a sold-out page is a rotated tab pinned to the right edge of the screen reading "Notify me when available," set vertically in grey. It reads as a feedback widget rather than an offer, and right now it is the only way an interested buyer can register that they want something. And Restock Rocket already supports pre-orders, which are switched off, so a buyer at peak intent leaves an email address where they could have left a payment. Separately, six of the eight products carry a title tag that disagrees with the name on the page, and a crewneck sweatshirt is priced the same $35 as a t-shirt.

The Collector's Club Is Off-Domain

The QR-code-to-Collector's-Club idea is the best strategic thinking on the site: scan a shirt, sign up, hand over name, email, mobile, birthday and zip. It runs at collectors-club-henna.vercel.app, an auto-generated hosting address that reads as a phishing link to a collector who has just met the brand at a table. It sits outside the site's own privacy policy, outside its analytics, and passes none of its value back to breaknbad.com. Moving it to breaknbad.com/collectors-club is a redirect and a settings change.

04 Under the Hood: SEO and AI Findability

This section is the technical one. It covers what is structurally wrong beneath the design, what search engines can and cannot do with the site as built, and separately what AI assistants can do with it, because those are no longer the same question.

The Structural Fault: One Template Is Dropping the Body

Twenty-two of 26 pages render the same fifteen words. We confirmed it twice, once with a raw fetch and once by rendering in a real browser so nothing loaded by JavaScript was missed. The blank set includes all four policies, the FAQ, the testimonials, the glossary, the live schedule and What We Break.

The sharpest way to say it: a working page on breaknbad.com has a fifteen-word body. A page that does not exist returns a 404. The live pages and the missing pages contain effectively the same amount of readable content. For comparison, fetched the same day, WeTheHobby's refund policy returns 475 words and Backyard's 221.

Separately, the Shopify-native policy URLs (/policies/refund-policy and the other three) all return 404. The policies were entered as custom pages rather than in Shopify's policy fields, which means they are not attached to checkout either. A buyer has no way to read the terms of a purchase before or during it, and this is the one item on the list with a compliance edge to it as well as a commercial one.

This is a single template file, not 22 separate repairs. Whoever built the theme needs to restore the page-content output. The moment that happens, nine pages of already-written material reappear at once.

The SEO Layer: What Google Is Working With

No meta description anywhere on the homepage. No Open Graph tags. No Twitter card. Of the seven sites examined for this report, breaknbad.com is the only one missing all three. Every share of the URL in a text message, a Discord channel or a group chat renders as a bare link with no image and no description. The title tag reads "Break'n Bad – Break'n Bad".

Zero application/ld+json blocks sitewide. Homepage, all eight products, all seven collections, all 26 pages, every blog article. No Product markup means no price, availability or rating can ever appear in a Google result. No Organization markup means no machine has a structured record joining breaknbad.com to the Fanatics Live, eBay, Whatnot, Instagram and Facebook accounts, so the brand reads to a search engine as several loosely related things rather than one business. Five of the six competitors ship this by default.

Duplicate and junk URLs are diluting what little there is. /pages/about-1 and /pages/about-us-1; /pages/the-team and /pages/the-team-1, byte-identical; three separate live pages (live-stream, live-streams, live-schedule). Nine theme utility pages are indexable, including set-password, profile-edit, edit-notifications, reward, referral and a store-locator. Twenty of the 26 pages cannot be reached from the navigation at all.

The blog is fragmented. Five articles across four separate blog sections, three of which are empty index pages duplicating an article's title. The grading article is reachable at several competing addresses. No article carries a written meta description, an author, a visible date or Article markup, and the grading article's title tag appears on five separate URLs. The writing is good; the container is working against it.

Six of seven collections hold zero products, three of them under the wrong name in both the heading and the title tag, and a leftover Shopify demo collection, media-and-entertainment-example-products, is live and sitting in the sitemap.

The net effect: a 54-URL site where roughly six URLs carry real content, competing against a 7,122-URL site in the same category on the same platform.

What a Full Crawl Turns Up That a Look at the Site Does Not

We crawled all 54 URLs in the sitemap, fetched each one, and built the internal link graph. Five defects only show up at that level.

A collection in the navigation returns a 404. /collections/collecting-supplies is linked from seven pages and the page does not exist. Anyone clicking "collecting supplies" lands on an error, and it has been that way long enough to be sitting in the site's own link graph.

Thirty-four of the 54 URLs have no internal link pointing at them at all. Not one. That includes every policy page, the FAQ, the glossary, the live schedule and What We Break. They exist, they are in the sitemap, and there is no route to them from anywhere on the site. A crawler reaches them once through the sitemap and finds fifteen words.

Every product hangs off a single thread. All eight product pages have exactly one inbound link, from the merchandise collection. Every blog article has exactly one, from its section index. The whole site averages 8.4 internal links per page and nearly all of them are navigation.

Fifty-three of 54 URLs serve a live 200 at two different addresses, with and without a trailing slash, and neither redirects to the other. The canonical tag is correct on both forms, which stops this becoming a true duplicate-content problem, so this is a crawl-efficiency and link-equity issue rather than a penalty risk. It is worth a redirect rule the next time the theme is open.

The sitemap is telling Google these pages have not changed since 2021. Thirteen URLs carry a lastmod older than 540 days, including the refund policy and the card breaking glossary at July 2021 and the shipping policy at January 2022. Combined with a fifteen-word body, that is a strong signal to a crawler that there is no reason to come back.

Two more from the same crawl. Duplicate titles and descriptions across the catalogue: three sweatshirts share the title "BnB Sweatshirt" and the description "Heavyweight Break'n Bad crewneck sweatshirt," three tees share "BnB T-Shirt" and "Break'n Bad cotton tee," and the grading article's title appears on five separate URLs. And 46 of the 54 pages fall under 350 words, which includes all eight products at roughly 250 words each, nearly all of it template furniture.

The AI Layer: The Rails Are Already Installed and They Lead Nowhere

This is the finding that will matter most over the next two years, and it is worth reading slowly.

Shopify has already fitted this store for AI shopping agents, at no cost and with no action taken by anyone at Break'n Bad. The robots.txt file advertises an agent instruction document at breaknbad.com/agents.md, a capability endpoint at /.well-known/ucp, and a live commerce endpoint at /api/ucp/mcp. There is a dedicated sitemap_agentic_discovery.xml. The instructions tell any assistant acting for a shopper how to search the catalogue, build a cart and complete a buyer-approved checkout.

Every one of those rails currently leads an AI shopping assistant to eight sold-out t-shirts and six empty collections, which makes the merchandise relaunch the natural moment to get this right. Layton's identical rails lead to 1,592 products. The infrastructure for the next wave of commerce is switched on and pointed at an empty room.

The rest of the AI picture follows from the same root cause. The FAQ content exists in Shopify and renders as a blank page, so the single strongest driver of being quoted by an assistant, a real question-and-answer page with FAQPage markup, is sitting one template fix away from working. There is no Organization record to tell an assistant that the Whatnot shop, the Fanatics shop, the eBay store and this domain are one business. And a brand-name query returns the site with +1 (555) 010-2026 as the contact number, which means the fictional number is now being repeated back by machines. That is a different and worse problem than simply having it on a page.

One free item is missing, and it is the one that matters most for ChatGPT. There is no msvalidate.01 tag on the site and no BingSiteAuth.xml file, so breaknbad.com is not verified in Bing Webmaster Tools. ChatGPT's search grounds on Bing's index rather than Google's. A findability effort that watches only Google Search Console is half blind, and verifying with Bing costs one meta tag and no money.

The good news is unambiguous: nothing is blocked. GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Googlebot and OAI-SearchBot all receive a clean, full-size 200. The engines can read this site. There is just very little on it to read.

What we could not verify from outside, and how to settle it in ten minutes

The findings above on what assistants currently say come from running buyer-intent questions through live AI-summarised web search and recording which businesses are named and which sources are cited. That is solid evidence of what the engines surface. It is not the screenshot-verified sweep of ChatGPT, Perplexity, Grok and Google AI Overviews in logged-out sessions that we would normally include, because all four block automated access. Read every visibility result here as "not visible from outside" rather than "does not exist."

Recommendation 06 sets out how to run the same five prompts by hand, including the per-engine setup, which is not simply "use incognito" for two of the four. It also asks for five different people rather than one, because these systems are not deterministic and a single run is a sample of one. That settles the question with Break'n Bad's own evidence rather than ours, and re-run monthly it is the scoreboard for every other item on the list.

05 Channel by Channel
Hosting & InfrastructureA
Nothing to fix, and the best in the peer group. Shopify behind Cloudflare, HTTP/2, 32ms of server processing time against 96 for Backyard Breaks, 207 for WeTheHobby and 231 for Layton. Valid SSL with HSTS, a content security policy, X-Frame-Options: DENY, and correct single-hop 301s from www.breaknbad.com, breakn-bad.myshopify.com and plain HTTP to the apex domain. The 404 handler returns a real 404 and the canonical tag is correct. This part of the stack is doing its job.

06 Run the AI Test Yourselves. Five People, Ten Minutes Each
This is the cheapest thing on the list and it frames everything else, but it only works if it is set up properly. Done casually it produces a reading of your own search history rather than a reading of the market.

The five prompts. Use them word for word. Rephrasing changes the answer, and the whole value of this test is that it can be repeated later and compared.
  1. "best sports card breakers to buy into online"
  2. "where can I join a $20 flat rate sports card break"
  3. "recommend a trustworthy breaker for NFL team breaks on Whatnot"
  4. "which sports card breakers sell break spots on their own website"
  5. "what do you know about Break'n Bad sports cards"

The fifth is the revealing one. It tests whether the engines know this business exists under its own name, and what they believe about it. Expect the fictional phone number to come back.

Setting up each engine, because "use incognito" is only correct for two of the four.

Google: an incognito window is the right control. It drops your account and your search history. It does not change your location, because Google reads that from your internet connection, so results will still be shaped by where the browser physically is.

Perplexity: works without signing in, so an incognito window is the right control here too.

ChatGPT: incognito will not do it. ChatGPT's Memory is attached to the account rather than the browser, it is switched on by default, and a private window does not touch it. Use Temporary Chat instead, which is the mode that ignores memory and history entirely. If you are signed in, also check Settings for Memory and confirm what it has stored about the business.

Grok: it requires a sign-in, so incognito just produces a login screen. Sign in and start a brand new conversation rather than continuing an existing thread, since an open thread carries its own context.

Now the part that makes the result trustworthy: have five different people do it. These systems are not deterministic. The same question asked twice can name different businesses, so one person running it once is a sample of one, and it can mislead in either direction. Five people means five different accounts, histories and connections, and what you get back is the range rather than a single reading. You have six breakers on the team page, so this is an afternoon, not a project.

If all five come back without Break'n Bad in them, that is a finding nobody can argue with. If two of the five name you, that is a better and more useful finding than one person's single miss, and it tells you which prompts you are already close on.

Keep it comparable. Same five prompts, same order, same day. Screenshot every answer, including the sources or citations panel where the engine shows one, because who gets cited matters as much as who gets named. Put everything in one shared folder labelled by person and engine. One note: if all five people are on the same office connection, Google sees one location for all of them, which is fine as long as you know it.

Re-run the identical set monthly. The movement in those answers is the scoreboard for every other item on this list, and it is the only measurement here that does not require installing anything.
