The opening, in one line

The strongest local competitor, Ruach Resources, has exactly one page targeting this market. Its title tag reads Aggregate & Gravel Supply in New Town MT. The headline on the page reads AGGREGATE PRODUCTS IN NEW TOWN, MT. New Town is in North Dakota.

We checked their other town pages. Parshall, Stanley and Watford City are all correctly labeled ND. The error appears only on New Town, the one town that matters to this project.

01 The Market
Where
New Town sits in Mountrail County on the Fort Berthold Reservation, in the middle of the Bakken. Work connected to the Four Bears Segment falls inside MHA Nation jurisdiction.
Who Buys
Not homeowners. Project managers, dispatchers, batch plants, excavation contractors and oil field service companies. They want to know you have the material, that it lands this week, and what it costs.
Demand, Four Legs
Oil field roads and pads. Tribal and public infrastructure. Commercial and residential building around New Town, Parshall and Stanley. Concrete recycling and washout.
Season
Roughly May through October. The two market plan exists because heavy iron that sits idle five months a year loses money in both directions.
Read the rig count honestly

North Dakota held at 23 to 24 active rigs through spring 2026, with Williston Basin production near 1.28 million barrels a day, about 9.8% of US output. That is a steady, disciplined Bakken. Operators are holding production flat and managing cost, not expanding.

So oil field aggregate demand here is real and repeatable, but it is not about to double. The drilling leg is the flattest of the four. The tribal infrastructure spending and the recycling side are what make this market interesting, and they are the two the operations memo spends the least time on.

The finding that applies to every one of them

Not one competitor in this market has FAQ schema anywhere on its homepage, and Ruach has no FAQ section anywhere on its site at all.

That matters more each month. When a contractor asks an AI assistant "how many tons of Class 5 do I need for a 200 foot access road" or "who delivers scoria near New Town," the answer gets assembled from pages that answered those questions in plain language and marked them up so a machine can read them. In this market, nobody has written those pages. That is an open lane, and it is cheap to take.

04 The Threat Nobody Is Watching

The instinct is to treat Ruach as the competitor and the national producers as the big names. On the evidence, the more serious threat is Hello Gravel, and it is the same name that came up as the top online threat in the Denton audit back in July. The same broker is sitting between this business and its buyers in both markets.

Here is what it has built. Hello Gravel publishes an llms.txt file and runs a live read-only API for AI agents, including a Model Context Protocol endpoint. In their own words, it lets an agent "answer the full pre-purchase chain, size the job, confirm delivery, and price it, using the same engine as the website."

  • Estimate material from length, width and depth
  • Check delivery and available dates for a ZIP code
  • List products available at that ZIP
  • Return an all-in delivered quote, price included
What that actually means

When someone asks an AI assistant where to get forty tons of gravel delivered near New Town, Hello Gravel can hand back a real, priced, deliverable answer without a human being involved. Every other company in this market can offer a phone number and an invitation to call for a quote.

They have no pit, no trucks and no yard within five hundred miles of New Town. They are winning the search anyway, because they built for how buying is starting to happen. A local operator with actual material and actual trucks should not be losing this, and the reason it is being lost has nothing to do with the material.

05 What Fort Berthold Changes

This is the part that separates New Town from any other town of its size, and it is not a marketing question.

Any prime contractor, subcontractor or supplier intending to work on the Fort Berthold Reservation must file a compliance plan and hold a license from the MHA Tribal Employment Rights Office. Applications are accepted in person only at the TERO building in New Town, Monday through Thursday, 8:00 to 3:30. No emailed, faxed or mailed applications. Fees changed on January 1, 2026.

The ordinance also requires preference in contracting to Indian owned firms, strongest for firms wholly owned and controlled by enrolled members, and the TERO director reviews whether a preferred contractor genuinely self performs the work or passes it through.

Sequencing
TERO licensing and the pit and sand arrangements are the long poles. They run on the Nation's calendar and in person. The website is not the long pole and nothing should wait on it.
Positioning
On Fort Berthold, "TERO licensed, hiring locally" is a stronger trust signal to a buyer than any amount of copy about equipment. It belongs on the homepage, not in a footer.
Open question
If there is tribal ownership or partnership in the structure, that is the headline of the whole business, not a compliance note. We need to know before homepage copy gets written.
06 Where Concrete Warrior Starts

Honest summary of the starting position, so nobody is working from a rosier picture than the facts support.

Behind on
Everything visible. No website, no Google Business Profile, no listings, no reviews, no name recognition in the Bakken. Ruach has fourteen years of local history here.
Even on
Answer content and AI readiness, where the entire local field is at zero. And on material specs, which every one of them under-publishes.
Ahead on
Concrete recycling and washout, carried up from the Texas operation. Thin local competition, and it is the one lane with a real operating history behind it.
The realistic read

Nobody catches a fourteen year incumbent on general aggregate searches this season. That is not the play. The play is to be the correct, specific, machine readable answer for New Town, North Dakota, on the exact terms where the incumbent is either absent, wrong about the state, or silent. That is a narrow front and it is winnable in one season.

07 Open Questions Before Anything Is Built
01What entity is Concrete Warrior?
A trade name of Patriot Stone Supply LLC, a separate North Dakota entity, or a joint venture. This decides the domain registrant, the legal name, the Google listing and the TERO application. It is the first fork in the road and everything else waits on it.
02Is there a staffed yard address near New Town?
This is the single biggest variable in the Google plan. A staffed address supports a standard listing, which is what wins "gravel near me" searches. Without one it becomes a service area listing, which works but ranks less strongly. A pit with nobody there is usually not enough.
03Where does TERO licensing stand, and is there tribal ownership?
Gates the work itself, not just the marketing, and changes the positioning entirely if the answer is yes.
04Can the batch plant partner be named publicly?
A named relationship is a real credibility asset in a market built on reliability. An unnamed one is worth nothing externally.
05One phone number and one address, and the material list with honest prices
Confirmed materials with North Dakota specs, price brackets per ton, and a realistic delivery radius so the service area claims are true. Publishing prices is unusual in this industry and it is one of the strongest things the Texas site already does.
