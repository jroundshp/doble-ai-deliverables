The opening, in one line

The strongest local competitor, Ruach Resources, has exactly one page targeting this market. Its title tag reads Aggregate & Gravel Supply in New Town MT. The headline on the page reads AGGREGATE PRODUCTS IN NEW TOWN, MT. New Town is in North Dakota.

We checked their other town pages. Parshall, Stanley and Watford City are all correctly labeled ND. The error appears only on New Town, the one town that matters to this project.

01 The Market
Where
New Town sits in Mountrail County on the Fort Berthold Reservation, in the middle of the Bakken. Work connected to the Four Bears Segment falls inside MHA Nation jurisdiction.
Who Buys
Not homeowners. Project managers, dispatchers, batch plants, excavation contractors and oil field service companies. They want to know you have the material, that it lands this week, and what it costs.
Demand, Four Legs
Oil field roads and pads. Tribal and public infrastructure. Commercial and residential building around New Town, Parshall and Stanley. Concrete recycling and washout.
Season
Roughly May through October. The two market plan exists because heavy iron that sits idle five months a year loses money in both directions.
Read the rig count honestly

North Dakota held at 23 to 24 active rigs through spring 2026, with Williston Basin production near 1.28 million barrels a day, about 9.8% of US output. That is a steady, disciplined Bakken. Operators are holding production flat and managing cost, not expanding.

So oil field aggregate demand here is real and repeatable, but it is not about to double. The drilling leg is the flattest of the four. The tribal infrastructure spending and the recycling side are what make this market interesting, and they are the two the operations memo spends the least time on.

This one lands on our strongest lane

Concrete crushing and recycling is the part of the Texas operation with a real history behind it, and it is the differentiator the operations memo leans on. In New Town, that category is held by one local competitor, in first place, on a single review, with no website connected to its listing.

A complete profile with hours, categories, photographs, a linked site and a handful of genuine reviews would very likely take that position. Not eventually. This season.

Read that again

The entire Google Maps category for sand and gravel supply in New Town is two businesses, and one of them has zero reviews.

In most trades in most towns, the top of the map is defended by an operator with two hundred reviews and it takes years to catch them. Here, a supplier who completes a season of work and asks every customer for a review would hold this market outright. A complete, correctly categorized listing is what gets you into the results at all. Reviews decide the order once you are there, and almost nobody has any.

Search: "ready mix concrete New Town ND"

This category is genuinely populated, unlike the others, and it is worth showing for contrast. Google returns Dickinson Ready Mix (4.6 from 11), Strata again, Bowman Ready Mix (5.0 from 1) and Tioga Ready Mix and Yard, a Ruach Resources plant (4.4 from 10). The map also surfaces Knife River Redi-mix, Johnny's Ready Mix, Souris Ready Mix, and West Dakota Ready Mix, which is marked permanently closed.

Two things follow. Google had to zoom out across most of western North Dakota to fill that list, with Dickinson and Bowman sitting 150 to 200 miles from New Town, and those two share a phone number so they are one operation. Even the healthy category is not really in New Town.

More usefully, that is the batch plant shortlist. The operations memo describes discounted tonnage to an unnamed batch plant partner. These are the plants within reach, and they can now be asked about by name.

The web set and the map set are different companies

Ruach appears on the map only at its Tioga and Williston plants, never in New Town, despite publishing a landing page claiming to serve the town. McCody and Fisher do not appear at all.

Knife River does appear, but under a name it no longer uses. Knife River completed its $454 million acquisition of Strata Corporation on March 10, 2025. Seventeen months later the New Town listing still reads "Strata Corporation," still has no website attached, and its single review is a customer writing: "The Strata has been sold they are now Knife River." A buyer is being told the business changed hands by another buyer, because the owner never updated the listing.

So the companies that rank on the open web and the companies a local buyer actually finds barely overlap, and the largest player in the market is representing itself in New Town through a dead brand with one review and no website. Both surfaces need answering and they need different work. The web and AI surface is a content and markup problem. The map is a listing and reviews problem, and it is much the cheaper of the two to win.

Search: "gravel hauling" and "excavating"

These two came back completely different from everything above, and they change part of the plan.

Gravel hauling Running Horse LLC (4.2 from 25), Smart Sand (4.1 from 24), Iron Horse Inc (3.7 from 9), Quale Trucking (5.0 from 8), plus roughly eight pins clustered along Highway 23. Genuinely contested. Real operators with real review counts, right in town.
Excavating Beaver Creek LLC (3.8 from 61), Young Wolf Trenching (no reviews, no website), A&W Bobcat Services (no reviews, no website). Then end of list. The customer pool, not the competition. Small, and mostly invisible online.
This flips one line of the business plan

The operations memo lists hauling as a revenue stream alongside aggregate, crushing and topsoil. On the evidence, hauling is the one category near New Town that is already well served. Running Horse alone has more reviews than every aggregate supplier in the market combined. Meanwhile scoria, crushed concrete and sand and gravel supply are close to empty.

There is a second reason to be careful here. Names like Young Wolf Trenching, Iron Horse and Running Horse suggest locally and tribally owned operators, and under the TERO ordinance firms owned by enrolled members hold contracting preference. Those companies are far more valuable as customers and hauling partners than as opponents. Selling them material is a better posture than competing with them for loads, and it is also the posture that survives a TERO review.

Concentrate on what is empty. Supply the material, let the local haulers move it.

One caveat, stated plainly: these searches were run from Colorado with the map centered on New Town, not from a phone in Mountrail County. The set of businesses in each category is unlikely to differ much, but exact ordering could. Worth one confirming look from someone on the ground.

The finding that applies to every one of them

Not one competitor in this market has FAQ schema anywhere on its homepage, and Ruach has no FAQ section anywhere on its site at all.

That matters more each month. When a contractor asks an AI assistant "how many tons of Class 5 do I need for a 200 foot access road" or "who delivers scoria near New Town," the answer gets assembled from pages that answered those questions in plain language and marked them up so a machine can read them. In this market, nobody has written those pages. That is an open lane, and it is cheap to take.

05 The Threat Nobody Is Watching

The instinct is to treat Ruach as the competitor and the national producers as the big names. On the evidence, the more serious threat is Hello Gravel, and it is the same name that came up as the top online threat in the Denton audit back in July. The same company is sitting between this business and its buyers in both markets.

It is worth being precise about what it is, because "a website that ranks well" undersells it badly. Hello Gravel is a venture backed startup founded in 2023 with about $5.5 million raised, from Breakwater Ventures, SNAK Venture Partners, Benson Capital Partners, the New Orleans Startup Fund and Tulane Ventures. It describes itself as the 1-800-Flowers of gravel delivery. One of its founders is a federal aggregate contractor with over 300 completed projects. This is a funded, deliberate attempt to own the customer relationship nationally and buy the material locally.

It attacks on four layers at once

  • Paid search. They run Google Ads, including a brand campaign bidding on their own name. Confirmed from the tracking parameters on a live ad click.
  • Programmatic local SEO. An auto generated page for nearly every town in America, New Town included.
  • A guides library. The answer content that everyone in this market is missing, they already have.
  • AI agent interfaces. They publish an llms.txt and run a live read-only agent API with a Model Context Protocol endpoint. In their own words, it lets an agent "answer the full pre-purchase chain, size the job, confirm delivery, and price it." An assistant can estimate the material, check delivery to a ZIP, list what is available and return an all-in delivered price, with no person involved.
What that actually means

When someone asks an AI assistant where to get forty tons of gravel delivered near New Town, Hello Gravel can hand back a real, priced, deliverable answer. Every other company in this market can offer a phone number and an invitation to call for a quote.

They have no pit, no trucks and no yard within five hundred miles of New Town. They win anyway, because they built for how buying is starting to happen. A local operator with actual material and actual trucks should not be losing this, and the reason it is being lost has nothing to do with the material.

The turn: they are also a channel

Hello Gravel does not own material. It brokers. It maintains a public directory of 2,051 verified pits and quarries across all 50 states, with a page for each one, and it routes paid orders to those suppliers. Every listing is checked before it goes live and re-checked annually, including a call to the published phone number and a match of the address against parcel records and satellite imagery.

Two numbers out of that directory are worth sitting with.

Their entire North Dakota supply side is one company

We read their state directory directly. It lists three suppliers in all of North Dakota, and all three are Knife River.

They generate demand across the whole state, with pages for Minot, Grand Forks, Watford City, Parshall and New Town among others. The only place to route a western order is Williston.
Denton: the rivals are listed
Their named Denton partners include Denton Sand & Gravel and Metro Materials. The July audit rated Denton Sand & Gravel a High threat. Hello Gravel is feeding that market's demand to them.
We are not listed
Checked directly in their Texas directory on August 5. Of 154 Texas suppliers, Patriot Stone Supply is not one of them, while competing Denton yards are.
The strategic read

This is not the usual "get listed in a directory" suggestion. A funded national company is spending money to create aggregate demand in North Dakota and has no independent supplier anywhere in the state to fulfill it. A licensed operator with a pit near New Town is not asking them for a favor. It is the thing missing from their map.

And the Texas side is the same argument in reverse. Their Texas network runs 154 suppliers across 98 cities, it includes competing Denton yards, and Patriot Stone is not in it. That is a company generating demand in Denton and routing every bit of it past a yard that has been sitting there the whole time.

That said, competing for the search result and joining their network are different games on different clocks, and neither replaces the other. A broker keeps the customer relationship and the margin, so owning the channel is still the long game and still the right one. But for a first season with no name in the Bakken, being on the receiving end of demand somebody else is paying to create is close to pure upside.

06 What Fort Berthold Changes

This is the part that separates New Town from any other town of its size, and it is not a marketing question.

Any prime contractor, subcontractor or supplier intending to work on the Fort Berthold Reservation must file a compliance plan and hold a license from the MHA Tribal Employment Rights Office. Applications are accepted in person only at the TERO building in New Town, Monday through Thursday, 8:00 to 3:30. No emailed, faxed or mailed applications. Fees changed on January 1, 2026.

The ordinance also requires preference in contracting to Indian owned firms, strongest for firms wholly owned and controlled by enrolled members, and the TERO director reviews whether a preferred contractor genuinely self performs the work or passes it through.

Sequencing
TERO licensing and the pit and sand arrangements are the long poles. They run on the Nation's calendar and in person. The website is not the long pole and nothing should wait on it.
Positioning
On Fort Berthold, "TERO licensed, hiring locally" is a stronger trust signal to a buyer than any amount of copy about equipment. It belongs on the homepage, not in a footer.
Open question
If there is tribal ownership or partnership in the structure, that is the headline of the whole business, not a compliance note. We need to know before homepage copy gets written.
07 Where Concrete Warrior Starts

Honest summary of the starting position, so nobody is working from a rosier picture than the facts support.

Behind on
Everything visible. No website, no Google Business Profile, no listings, no reviews, no name recognition in the Bakken. Ruach has fourteen years of local history here.
Even on
Answer content and AI readiness, where the entire local field is at zero. And on material specs, which every one of them under-publishes.
Ahead on
Concrete recycling and washout, carried up from the Texas operation. Thin local competition, and it is the one lane with a real operating history behind it.
The realistic read, revised after the map research

The original version of this section said nobody catches a fourteen year incumbent on general aggregate searches in one season. The map results changed that assessment, and it is worth being direct about why.

There is no incumbent to catch in New Town. Two businesses in the sand and gravel category, one with no reviews. One crushing competitor in first place on a single review, under a brand name its owner retired seventeen months ago, with no website. Two scoria suppliers ranked below a bank. The web incumbents, Ruach and the national producers, have no local presence here at all.

What that means practically: a complete, correctly categorized Google listing with real photographs, real hours and a linked site would very likely be the strongest profile in this market within weeks of verification, and a season of honest review requests would put it beyond reach of anyone currently listed. Concentrate on materials, where the field is empty. Leave hauling to the local operators and sell to them instead.

08 Open Questions Before Anything Is Built
01What entity is Concrete Warrior?
A trade name of Patriot Stone Supply LLC, a separate North Dakota entity, or a joint venture. This decides the domain registrant, the legal name, the Google listing and the TERO application. It is the first fork in the road and everything else waits on it.
02Is there a staffed yard address near New Town?
This is the single biggest variable in the Google plan. A staffed address supports a standard listing, which is what wins "gravel near me" searches. Without one it becomes a service area listing, which works but ranks less strongly. A pit with nobody there is usually not enough.
03Where does TERO licensing stand, and is there tribal ownership?
Gates the work itself, not just the marketing, and changes the positioning entirely if the answer is yes.
04Can the batch plant partner be named publicly?
A named relationship is a real credibility asset in a market built on reliability. An unnamed one is worth nothing externally.
05One phone number and one address, and the material list with honest prices
Confirmed materials with North Dakota specs, price brackets per ton, and a realistic delivery radius so the service area claims are true. Publishing prices is unusual in this industry and it is one of the strongest things the Texas site already does.
