Turn the pre-order on for real

An afternoon
What's wrong
All 30 variants across your 8 products are marked unavailable, so the product page shows a greyed-out Sold Out button and the word "pre-order" does not appear on it anywhere. The Instagram launch — "Merch is now LIVE for preorder!!!!" — sends people to that page. Nobody could pre-order even if they wanted to. The video in that post is a screen recording of the products page, and the recording itself shows the filter reading In stock (0) · Out of stock (8).
Why it matters more than the lost sales
The point of the campaign was to find out which designs deserve a run of 100 and which deserve 25. Because nothing was buyable, it produced no demand data at all — the decision it was meant to inform is still open.
What it takes
Set each variant to continue selling when out of stock, which turns Sold Out into Add to Cart. Then switch on Restock Rocket's pre-order mode — it is already on the account and currently off — so the button reads Pre-order and sets expectations properly. One thing must change in the same pass: the page currently says Ships within 24 hrs from South Bend, IN, which contradicts a pre-order. Swap it for a real expected ship date before any money is taken.
Biggest single return on this listShip all three parts together

Move the policies into Shopify's own fields

An hour
What's wrong

Add the identity markup

Half a day
What's wrong
There is no structured data anywhere on the site. Most Shopify themes ship with it; this one has had it removed. It is the machine-readable summary that tells Google and the AI assistants who you are, and without it they are guessing from raw page text.
What it takes
Four blocks, in order of payback. Organization — legal name, logo, the real phone number, and links to your Fanatics Live, eBay, Whatnot, Instagram and Facebook accounts, so machines understand seven scattered profiles as one business. Product on product pages, which is what lets a search result carry a price. FAQ markup, the single strongest driver of getting quoted by an AI assistant. Breadcrumbs last.

Words

No technical skill required

Nothing in this section is technical. It is the difference between a site that lists products and one that answers what a collector wants to know before sending $20 to a stranger.

A page for the Buyback Program and where you rank

A day of writing
What's wrong
The 100% Buyback Program does not appear anywhere on breaknbad.com. We checked every page. It is the thing no other breaker offers, it is the direct answer to the fear that stops a first-time $20 buy-in, and your own Fanatics Live shows are named after it — "100% BUYBACK BREAKS ALL NIGHT." Someone hears that in a stream, searches for it, and finds nothing on your own site confirming it is real. The same goes for standing: the homepage does not contain the word "Fanatics," the #3 ranking, the 116,000 buyers, the 94+ employees or the $20M. Your media kit leads with every one of them.
What it takes
One page, written plainly: what the buyback is, how it works, who is eligible, and where you rank with the source named. That is the page a search engine quotes, the page an assistant reads when somebody asks whether you are legitimate, and the page a sponsor lands on after the kit. The single most valuable thing missing from this site.

Publish what the breaks produce

A programme, not a fix
What's wrong
You run breaks daily and publish none of the results. The one company that has genuinely solved search in this category did it by publishing a page for every completed break — thousands of them — each proving what came out of the box.
What it takes

Run the AI visibility test yourselves

An afternoon, five people
What's wrong
Nobody currently knows what ChatGPT, Perplexity, Grok and Google say when a collector asks them for a breaker. Expect the fictional phone number to come back.
What it takes
Five people, five fixed questions, four engines, screenshots of everything including the sources panel. Setup matters more than it sounds: a private window is the right control for Google and Perplexity only — ChatGPT needs Temporary Chat, because its memory is tied to the account rather than the browser, and Grok needs you signed in. Run the identical set monthly; the movement is your scoreboard.

What anyone doing this work will need

Gather it once, up front

This applies whether the work goes to your own developer, to us, or to somebody else. Gathering it in one pass rather than one login at a time is the single biggest thing that keeps a job like this from dragging. Wherever possible, create an account in the person's name rather than sharing a password — it leaves a trail and you can revoke it in one click when the work ends.

What nobody doing this work should need

Worth stating plainly, and worth holding any outside party to — including us.

No banking, no payment processor, no Shopify Payments or payout settings, and no customer personal data. Nothing on this list requires seeing a customer record or a transaction. Even the one job that touches buying — switching on pre-orders — changes a product setting, never a payment setting.

Every account you grant should be in your name, on your billing, and revocable by you in one click. That includes anything set up on your behalf, like an analytics or Bing account.
