What nobody in town is serving

Schnitzel. A plated pot roast. Katsu and Japanese curry. Paprikash. A pierogi skillet. Five open lanes on the cards we could fetch, and the recommended card takes four of them.

What we could not see The Doubleday dinner fetch came back thin. Tunnicliff and Lake Front are open and serving and we did not get usable menus off either one, so nothing above claims to know what they cook. Hoffman Lane is inactive. Alex & Ika is closed and 149 Main is Norbu now. The Pepper Mill closed in 2016 and that address is Upstate. Stagecoach Coffee runs 6:30am to 6pm with sandwiches to 3pm, which makes it a lunch competitor and not a dinner one.

If somebody on the team has eaten at Tunnicliff or Lake Front recently, that is worth thirty seconds before the schnitzel goes on. Those are the two rooms where a real collision could still be hiding.

The industry read, for what it is worth The National Restaurant Association's 2026 What's Hot list, surveyed in October 2025 and released 19 November 2025, has local sourcing, comfort food, value and global comfort food as four separate Top 10 items. Datassential's 2026 New Classics names butter chicken, hot honey, smashed formats and Basque cheesecake. We take the hot honey and the smash, both of which Fenimore already runs, and we leave butter chicken alone because the trend does not care that Norbu is four minutes away. A note on sourcing: the maple-kimchi crispy chicken line people are passing around is on Technomic's global 2026 page, not their US one, so it is not cited here as a US trend.

03

Keep, move, kill

Keep on the dinner card

Wings. Blue-cheese chips. Tuna nachos. Elote $15 live. Cauliflower $14 live. Smash. NY strip. La Brasa chicken. Salmon. Margherita. Hot honey pepperoni. The fried-chicken sandwich stays, on lunch.

Move off the printed dinner card

Grilled cheese. Caprese. Quesadilla $15 and $17 live. French Connection $18 live. All four go to lunch or Sunday. They are not bad items, they are taking dinner real estate that four supper plates need.

Kill from the proposal

The waffle. Sunday gravy pasta. Butter chicken with naan. Another brat. And the words "Old World Sausage Flight," which is a wine bar's phrase on a supper-house board.

Off the permanent card, kept in the back pocket Paprikash, the Polish supper plate, pumpkin ravioli, the Thanksgiving bowl, Basque cheesecake, butter chicken, chicken and waffles, one more brat. Some of those are good specials. None of them earn a printed line. Currywurst fries run as a six-week limited item, not in print.

Test order

  1. Pierogi Skillet, Schnitzel, Katsu CurryThese three first, in that order. Each one takes an open lane, none of them needs a new supplier, and all three tell a guest what the card is about in a single visit.
  2. Yankee Pot Roast, then Crispy Chicken with Korean MapleBoth are proven forms in a new room. The pot roast is the value plate at $27 and the Korean maple chicken is the one that argues with Ommegang without imitating them.
  3. Three Sausages, once a butcher is lined upThis one waits on sourcing. A $26 board of three has to be three sausages worth eating, and that is a supplier decision before it is a menu decision.

Sunday football, eight items only

Wings $18. Blue cheese chips $14. Tuna nachos, confirm in Toast. Pierogi skillet $16. Three sausages $26. Smash $17. Yankee pot roast $27. Bread pudding $12. Eight lines, one page, no create-your-own anything on a full Sunday.

The one thing that outranks this document

Every price on the target lines is a menu recommendation, not a costed one. Before anything prints, the target plates need a plate cost and the four keepers need a Toast pull.

If Toast says an item on the move or kill list is already printing money, Toast wins. This card is built from what the competition serves and what the town has no source for.
