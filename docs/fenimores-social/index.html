Fenimore's social media plan, September to March

Two parts running at the same time. The posts we already know are coming get built ahead and scheduled so they publish on their own. The people at the restaurant post what's happening that day, without waiting on anyone.

For the Fenimore's and Cooperstown Experience team. Working brief, not a public page.
This page changes as you send feedback. New dates, corrected hours and anything we got wrong get added here, and the log at the bottom says what changed and when. The link stays the same.
Companion pages: private events, where the website stands, and the Cooperstown index.

01

The plan

Part one · built ahead

Scheduled posts that publish themselves

Most of the next six months is already on the calendar: every football Sunday, Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year's, the long weekends, the Super Bowl. Each of those posts gets written ahead of time, with its photo, caption and link, and scheduled for its date in Meta Business Suite.

A post scheduled in September goes out on Thanksgiving morning whether or not anyone is near a phone.

Part two · same day

The people on the ground post as things happen

A good plate, a full room on a game day, a special the kitchen just put up. Whoever takes the photo, or whoever is posting that stretch, puts it up right then instead of emailing it around.

Each person uses their own login. Nobody shares a password.

The two parts fit together because everyone who posts can see the same planner. The scheduled posts keep a steady rhythm going through the slow weeks and the busy ones, and the same-day photos fill the gaps in between. If a photo in someone's hand is better than what's scheduled for that slot, swap it instead of running both.

Honestly, part two is the harder one. Part one is a few sittings of desk work and then it runs. Part two depends on people remembering during service, and that usually fades after the first couple of weeks unless one person owns each stretch of the week. That's why the first question at the bottom of this page is about names.

02

Every photo is a real one

Posts that look machine-made have already drawn pushback in Cooperstown. It was said plainly on the 9 September call: why would anyone go somewhere that isn't real? In a village this size that's hard to walk back.

So both parts use real photographs of Fenimore's: the actual food, drinks, room and staff. That includes the scheduled posts. The best time to shoot for them is an ordinary weekday, well before the date, like shooting a wings plate on a quiet Thursday for the Thanksgiving night game.

04

How the people at the restaurant get to post

Nobody gets handed the password. A shared login can't be taken back from one person without changing it for everyone, and it breaks the two-factor login that protects the account.

Instead, each person signs in to their own Facebook or Instagram account. Someone who already has full control of the Fenimore's page adds them in Meta Business Suite with permission to create posts, schedule them, and reply to comments and messages. They don't get settings, billing, ads, or the ability to add other people. Meta renames these screens often, so follow the labels on the screen in front of you.

Staff often turn this down because they worry about their personal profile, so say this when you ask: being added to a business page gives the restaurant nothing on their private profile. Nothing posts there and their friends see nothing. They just get a switch on their phone to move between themselves and Fenimore's.

Check two things first. The Instagram account has to be a professional account, not a personal one, and it has to be linked to the Facebook page. If either is missing, the options to add people and schedule posts won't show up, and the screens will look broken. We can't see either from outside.

Then, for the daily rhythm:

  • One named poster for each stretch of the week. For example, one person for weekday daytime and one from Friday afternoon through Sunday, when the restaurant is busiest and the photos are best. A photo sent to five people usually gets posted by nobody.
  • Look at the planner before posting. If something is already scheduled for that hour, hold yours for the next gap. It takes about fifteen seconds on a phone.
  • Post at the hour people are deciding. Breakfast by 8:30am, lunch by 11:15am, drinks and dinner between 4:30 and 5:30pm, game days in the morning and again near kickoff.
  • Whoever posts in a stretch also answers the comments and messages. That's where bookings and "are you open for breakfast?" questions come in.

05

One detail that trips people up: links

On Facebook, a web address in the post can be tapped. On Instagram it can't, so an Instagram caption should say "menu link in our bio" and the link in the bio has to go somewhere useful. As of 10 September the @fenimorestable Instagram bio reads Cooperstown, NY / Elevated dining & craft cocktails and doesn't mention breakfast. Where its link points isn't visible to us signed out, so someone should check it on a phone.

The pages worth linking to are fenimorestable.com/breakfast/, /menu/, /drinks/ and /private-events/.

06

What we need to get started

  1. Who at Fenimore's should be set up to post? Two names, one for each stretch of the week. This is the one that blocks part two, and it's the team's decision, not ours.
  2. Who has full control of the Facebook page? They're the person who adds those two names. It takes a few minutes, and they can remove someone just as quickly.
  3. Dates we've missed, and the holiday hours. Reply with any town events, local traditions or plans that belong on the calendar, and whether Fenimore's is open on Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and New Year's. We'll add them to this page as they come in.

This brief doesn't move any account, change any login, or take work away from anyone. Whoever runs @fenimorestable today still runs it. If there's already a way of doing this that works and we've missed it, tell us and we'll rebuild the plan around it.
