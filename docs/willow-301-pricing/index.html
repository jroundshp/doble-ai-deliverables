01 · The Short Version

Three things, before the detail

  1. Your property is priced above what this market will pay, and it needs a reduction.
  2. Vail Village is active, but the sales setting the high prices per square foot are four and five bedroom properties. Two bedroom demand has not supported this price per square foot, and it still does not.
  3. This is not your fault. It is the market speaking after more than 470 days.

A buyer in Edelweiss has shown us what a finished unit here is worth to them. They bought #203 in original condition for $3,200,000 and are now spending $800,000 to $1,000,000 to renovate it. Call it $4.0 to $4.2 million all in. That is a cost rather than a sale price, and a renovation done to one owner's taste never returns dollar for dollar. It is still the closest like-for-like comparison that exists, and it is the one a buyer's broker will find.

#302 is the other half of the story. It asked the same $4,950,000, never tested a lower number, and came off the market in July. Holding a price is not the same as getting one.

Two things in your favor

With #203 under renovation for about a year and #302 withdrawn, yours is the only Edelweiss unit anyone can buy right now, and the only finished one. That advantage has a shelf life.

And this is not a building problem. Edelweiss completed a full exterior renovation in 2014, the building is in excellent condition, and there are no upcoming special assessments. Nothing about the property is holding the price back.

Altus is the immediate problem. A buyer at your price can currently choose new construction in Vail Village with more square footage for less money. We answer that with the Gore Creek and Bishop Park setting, the corner position, a finished renovation and two deeded parking spaces. Those are real answers and they are worth a premium. They are not worth being the more expensive option.

A renovated Village two-bedroom of nearly identical size closed six days ago at $3,950,000. It asked $4,350,000 and closed at 90.8 percent of that. Buyers in this segment negotiate, and they negotiate from closed sales.

The buyer we want is someone who already owns outside the resort and wants a Vail Village ski connection with two spaces. That buyer is real, and the parking is genuinely scarce for them. Forty five open houses have not produced one at this price.

05 · Recommendation

One decisive reduction, not another step

Recommended reprice

$4,500,000

$4,029 per square foot · a $450,000 reduction

This puts you below new construction instead of above it, and it puts a number in front of the buyers who stopped watching in April. In one move. A third small step reads to the market as a seller edging downward and spends what credibility the price has left. One decisive cut reads as a decision.

If the goal is a signed contract this season

$4,150,000 – $4,350,000

$3,715 to $3,894 per square foot

You should have both numbers. The closed sales support a figure below $4,500,000. Nothing that sold in Vail Village this year supports $4,432 a foot for a two bedroom, and $4,029 is still above every one of them. The #203 owner's all-in cost works out to roughly $3,676 to $3,860 a foot, which brackets this lower band almost exactly.

So $4,500,000 should bring buyers back to the table and then be negotiated. One working the same 90.8 percent that sale took would land near $4,086,000. If a signed contract before the season matters more than the headline number, pricing into the lower band now gets there faster and leaves you the same room.

The dues cover utilities and internet, which makes that number less punishing than it looks and is worth saying to a buyer. It is still about $2,500 a month with nothing coming back the other way. Another season costs roughly $29,600 to hold a price the market has now answered twice.

The window

  • Yours is the only Edelweiss unit available. #302 is withdrawn and #203 is a construction site for about a year.
  • That ends when #203 finishes. A second renovated unit in the building, priced by an owner who knows exactly what they paid, changes your position.
  • Buyers choose before the season, not during it. A reduction that lands now is in front of them while they are still deciding.

My recommendation

Everything we can control has been done, and done well. The number is the last variable left and the only one we can still change. I would change it once, decisively, and go sell this before the season starts.
