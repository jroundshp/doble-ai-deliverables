Why this band

  • It stays well above #203. At $4,250,000 the unit holds a $1,050,000 premium over the original-condition sale in its own building, which is the renovation, the corner, the two deeded spaces and the sauna paid for in full.
  • It sits above the Village Center 3G close, not below it. $4,250,000 against a $3,950,000 close for nearly identical square footage keeps a defensible Edelweiss and parking premium rather than conceding the comparison.
  • It clears the twin-ask trap. As soon as #301 is the lower number in the building, the two units stop cancelling each other out and #301 becomes the one that gets shown.
  • It moves out of the new-construction fight. At $4,250,000 the buyer comparing against Altus at $4,895,000 is now looking at a $645,000 saving, which reframes the square-footage difference.
  • It leaves negotiating room that matches the market. If a buyer works the same 90.8 percent that Village Center 3G took, a $4,250,000 ask still lands near $3,860,000, inside the band where Village two-bedrooms are closing.

Confidence, stated plainly

Strong that a material reduction is needed. Two seasons of marketing, a $350,000 cut that produced nothing, an identical ask in the same building and a $1 million gap to the closest comparable sale all point the same way.

Medium on the exact dollar. The sold set behind the $3.4 million to $4.0 million band is not yet fully enumerated, and the special assessment position is unknown. Both could move the number.

One structural point for the owner conversation

A single decisive cut reads very differently to the market than a third small step. The property has already made one $350,000 reduction that did not produce a buyer. Another $200,000 move would most likely produce the same result and would spend the remaining credibility of the price. If the owner is willing to reduce at all, the argument for doing it once and doing it properly is stronger now than it was in April.

08 · Before The Owner Call

Three questions for John

  1. What is the owner's actual motivation and timeline? A reprice sized to close before the 2026 ski season starts is a different number than one sized to be well positioned for spring 2027. The band above assumes we want a buyer this season.
  2. How far will the owner go in one move? The recommendation is built around a single decisive cut. If the owner will only accept another step of $200,000 or so, tell us now and we will build the case differently, because that is a materially weaker position and should be argued as such.
  3. Can we get the HOA assessment documents first? The MLS points to remarks and we do not have the documents. If there is an open or pending assessment at Edelweiss, it belongs in the pricing conversation rather than surfacing during a buyer's due diligence.
