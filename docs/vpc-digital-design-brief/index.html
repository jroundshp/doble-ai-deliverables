VPC digital design brief

What the digital presence should look like across every division, how the sites relate to each other, and how the whole estate works as a sales tool for the 2027 setup year and the 2028 target.

Every claim about the current estate in section 02 was checked live on 12 September 2026, against the pages as they served that morning. Nothing here uses VPC analytics, because we do not have access to them. Companion detail: 2027 and 2028 owner plan, digital options, findability review, website work order, closest job plan, EasyRec build.

00  The short version

VPC has five divisions, three public domains and one rented catalog, and a buyer cannot tell from the outside that they are one company. The fix is a single owned hub with two clearly marked doors, municipal and plant FRP on one side and aquatic and recreation on the other, with the division sites kept as owned siblings that say whose they are in the markup and not only in the copyright line. The catalog comes home. That is the difference between a brochure and the thing an engineer uses to start a quote.

01

The premise this is designed against

The 2027 and 2028 owner plan is treated here as settled: Traditional FRP funds the floor, InterActive Play buys the upside, EasyRec is the short cycle attach, and municipal work gets specified into a drawing eighteen months to three years before anyone breaks ground. A website that only describes the company is worth very little against that. A website that gets VPC specified, and then makes the quote easy to start, is worth a great deal.

Five people have to be able to land, recognize what they are looking at, and act. They do not want the same things, and today they all arrive at the same front door.

Who landsWhat they came forWhat has to be one click away

Utility project managerConfidence in the manufacturer, and a name to give the engineerInstalled examples, certifications, who to call, plant locations
GC or construction managerLead time and whether the submittal will go throughLead times, submittal support, or-equal language, a quote form that takes a file
Parks or aquatic buyerWhat a splash pad or a pool deck costs and looks likeProject types, photography, design assist path, accessories

Design rule for the whole estate: every page answers one buyer's question and ends in one quote path. If a page cannot say which of the five it is for, it is a brochure page.

What we found this morning

  • Act now  The EasyRec staging site is open to search engines. new.easyrecreation.com serves a 200 and carries no noindex tag, and its robots file allows crawlers everywhere except /wp-admin/. It is a near copy of the live site, under the same brand and the same product terms, with nothing telling a crawler to stay out. The setting that fixes it is a checkbox in WordPress under Settings and Reading. It is not reachable over the API, so somebody has to click it.
  • Two divisions share one URL. Composite Fan and Primary Plastics are anchor links on the /divisions/ page, at #cft and #pp. Together they have been about 17 percent of six year sales. A page fragment cannot rank, cannot carry its own title or description, and cannot be sent to a buyer as a link that means anything.
  • compositefan.com lands on the corporate homepage. VPC owns the domain and it resolves to vpcfiberglass.com with a canonical pointing at the homepage. Somebody typing the division name by hand gets a general page about fiberglass.
  • The division sites admit their parent only in the copyright line. easyrecreation.com reads "A Division of" in the footer, and interactivewaterparks.com reads "Interactive Play is a Division of" in the same place. That is the weakest position on the page, and neither site says it in the markup: there is no parentOrganization on either.
  • easyrecreation.com publishes no structured data at all. No Organization, no Product, nothing. interactivewaterparks.com has an Organization block but no parent link and no Product markup.
  • The corporate homepage declares itself three times. There is an Organization node with a proper identifier, plus a second Organization and a LocalBusiness with no identifier at all. Three unreconciled descriptions of one company is how an entity gets read as ambiguous.
  • No llms.txt on the main domain. The path returns the WordPress 404 page.
  • The best product data VPC has sits on the rented platform. The catalog tags a specification against every product; the pages VPC owns carry no Product markup at all. The exact counts are the ones measured for digital options on 11 September, and they are a moving target on a platform we cannot log into, so treat the totals as of that date rather than as a standing figure.

03

The architecture to build toward

One company, two buyer doors, and a clear home for every division. The hub carries the corporate identity and routes; the doors separate the two buyers who have nothing in common; the division sites stay where they are and stop behaving like orphans.

The hub

vpcfiberglass.com

Who VPC is, where it manufactures, the certifications, careers, contact, and the router into everything else.

  • Owns the Organization identity, the addresses and the phone
  • Owns the domestic content story as readable text
  • Owns the engineer resource library
  • Two large doors above the fold, not a product grid

Door A, municipal and plant FRP

vpcfiberglass.com/frp/

Flumes, metering manholes, shelters and enclosures, troughs and weirs, duct, fans. Where the consulting engineer lands and where a spec gets written.

  • Product families with real specifications
  • Guide specs, CAD, STEP, domestic content packets
  • Lead times and submittal support for GCs
  • The RFQ form, with file upload

Door B, aquatic and recreation

vpcfiberglass.com/aquatic/

A chooser, not a catalog. It exists so a parks buyer never has to scroll past a manhole to find a splash pad.

  • Routes to InterActive Play and to EasyRec
  • Speaks capital plans, bonds and pool remodels
  • Surfaces the EasyRec attach on the way through

Paths on one domain, not subdomains

Put the two doors on vpcfiberglass.com as /frp/ and /aquatic/ rather than on frp.vpcfiberglass.com and aquatic.vpcfiberglass.com. Everything the hub earns in search then counts for the doors, there is one certificate and one WordPress to keep patched, and nobody has to remember which subdomain a resource lives on. A subdomain only makes sense if a division is going to be run, hosted or sold separately, and there is no sign that either of these is.

The two existing division sites are the exception, and they should stay on their own domains. interactivewaterparks.com and easyrecreation.com both have history, links and buyers who know the names. Moving them would throw that away to fix a problem that a footer line and a schema field can fix instead.

Composite Fan and Primary Plastics need a page each

Not a site each. A real page at /composite-fan/ and /primary-plastics/, with its own title, its own description, its own products and its own quote path, linked from the hub and from Door A. compositefan.com then points at /composite-fan/ instead of at the homepage, which costs nothing and immediately starts working. Seventeen percent of six year sales is too much revenue to leave living at a page fragment.

The catalog comes home

Product truth moves onto VPC pages: the specifications, the downloads and the Product markup. Navigator becomes an optional lead channel rather than the system of record, with redirects from the old catalog URLs so nothing that has been linked or bookmarked breaks. The directory listing stays only if attribution shows it pays for itself. This is Option C in digital options, and nothing else in this brief works properly until that call is made.

The test for the whole architecture: a stranger should be able to tell within five seconds which door they are in, and within one click that it is all VPC.

04

What goes on each property

Hub home

  • One line on what VPC is: a fiberglass manufacturer, plants in Medina and Lockport, about 57 people, made in the USA and ready for federally funded work.
  • Two doors, given equal weight and equal size. Municipal and plant FRP, aquatic and recreation.
  • Proof above the fold: certifications, notable installations, and a strip of engineer resources that goes straight to the library.
  • A third, quieter strip for Composite Fan and Primary Plastics, so those buyers have somewhere to go without diluting the two doors.
  • Phone, quote email and both plant addresses, identical to every other property.

Door A, the FRP sales tool

  • Product families, each with machine readable specifications rather than a picture and a paragraph.
  • The resource library kept at VPC owned URLs. The 316 files already sit on VPC's own WordPress uploads, so they carry across as they are and the links engineers have saved keep working.
  • A "for engineers" section: guide specs, CAD and STEP, domestic content packets, and a lunch and learn request form that books the meeting rather than starting a thread.
  • A "for contractors" section: lead times, submittal support and or-equal language, which is the page a GC actually needs and almost nobody publishes.
  • The RFQ form on VPC's own Gravity Forms, with file upload, on VPC pages. Never only inside the catalog.
  • The public promise stays what the contact page already says in Corey's name: a quote inside 24 hours when the specifications are complete. The form's job is to collect complete specifications so that promise is keepable.

The closest job index is internal. It never appears on the public site, it is not a feature to advertise, and it is not mentioned in any public copy. Same for anything touching Salesforce.

Door B, the aquatic hub

  • A two card chooser with real photography: InterActive Play for the park itself, EasyRec for what goes around the water.
  • Language the buyer uses: capital improvement plan, bond, splash pad, pool remodel, accessibility upgrade.
  • An EasyRec module on every path through, so the attach happens while the buyer is already reading.

interactivewaterparks.com

  • Project types, and photo and video proof of installations rather than renderings.
  • Two distinct paths, and this is the one thing the site does not do today: design assist for a 2028 opening, and buy now for this coming summer. They are different buyers on different clocks and they should not share a form.
  • A "complete the site" module on every project and quote path, carrying benches, ADA stairs and accessories.
  • Links up to the hub for shelters, enclosures and chemical buildings, because a park that needs one will not think to look for it on a fiberglass site.
  • "A division of VPC Fiberglass" in the header, in the footer and in the markup, not only in the copyright line.

easyrecreation.com

  • Catalog clarity first. This is a products site and it should read like one.
  • The ADA measuring flow as one question per screen with photographs inside the form, built as a form rather than a PDF somebody prints. It exists to stop the phone tag that a wrong measurement causes.
  • Bench color and logo preview, so a buyer can see what they are ordering before they ask.
  • A clear where-to-buy path for reps and dealers, and an order path that produces a labeled submission.
  • Cross links up to InterActive Play and to the hub.
  • The staging site locked before anything else on this list. See the EasyRec build.

Composite Fan and Primary Plastics

  • One real page each, with its own products, its own buyer language and its own quote path.
  • Fan buyers are chemical, pulp and paper, and wastewater plant people. They should not have to read water and wastewater copy written for flumes to work out whether VPC makes what they need.
  • compositefan.com points at the Composite Fan page.
  • One set of product pages, not two. Door A lists fans as a product family with the specs and the quote path; the division page is the buyer's entry, written for the chemical, pulp and paper and wastewater reader, and it links to the same product pages rather than restating them. Two copies of the same fan content competing with each other is the problem the catalog already has, and it should not be rebuilt by hand.

06

Findability and AI visibility

Two audiences read these pages before a human does: the search index, and the AI assistants that engineers and parks staff now ask first. Both need to work out that five names are one company, and what that company makes.

Fix the entity first

This is the part that is broken today and the part that everything else compounds off.

  • One Organization, one identifier The hub declares VPC once, with a stable identifier, and the duplicate Organization and LocalBusiness blocks without identifiers come out. Three descriptions of one company is how a search engine ends up unsure which is the company.
  • Parent and children declared Each division site names VPC as parentOrganization. The hub names them back. This is the single cheapest change on the list and it is the one that makes the five names read as one business.
  • Visible parentage, not only markup "A division of VPC Fiberglass" in the header and footer of both division sites, linked, not buried in a copyright string that a reader treats as furniture.
  • One set of contact facts Name, addresses and phone identical across every property and anywhere VPC is listed. Different formatting is read as different companies.

Then the page level work

  • Title, meta description, H1 and a social image on every key template, written for the page rather than generated from the post name. No property ships a default WordPress title.
  • Product markup on the product pages VPC owns, once the catalog comes home. This is the asset nobody in the peer set has, and today it only exists on the rented platform.
  • FAQPage markup only where there are real questions and real answers on the page. The markup gets cut down to the visible copy, never the other way round.
  • LocalBusiness only where it is accurate, which means the plants, not every division.
  • WebSite with a search action only if site search actually works.
  • Certifications and the domestic content story as crawlable text on a page, not only as a PDF. A PDF is evidence; the page is what gets found and quoted.
  • Specifications and drawings named so the filename and the product name match, so a search for the product finds the drawing.
  • A sitemap and a clean robots file on every owned property, and an llms.txt on the hub. The hub has no llms.txt today.
  • Staging locked. new.easyrecreation.com is indexable right now: no noindex tag, and a robots file that lets crawlers in.

What we are not going to claim. Schema and a clean technical setup do not, on their own, get VPC cited by an AI assistant. They are the floor that makes a page readable. What actually moves citation is being mentioned in places the models already read, and having one clear identity they can attach those mentions to. That is why the entity work above comes before the markup checklist, and why the engineer program in the owner plan matters more to findability than any tag on any page.

The corollary: do not leave product truth on Navigator and describe the result as an owned presence. If the best machine readable description of a VPC product lives on someone else's platform, that is where it gets read from, and VPC does not control what happens to it.

08

Design system and how it should behave

One visual language across the estate, with a division accent color so a buyer knows which door they are standing in without feeling like they have been handed off to a different company.

  • Shared, not uniform One type scale, one button, one form pattern, one photographic style. Division accent colors on top. Same family, different rooms.
  • Photography, not stock Proof sits next to the call to action, and it is a photograph of something VPC built. A stock image of a water treatment plant helps nobody decide anything.
  • Mobile first Municipal engineers and parks staff read on phones, often standing on a site. Tables scroll, forms work with one thumb, and nothing important hides behind a hover.
  • Forms before chat A form that takes a drawing as an upload beats a chat widget that cannot. The RFQ is the most important object on the estate and it should look like it.
  • Accessible on purpose Contrast, focus states, real labels and keyboard paths. EasyRec sells ADA products, so an inaccessible EasyRec page is an argument against the product.
  • Fast Compressed images and no multi megabyte hero. A slow page on a phone on a job site is a page nobody reads.

House rule carried across from the rest of this work: no em dashes in client copy, and every property ships with a real title. Nothing goes live reading like a default install.

Who builds it

Doble AI does the build, with Amanda on the VPC side. The split that works: we do the architecture, the design system, the templates, the schema, the catalog migration and the redirects. Amanda holds the things only someone inside VPC can hold, which is the vendor relationships, the domain and registrar access, the content nobody outside the company has, and the changes that need a VPC login rather than an API call.

The ownership test is the same one set out in the closest job plan, and it is the part worth settling before anything gets built. VPC owns the domains, the hosting, the accounts, the code and the content. Doble AI leaves and the site still runs. If that is not true at the end, the setup was wrong.

Scope and terms are a separate conversation and this brief does not settle them. A rebuild on this scale is its own piece of work, not something that quietly rides along inside the current arrangement, and you should see what it is before you agree to it.

One decision this raises that is yours alone. Thomas built and hosts the main site today. A rebuild changes what that relationship is for, and it may end it. That is a call about a supplier you have a history with, so we are not going to make it inside a design document. Tell us what you want to happen and we will design around it either way.

What this brief deliberately does not do

  • Put the closest job index, or anything reading Salesforce, on the public web.
  • Leave the product system of record on Navigator while calling the result an owned presence.
  • Drop park buyers into flume catalogs without a reason they can see.
  • Launch any staging domain without noindex, ever again.
  • Put a Doble AI or Atlas brand anywhere on a VPC property. These are VPC's sites.
