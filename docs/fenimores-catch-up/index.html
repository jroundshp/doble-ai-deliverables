Decisions still open on the events side

Bob's manual has its own launch checklist, and most of it is still open. These are the ones a first proposal will run into:

  • Deposit amount, cancellation terms, service charge, food and beverage minimums, and how long a date is held.
  • Final per-person pricing for the four packages. Bob's ranges are preliminary and internal.
  • Who supplies the enhancements (chairs, linens, lighting, dance floor, staging). The page says they are quoted on selection.
  • Outside cake and any cutting fee, and the number of parking spaces on site. Parking is worth saying out loud on the page, since it's a real advantage over the village.
  • Whether brunch is a public Sunday service or an events package only (more in section 05).

Demand Bob has already mentioned

  • A group of about 60 doctors who want to bring in a screen.
  • A motor coach of about 55 on a Hall of Fame trip, looking for a buffet.
  • A hospital party. The size came through differently on two calls, so confirm it with Bob before quoting a number.
  • Bob has a list of 100 or more target organizations for outbound event sales, and he expects events to out-earn the regular restaurant.

Two local facts worth using: the Hampton Inn is next door at 4882 State Highway 28 (it's on the page), and Bassett Medical Center is a few miles up the same road, which is where the hospital and doctor groups come from.

The website: what changed, and what's left

We hold administrator access to fenimorestable.com, so changes don't wait on a vendor. The detailed record is the Where things stand readout (dated 10 September) and the menu sync from 11 September. The highlights:

  • Search engines and AI assistants can now read the restaurant. Hours, address, price band and every menu item with its price are in machine-readable form on the menu and breakfast pages, and page titles and descriptions are written for every page.
  • Menus match the printed cards. Wrong add-on prices corrected, retired dishes removed, "Koren" fixed to "Korean," vegetarian items marked, and Fire-Roasted Chicken renamed Fenimore Half Chicken per Tammy.
  • Hours agree everywhere. Two pages had been contradicting themselves on Tuesday and Wednesday. Dinner now reads Thursday through Monday, 4PM to 9PM.
  • Faster on a phone. Homepage photos converted to a lighter format, and pinch-to-zoom turned back on so people can enlarge the menu.
  • Sitemap fixed and Google Analytics live on every page as of today.

Since the 10 September readout, a few of its open items have closed: the events page is live, capacity is set, and the old /brunch/ address now forwards to the breakfast page.

05

Social media

There are two documents, and they work together.

  • The social media plan says what gets posted and when. Posts for dates we already know (every football Sunday, Thanksgiving night Chiefs at Bills, Christmas, a Super Bowl on Valentine's Day) are built ahead with real photos and scheduled. Same-day photos from the floor fill in around them. The calendar runs through Easter and still needs town events and holiday hours.
  • The posting playbook says how. It covers every post from start to finish: check the planner, take a real photo, tag and add the location, write the caption, share to the story right away, and have staff like it from their personal accounts. It includes copy-and-paste captions.

Who posts: Shelley schedules and keeps the events posting calendar she built with Bob. Grace posts on weekdays and makes graphics in Canva (she prefers that to AI images). Bob shares specials to the Celebrate Cooperstown and Milford Facebook groups. Katie, Shelley, Brooks and Bob all have access to the Facebook page.

Bob's weekly ad set

Bob put together seven ads meant to repeat every week: Private Events, Thursday Chicken Riggies, Friday Fish Fry, Saturday Prime Rib, two Sunday Football ads and Sunday Brunch. John's reply on 14 September asked Bob to confirm a few things so the ads and the website say the same thing:

  • The three dinner specials as standing weekly items, with prices and sides as Katie serves them.
  • Game Day Nachos and the Sunday Football prices, if they belong on the menu.
  • Sunday brunch hours and the mimosa and Bloody Mary prices. The ad says 10 to 2 and $5. An earlier ad said 10 to 4. Shelley says brunch is the breakfast menu for now. Brunch is on hold until this is settled, and then a brunch page gets built.
  • Happy Hour days, times and prices, if it's going ahead. Tuesday and Wednesday close at 4, so an evening happy hour can't be advertised as daily.

The Private Events ad also still says "Coming Soon," and it can now say "Now Booking" and link to the live page.
