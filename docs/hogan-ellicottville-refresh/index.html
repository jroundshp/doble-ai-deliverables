00 Start Here — What Got Fixed

Both July audits are now substantially out of date, and that is a good problem. Between 18 and 21 August the Bake Shop replaced its entire website, and between 18 and 20 August Alpine had a full SEO layer installed. Fourteen separate items we had written up as defects are simply gone. We re-checked every one of them at source on 24 August.

Ellicottville Bake Shop — the new site shipped

The old anima build is gone. The live site is a new Elementor build on the foodie theme with real, specific meta descriptions on six of seven pages (there were none at all), Open Graph and Twitter cards (there were none), structured data, and a mobile viewport that no longer blocks pinch-to-zoom. The three leftover 2022 demo pages are gone. The homepage has an H1 where it previously had none. Server response is fast at 0.32 s to first byte.

Ellicottville Bake Shop — the staging site closed itself cleanly

Through late July and August a full copy of the rebuild sat at new.ellicottvillebakeshop.com, open to Google, with its own sitemap and a canonical pointing at itself. That was the one genuinely expensive thing on either site, because a half-built duplicate can outrank the real one. At launch it went to 404 completely. Nothing needs doing. One optional tidy-up is in the shared list below.

Alpine Bistro — the technical SEO gap is closed

The July audit graded Alpine's technical SEO F and said there were no meta descriptions, no Open Graph tags, no Twitter cards and no structured data anywhere. That is no longer true of any of it. Rank Math is now installed and configured: all six pages carry specific, well-written meta descriptions, Open Graph and Twitter cards are present, and there is real structured data including a postal address, a phone number and opening hours. The page titles have been rewritten from generic labels into the phrasing people actually search. That grade moves F to C, which is the largest single jump anywhere in the portfolio.

Worth saying plainly

Across nine audits in this portfolio, website findings had never been actioned by anyone. These two are the first sites where that changed, and on both of them it changed fast. Everything that follows is written on the assumption that it will be read by someone who fixes things, so it is specific about where each item lives.

01 The Question From 22 August, Answered

On 22 August the group asked why neither business appeared in a Google AI answer to “what activities are going on in Ellicottville New York this weekend.” We went and looked. The cause is not technical and nothing is broken. It is that neither website contains a single dated event.

The Bake Shop has an events page. Here is what it says.

Verified by render The page at /portfolio/bakery-events/ reads, in full: “There are no scheduled events at this time. Check back soon!” Underneath it says the shop hosts seasonal and holiday events through the year. That page is also not listed in the sitemap, so Google may not know it exists at all. Alpine's only events URL is /event/, which is a private-party and catering enquiry page, not a calendar.

For comparison, here is what the businesses that did appear are doing. Both were checked the same day.

The honest version, because it matters for what you decide to do

Villaggio has no event markup whatsoever and was named anyway. Balloons does everything correctly and was not in the visible part of that answer, though the screenshot was cut off, so we cannot say it was absent. So this is not a structured-data problem and we are not going to tell you it is. Dated pages get you into the pool Google draws from. They do not buy you the slot. What separates Villaggio from you is not markup, it is that a page exists at all saying something happens on a specific date.

The opening nobody in the village has taken

We checked Balloons, Villaggio, Ellicottville Brewing and Alpine. Not one of them publishes weekend tie-in content. A page that says “Racing at HoliMont on Sunday? We open at 7:30 so you can eat before the first run” is content about this weekend, on your own site, using the proper nouns and dates the engine is already trying to resolve. The village generates a free event calendar every single week and any business willing to write two sentences becomes content about it. Roughly fifteen minutes a week.

And it is not only live music. A Saturday-only sourdough out of the oven at eight, a seasonal drink, a dated autumn menu, extended hours for race weekend — all of it counts, and the Bake Shop already has a /portfolio/seasonal-specialty-coffee-menu/ page that is currently invisible to Google for the same reason as the events page.

Our reading, offered as a question Our honest guess is that this is the highest-volume search there is in a destination town, and that the five prompts in the July audits would never have caught it because every one of them assumes the searcher already knows they want coffee, or breakfast, or a bakery. This one names no category and carries a time window. We have added it to how we test both businesses from now on. Does that match how you think visitors actually arrive?

02 Ellicottville Bake Shop
Ellicottville Bake Shop 6.0 / 10 ↑ from 5.5 in July

The new site is genuinely good. It is fast, it is well written, the mobile zoom lock is gone, and it carries far more real content than the old one. Second-highest score in the portfolio. The problems that remain are all in one area: what the site tells Google exists.

The main finding — the sitemap is pointing the wrong way round

Verified at source The sitemap submits 23 URLs. Sixteen of them are theme furniture — /layouts/slide-1/, /layouts/mailchimp-popup/, /layouts/restaurant-header-default/, /testimonials_group/clients/ and similar. Every one loads as a real page carrying two to three thousand words of duplicated header and footer, and nothing on the site links to any of them.

Meanwhile 29 real content pages are missing from every sitemap, including 25 individual menu-item pages — one each for the Smoked Salmon on Everything Bagel, The Ellicottville, Pain au Chocolat, Cinnamon Buns, Burrata Toast, the Berry Vinaigrette Salad and twenty more. They are live, they are indexable, and each one carries its own unique description. The bagel page reads “smoked salmon layered with red onion, tomato, capers, and fresh spinach, finished with creamy lemon dill cream cheese on your choice of bagel.” That is exactly the text an AI assistant would quote to somebody asking where to get a smoked salmon bagel in Ellicottville, and Google is not being told the page exists.

Also missing from the sitemap: the four /portfolio/ pages — Private Events, Events, Brunch Sips and the Seasonal Coffee menu — plus both catering PDFs. None of those 29 pages carries a canonical tag or a meta description either, so even where Google finds them it is given very little to work with.

The fix is small. In Yoast, exclude the layout, services-group, portfolio-group and testimonials post types from the sitemap and set them to noindex, then include the services and portfolio post types. Two settings screens. It is not a project.

The fair objection to this, and our answer

Somebody could reasonably push back that these menu pages are thin. They would have a point and we want to put the number on the table rather than have it found later. We compared four of them line by line: each page shares 49 lines of identical header, footer and navigation, and carries only 22 to 37 words of its own — the item name and one description sentence. That is genuinely thin by any measure.

We still think submitting them is right, for two reasons. The unique sentence is the part that matters, and it is the exact language somebody types when they want that thing. And a product or menu-item page with a real, unique description is a normal, legitimate page type — this is how every restaurant and retail site on the internet is structured. What would make us wrong is if the copy were not unique, and on 22 of 25 it is. So: fix the three placeholder pages, give all 25 a meta description and a canonical, and submit them. If you would rather be cautious, submit the four /portfolio/ pages and the strongest dozen menu items first and watch what happens.

Second finding — the structured data does not contain the bakery

Verified on all seven pages The site's structured data has no address, no phone number, no opening hours and no bakery or local-business type anywhere on it. What is there is the WordPress default set describing a generic organisation and a website. All of that information is on the page for a human to read — the footer carries 5 E Washington St., Suite 5a, the phone number and “Daily: 7:30 AM – 2:30 PM” — it is simply not in the machine-readable layer. For a single-location bakery this is the highest-value markup available and it is the one thing absent. Alpine, a block away, already has it.

Third finding — nine good answers, none of them marked as answers

Verified by render The About page carries a genuinely excellent FAQ written in exactly the phrasing people search: “What is the best bakery in Ellicottville for fresh, from-scratch baked goods?”, “How do I keep pastries fresh?”, “Why do preservative-free baked goods have a shorter shelf life?” Nine questions, real answers, well written. Nothing on the page marks them as questions and answers, so none of it is eligible to be surfaced or quoted. This is the same finding as Alpine's, on a different site, and in both cases the writing is done and only the wiring is missing.

Fourth finding — a café with no coffee prices on its website

Verified across every page The Bake Shop's site publishes no espresso menu at all. There is no cappuccino, no latte, no americano, no drip coffee price anywhere on the site. The only coffee page is /portfolio/seasonal-specialty-coffee-menu/, which lists five seasonal drinks at $6.25 — and that page is one of the 29 missing from the sitemap, so even it is not being offered to Google.

Alpine, a block away, publishes a full one: espresso $3.50, brewed coffee $3.50 / $4.50, cappuccino and latte $4.75 / $5.25, americano $4.00 / $4.75, cold brew $5.50. Somebody searching “how much is a coffee in Ellicottville” can find out about Alpine and cannot find out about the Bake Shop, which matters for a bakery whose morning trade is coffee.

03 Alpine Bistro
Alpine Bistro 5.5 / 10 ↑ from 5.0 in July

Alpine went from having no search layer at all to having a properly configured one in about two days. The Google profile remains the strongest single asset either business owns, at 4.6★. The catch is that the new structured data contradicts itself, and the version Google is most likely to believe says you close at five o'clock every day.

The main finding — three different sets of opening hours

Verified on all six pages Every page emits two separate business descriptions in the same block of structured data, and they disagree with each other and with the page itself.

Every single day is wrong in at least one of the two blocks. Both say you open an hour later than you do, which quietly costs the entire eight-to-nine morning coffee run. The second block is worse: it tells Google the bistro shuts at five o'clock every day including Friday and Saturday, which is precisely the après-ski and evening bourbon trade the downstairs bar exists for. Somebody checking on their phone at seven on a Saturday can be told you are closed while you are serving.

Where it lives: Rank Math is emitting both. Block one comes from its Local SEO business settings, block two from a Restaurant schema template on the pages. They need to be reconciled to one, matching the Google profile. Two related errors sit in the same place: block one has no town in the address at all — it carries the street, state and ZIP but never the word Ellicottville — and the second block's hasMenu field points at whichever page you happen to be on, so the About page tells Google the menu is at /about-us/. Verified by render The visible address on the homepage has the same gap: it reads “16 Washington Street, NY 14731” with no town.

Second finding — six duplicate pages presented to Google as French

Verified by character comparison Weglot is installed with one target language, French, and automatic translation switched on. It has generated /fr/, /fr/menu/, /fr/about-us/, /fr/bar/, /fr/event/ and /fr/ellicottville-coffee/. Each one returns 200, declares itself French in the page code, canonicals to itself, and is pointed at from every English page by an hreflang tag, so Google will find all six.

None of them is in French. We compared the French homepage against the English one: identical length to the character, 99.6% identical text, and not one French word in the body. So the site currently has six duplicates of itself declared as a language edition that does not exist, and none of them is in the sitemap.

Question rather than a finding Somebody enabled this deliberately and the strategy may well be sound — but we do not know who it was aimed at, so we are not going to tell you whether to keep it. Who was the French for? If there is a real audience, the translation needs to actually run and the pages belong in the sitemap. If there is not, removing the language is one setting. What should not continue is the current state, which carries the duplicate-content cost of a second language without delivering a second language.

Third finding — the bar page weighs 7.2 megabytes

Measured 24 August The /bar/ page loads 25 images, every one a PNG, totalling 7.2 MB, and not one of them has alt text. They are also all requested over http:// rather than https, which is true of 34 image references across the site. Converting those to JPEG or WebP would cut the page by roughly ninety per cent. On ski-town mobile signal this is the slowest thing either business owns, and it is the page selling the highest-margin drinks.

04 True of Both Sites
A good FAQ that no machine can read
Both sites carry a real FAQ written in buyer-intent phrasing — nine questions on the Bake Shop's About page, ten on Alpine's. Neither is marked up, so neither can be surfaced as an answer or quoted by an assistant. This is the single cheapest win available on either site, because the hard part, writing good answers, is already done on both.
Neither site is verified with Bing
No msvalidate.01 tag and no BingSiteAuth.xml on either domain. This matters more than it used to: ChatGPT's search runs on Bing's index. Verification is free and takes about five minutes per site.
Both publish a WordPress username
/author/admin/ on the Bake Shop and /author/Alpineadmin/ on Alpine both load and print the account name. The Bake Shop's is the default “admin”, which is the one worth changing, since publishing it hands over half of a login. Disable author archives in the SEO plugin.
Neither has a single dated page
No events, no seasonal posts, no weekend content, and no event markup on either site. Covered in section 01 — this is the one that answers the 22 August question, and it is the only item on this list that needs a habit rather than a fix.
The two sisters barely acknowledge each other
Alpine's menu page credits “bread baked daily at our sister bakery, Ellicottville Bake Shop” in its description, which is good. Beyond that the cross-linking is thin in both directions. Two well-reviewed businesses a block apart, sharing owners and an audience, still pass each other very little.
One optional tidy-up
new.ellicottvillebakeshop.com now returns 404 rather than redirecting. If Google indexed it during the weeks it was open, that credit is dropped rather than passed to the live site. Whether it was ever indexed we cannot see from outside. A redirect from the old subdomain to the live site is free and correct either way.
05 What We'd Do, In Order
01Fix the Bake Shop sitemap — Yoast, two settings screens
Exclude the layouts, layouts-group, portfolio-group, services-group and testimonials-group post types from the sitemap and set them to noindex. Then include the services and portfolio post types. That removes 16 pieces of furniture and adds 29 real pages, including 25 menu items. Highest ratio of value to effort on either site by a wide margin, and free.

Do one thing first, though. We read all 25 menu pages rather than a sample, and three of them still carry template placeholder text — the “Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet” filler — where the product description should be: /services/pesto-melt/, /services/almond-croissants/ and /services/cinnamon-buns/. The other 22 are clean and have real copy. Write three sentences before you submit the set, or you will be handing Google three placeholder pages. This took us a scan of all 25 to find and it is exactly the kind of thing that only shows up if you look at every one.
02Reconcile Alpine's opening hours to one set
In Rank Math, make the Local SEO business hours and the Restaurant schema template agree with each other and with the Google profile: open 8 AM, close 4 PM Sunday to Thursday, 8 PM Friday, 9 PM Saturday. While in there, add Ellicottville to the address and point hasMenu at the menu page. This is the only item on either site that is currently telling people something untrue. Free.
03Give the Bake Shop a Bakery listing in its structured data
Address, opening hours, phone, geo, and a link to the menu. Every value is already visible in the site footer. Yoast's local-business settings will do it, or Rank Math if the Bake Shop moves to it for consistency with Alpine. Free.
04Mark up both FAQs
Nineteen good answers already written across the two sites, none of them currently eligible to be surfaced. Both SEO plugins have an FAQ block that does this. While you are there, add vegan and gluten-free to Alpine's, which are the two questions genuinely not answered anywhere. Free.
05Publish one dated page a week, on either site
This is the answer to the 22 August question and the only recommendation here that is a habit rather than a task. One URL, who and what and when in the title, a few sentences in the body. A Saturday sourdough, a seasonal drink, race-weekend hours, a holiday order deadline. The Bake Shop's events page is already built and currently says there are no events. Fifteen minutes a week, and it is the item most likely to change what an AI answer says about Ellicottville.
06Verify both domains with Bing
Free, five minutes each, and it is the index ChatGPT searches. Do the same in Google Search Console if it is not already set up, because it is the only way any of this becomes measurable rather than inferred.
07Cut the weight of Alpine's bar page and re-enable pinch-to-zoom
25 PNGs at 7.2 MB converted to WebP or JPEG, alt text added, and the image URLs switched from http to https. Then remove maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=0 from the viewport tag so the site can be zoomed. The Bake Shop rebuild already did the viewport half.
08Housekeeping on both
Turn off author archives so the usernames stop being published. Replace the dead new.ellicottvillebakeshop.com logo reference in the Bake Shop's slide-out menu. Add canonicals and meta descriptions to the menu-item pages. Redirect the old staging subdomain to the live site. Confirm the Bake Shop's Google hours, since Google says somebody other than the owner last edited them.
06 Three Things That Are Decisions, Not Website Jobs

These are deliberately kept out of the list above, because none of them is ours to answer and each needs a person rather than a plugin.

Who was the French for?
Six French URLs exist, none of them in French. Finishing it and removing it are both defensible; leaving it as it is is the only option that costs something and returns nothing. That decision depends on an audience we cannot see from outside the site.
Are the two price lists meant to differ?
The one item class both businesses publish is seasonal specialty drinks, and they are $7.00 at Alpine and $6.25 at the Bake Shop — a 12% gap, two doors apart, same owners. On the everyday espresso menu no comparison is possible at all, because only Alpine publishes one. A deliberate difference is completely defensible; the two rooms are different and Alpine pours into the evening. Question, not a finding We simply cannot tell from outside whether it is deliberate or drift, and it is the sort of thing that is easier to answer now than after a customer asks. Worth ten minutes with both menus side by side.
Is anybody going to keep an events page fed?
Recommendation 05 works only if somebody owns it weekly. An events page that says there are no events is worse than not having one, and that is the current state. Better to decide who does it, or decide not to, than to build the page again and let it go quiet.
07 Run the AI Test Yourselves

Do not take our word for any of this. Ten minutes and you can see what we see. We have deliberately not scored a full AI-visibility sweep in this refresh, and the reason is worth knowing: the Bake Shop's website is three days old, and the assistants are still serving answers built from the previous site. Measuring now would produce a number about a website that no longer exists. The right time is mid-September, once the engines have re-crawled. What follows is the method, so you can run it whenever you like and so the September numbers mean something.

Ask these, exactly as written. Never search the business name until the last one, because that only proves the name exists.

For the Bake Shop: best bakery in Ellicottville NY · where can I get fresh bread in Ellicottville · recommend somewhere for breakfast near Holiday Valley · where can I get a smoked salmon bagel in Ellicottville · what do you know about Ellicottville Bake Shop

For Alpine: best coffee in Ellicottville NY · where to get breakfast in Ellicottville · recommend a bourbon bar near Holiday Valley · where can I get a drink after skiing in Ellicottville · what do you know about Alpine Bistro in Ellicottville

And the one from 22 August, for both: what is going on in Ellicottville New York this weekend. Score that one separately from the rest — it is a different kind of question and averaging it in would hide what it tells you.

Set each engine up properly, because “use a private window” is only correct for two of the four.

Google and Perplexity: an incognito window is the right control. It drops your account and your search history. On Google it will not change your location, which it reads from your internet connection, so results still reflect where the browser physically is.

ChatGPT: incognito will not do it. ChatGPT's Memory is attached to the account rather than the browser, it is switched on by default, and a private window does not touch it. Use Temporary Chat instead, the mode that ignores memory and history entirely. If you are signed in, also open Settings and read what Memory has stored about the business, which is worth knowing in its own right.

Grok: it requires a sign-in, so incognito only produces a login screen. Sign in and start a brand new conversation rather than continuing an existing thread, since an open thread carries its own context.

Have five different people run it, not one. These systems are not deterministic. The same question asked twice can name different businesses, so one person running it once is a sample of one and can mislead in either direction. Five people means five different accounts, histories and connections, and what comes back is the range rather than a single reading.

Keep it comparable. Use the prompts word for word, since rephrasing changes the answer. Same order, same day. Screenshot every answer, including the sources or citations panel where the engine shows one, because who gets cited matters as much as who gets named. Put everything in one shared folder labelled by person and engine. If everyone is on the same office connection, Google sees one location for all of them, which is fine as long as you know it.

Re-run the identical set monthly. The movement in those answers is the scoreboard for the rest of the work.

08 How We Checked, and What We Don't Know

Every claim above is tagged with how we know it, so you can tell our observations from our opinions.

Verified by render
We loaded the page in a real browser and looked at it, or measured the file directly. Everything about hours, addresses, page weight, the FAQ text and the events page is this.
Inferred
Our reading of why something is the way it is. There are two in this document, both written as questions, both in section 06.
What we cannot see
We hold no Search Console or Analytics access for either site. That means we can tell you what the sites say and what Google's public surfaces show, but not what anybody actually searched, how many impressions you got, or which pages Google has indexed. Read access turns most of the inferences in this document into measurements, and it is the single thing that would most improve the September re-run.
One thing we deliberately did not do
We did not score AI visibility this month, for the reason in section 07. We would rather report nothing than report a number that measures a website you have already replaced.
09 Three Ways To Do This

Nothing in this document is withheld. Every finding names the file, the setting or the page it lives in, and it is written that way on purpose.

1. Take it and run it yourselves

If you have somebody technical with the hours, this is the right answer and we will say so. Recommendations 01 through 04 and 06 are all free, all live inside settings screens you already have, and between them they carry most of the value in this document. Whoever rebuilt the Bake Shop site in a fortnight can do every one of them. Recommendation 05 is a habit and no outside party can own it for you anyway.

2. We specify, your people build

We turn each finding into something buildable — the exact settings, the exact values, the exact markup — and then check the result against the original finding once it ships. You keep the work and the keys. Quoted once we agree the scope.

3. Hand us the keys

We do the work, you review everything before it goes live, and every account stays in your name. Quoted once we agree the scope.

Do the free items first regardless of which route you pick. There is no version of this where paying somebody to tick two Yoast checkboxes is a good use of money.
