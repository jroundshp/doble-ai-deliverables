VPC is hard to find, and the reason is that the product pages are the least readable thing the company owns

This is the short version, written to be read in four minutes. Every claim in it links to the evidence in the full review.

The finding

Not the marketing pages. The products.

VPC publishes roughly 100 indexable pages across four properties. The pages that describe the actual products are the ones a search engine or an AI assistant can read least.

An outside firm told Corey that VPC is not findable. That verdict is correct, and it matches what our July audit found. What we can add is the mechanism, because “not findable” is a symptom, and the causes are specific, fixable, and mostly cheap.

Why this is worth real effort at VPC specifically VPC has to find new large buyers more or less continually. The six-year sales history shows a top of the revenue stack that rebuilds itself: most customers who reach the largest tier in a given year never reach it again, and fewer than half who buy in one year buy anything the next. That is ordinary for project work. It also means discovery is a structural requirement here rather than a marketing preference, and the pages a new buyer lands on are the ones a machine can read least.

The causes

Five of them, all fixable

01
The product catalog speaks in the quieter dialect

products.vpcfiberglass.com holds the real products across 289 URLs and carries no JSON-LD at all. Its microdata is noisy enough that a parser is told a product is called “All Categories Product Type”. Every page on the main site links into it 37 to 50 times, so VPC pours its link equity into a subdomain it may not control. Evidence

02
The play catalog has no product markup at all

Twenty-four product pages with names, descriptions and images on screen, and nothing a machine can read as a product. Evidence

03
Nothing is built to be quoted by an assistant

The knowledge exists in the resource pages and the 15 case studies. It is not written as questions and answers, which is the shape an assistant can lift. Evidence

04
The two satellite sites do not say which company they belong to

EasyRec and InterActive Play carry no Organization or LocalBusiness markup and no parent link back to VPC. InterActive Play’s homepage is still titled “Homepage - Interactive Play”. Evidence

05
VPC’s own markup vouches for a record nobody has filled in

VPC’s structured data names its ThomasNet profile as an authority on VPC. That profile reports “1-9 employees” and “Under $1 Mil”. There are 57 people at VPC and revenue has been above ten million every year since 2022. It is an hour of field editing, not a purchase. Evidence

The call

Two outside programs are on the table, and the order matters more than the choice

Growth Marketing Pro has proposed a six-month SEO and GEO roadmap at $48,000. Thomas quoted $1,300 a month on twelve months back in February. Both are good-faith programs, their research agrees with ours, and this document does not argue for either one over the other.

What neither one includes is the structural day. Both buy new pages. Neither repoints the three redirects that currently send an equipment-enclosure link to a storm drain manhole cover, closes the author archive still sitting in the sitemap, sets the default share image, adds the first product markup VPC would own anywhere, or tells the satellite sites which company they belong to.

That work is about a day, it needs no vendor, and it is the surface every new page either program publishes would land on. Do it first and both proposals are worth more. That sequencing is the only thing we argue for here. The full read on both programs

One correction

Revenue rank was the wrong way to weight this, including ours

InterActive Play is VPC’s largest division across six years at 44% of sales. It is not the largest this year: its lead over VPC Traditional closed every year from $4.40M in 2023 to $0.04M in 2025, and in 2026 to date Traditional is ahead.

The shape matters more than the rank. Across 33 months, InterActive Play booked nothing at all in six of them and took two thirds of its revenue in four. VPC Traditional booked revenue in every single month. Search compounds where demand is continuous and buyers are many. Against project revenue it is one input into a long specification process. The fix list does not move, but the reason under it does. The numbers by year

The blocker

One question decides whether the biggest item is a task or a project

First, the thing that makes this confusing products.vpcfiberglass.com looks like VPC’s own website because the address ends in vpcfiberglass.com. VPC owns the address. Thomas owns the building. VPC controls the DNS record, so VPC decides where that address points, but the pages it currently points at are built and run by Thomas on a system called the Navigator Platform. It is stated in the footer of every page: “Site created by Thomas Marketing Services and Powered by Navigator Platform.” Three of VPC’s four properties are WordPress. This one is not, and no WordPress login reaches it.

So the open question is whether Thomas will let VPC change what those pages emit. That is what “edit the templates” means: the catalog builds all 289 pages from a small number of shared page designs, so the broken product markup is one shared fault, not 289 separate ones.

If Thomas will change it, this is one edit to pages that already exist, measured in hours, and it is the cheapest large win in the estate. If Thomas will not, the only remaining fix is to move the product content onto the WordPress site VPC does operate, which is 144 item pages, 121 listing pages and 23 categories, plus a redirect map so the existing links keep working. That is a project with a budget and a timeline.

Until somebody answers, we cannot tell VPC what it costs, where it belongs in the order of work, or whether it is worth doing at all, because the honest answer to each is different in the two cases. It has been open since 14 August. Why it blocks so much

What the WordPress access already changes. It covers three of the four properties and it unblocks the biggest item we recommend, because the play catalog runs on WordPress and the drop-in pack for it is already built and demonstrated. The work can start as soon as the backup exists.

What it does not change. The catalog question. products.vpcfiberglass.com is the one property that is not WordPress, and it is the one holding the largest markup problem. That answer has to come from Thomas, and asking for it costs one email.

The order

What we would do, in this order

  • The half that needs no budget and no vendor. VPC’s own record on ThomasNet and the five other directories that mirror it, the three unfinished August items, an identity block on the two satellite sites, the questions written down as questions, and a page for the winterization service VPC sells every year and mentions nowhere.
  • Then product markup on the play catalog. Not because it is the biggest division, but because it is the only place VPC owns the markup outright, the drop-in pack is already built, and it becomes the template every other line reuses.
  • Then the catalog, once the question above is answered. Largest return in the estate on one answer, a project on the other.
  • Our recommendation on the website itself is a tune-up, not a rebuild. The site is fast, correctly configured and structurally sound. The findings are structure and data, not construction.

One optional ask, only if VPC wants the next round measured rather than argued: Search Console read access on the four properties. It takes a minute to grant and turns most of the reasoning in these documents into numbers.
