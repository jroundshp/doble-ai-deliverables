What we did not have in August

The July pricing plan and the August discount analysis were both built the same way: from the shape of the Ellicottville year. Festivals, ski weeks, mud season. That reasoning was sound, but it was reasoning. We had no competitor prices in either document, and no measurement of your own listing. Now we have both, on four dates chosen to sit at four different points on the calendar.

Two corrections to carry into the call. Alpine Overlook is a three-bedroom, so the two-bedroom units nearby are not its competition and comparing against a village-wide median understates you. And it is a new listing with no prior season, so there is no booking history to settle the occupancy question with. Both change the advice.

The short version

Your pricing is in better shape than the last document assumed. Your winter calendar is the problem.

Against three-bedroom listings, which are your real competition, Alpine Overlook sits about 15% above the village median on a quiet fall weekend. That is a defensible premium for the best address in town, and it is not the mispricing the flat-rate story implied.

But Alpine Overlook is not bookable on any of the three winter dates we checked. Christmas week, Presidents' weekend, and an ordinary January midweek are all closed. Whether those are bookings or blocks is now the single question that decides what to do about the 85-night winter offer, and it is the first thing to settle on this call.

You are priced sensibly, once you compare like with like

Against every listing in the village, your $486 a night looks 52% high. Against three-bedroom listings, the median that weekend is $421 and you sit about 15% above it. That is a premium, and for the best address in the village with a five-star record it is a defensible one. The village-wide number was misleading because it averages in studios.

You are the only one with no cleaning fee

Your four-night price is exactly twice your two-night price, so your cost to a guest is pure nightly rate. Every comparable we could decompose carries a fixed charge: $226 at Serenity Suites, $273 at the Old EVL Inn, $454 at Stay in Style. On a one or two-night stay that makes you materially cheaper than the sticker suggests, and nothing in your listing says so.

There is no going rate here

On one ordinary fall weekend, two-bedroom units on your block ranged from $354 a night to $1,397. Set aside the flat-rate outlier in the box below and it is still $354 to $762, better than double. Same village, same dates, same size. The listing charging the least has the most reviews. The one charging the most has none.

Presidents' weekend is already gone

Four of the six were not bookable for Feb 12–14 when we checked, roughly six months out. The two you could still book were the two most expensive. This is the clearest evidence yet for the rule the last document ended on: never discount that week.

The question that decides the winter

Alpine Overlook was not bookable on any of the three winter dates we checked. Christmas week, Presidents' weekend and an ordinary Tuesday in January, all closed, on a listing that has been up for a matter of months.

From outside we cannot tell a booking from a block, and the difference is the whole decision. If those nights are sold, you are running far ahead of a village that averages 27.7% and the winter block is plainly the wrong move. If they are held, whether for the block, for family, or because the calendar was never opened, then you are looking at an empty winter that you created rather than one the market handed you.

One listing prices every night the same, and it is instructive

Greg's Old World Charmer quoted exactly $2,794 for two nights on all four dates. Fall shoulder, a random January midweek, Presidents' weekend and Christmas, all identical. It was also one of only two of the six still open on Presidents' weekend. We cannot see anyone's booking calendar from outside, so we will not claim it never sells. What we can say is that the one listing on the block that refuses to move its price is also the one still sitting available on the weekend everybody else is spoken for.

The July plan puts in-season Monday to Thursday in the Low tier at $270, about 23% below the $350 shoulder anchor. Among three-bedroom listings the village barely makes that distinction: a ski-season Tuesday runs $407 against $421 for a fall Saturday, roughly level.

Those in-season midweek nights are the same 36 nights the 85-night block discount was aimed at, and the same nights the café perk was built for. If the village prices them level with a fall weekend and your plan marks them down by a quarter, the Low tier is cut deeper than it needs to be. Worth revisiting before the winter rates publish.

An earlier draft of this page made a stronger claim here, that ski midweek out-prices a fall weekend outright. That showed up in the all-sizes numbers and disappeared once we matched on bedroom count, so treat the size-matched version above as the real one.

Why these numbers are comparable and the July ones were not

04

The 81% question, and why it changed

August ended on a cliffhanger. Pricing the 85-night winter block to demand beats taking it as one guaranteed booking, but only above 81% winter occupancy. The plan was to settle it with last winter's booking history. There is no last winter. Alpine Overlook is a new listing, so that evidence does not exist and will not exist until spring.

The village, measured

Across 136 active listings in the Village of Ellicottville over August 2025 through July 2026: average occupancy 27.7%, average daily rate $320, average annual revenue $25,598. Peak season runs 41.8%; the best month, January, reaches 48.1%.

What a first season usually looks like

A listing with no history ranks lower in search and converts worse, because guests sort on reviews and Airbnb sorts on booking record. First seasons normally land below the market average, not on it. That pushes 81% further out of reach, not closer.

Except that you are not behaving like a normal new listing

Nine reviews, a five-star average, and a Guest Favourite badge inside a first season is a good start by any measure, and the badge is not handed out for a quiet calendar. On top of that, all three winter dates we checked are already closed.

So the honest position is this. The market data says a new listing should expect a thin first winter and should therefore welcome a guaranteed booking. Your own early signals point the other way. Those two readings cannot both be right, and the thing that separates them is whether your closed winter nights are sold or held.

Revised recommendation

Do not take the 85-night block as written, and do not decide anything before checking the calendar. August leaned toward the block on the strength of a 81% break-even nobody could test. We can now say the break-even is unreachable in a first season on market-typical performance, which would argue for taking it. But a guaranteed winter is the wrong trade for a listing that is already filling one.

If the winter dates are booked: turn the season-long discount off entirely. You are outperforming a 27.7% market and a block at $344 a night would cost you the best weeks of the year.

If they are blocked: open them, price them to the calendar, and give it four to six weeks before entertaining any season-long offer. A held night looks identical to a sold-out night on this page, and only one of them pays.

The part the revenue arithmetic misses

An 85-night booking is one guest. It earns you one review, maybe two. The same 85 nights sold as weekends and short stays might earn you twenty-five.

In your first year that difference is not a rounding error, it is the whole asset. Reviews are what let the Old EVL Inn condo sit at $354 a night with 127 of them and stay booked straight through the winter, while the flat two doors down asks $762 with none and sits open. You are nine reviews into building the first position and considering a deal that would stall you there until spring. The winter block does not just cost you peak rates. It costs you a season of proof.

If you want the certainty anyway, split the difference

There is a version of this that keeps most of the guarantee and none of the damage. Carve the marquee weeks out of the block. Christmas through New Year, MLK, Presidents' week and Mardi Gras stay on your own calendar at tiered rates with a minimum stay. Everything else, the soft midweek stretches of January and March, goes to the long guest at a discount.

You keep the four windows that carry the year's best rates, you keep the short stays that generate reviews, and the long guest still gets a genuine deal on the nights that were hardest to sell anyway. It is the same logic as the August fixes, applied to the calendar instead of the rate card.

05

What the block already does that you don't

Three of the four fixes we recommended in August turn out to be in use within a few doors of you. That is worth knowing before the call, because it means none of this is exotic.

  • Minimum stays on the weeks that sell themselves

    Jake's flat does not sell Christmas by the night at all. It is a seven-night minimum. Greg runs a four-night minimum over Fall Festival. We suggested three nights on your marquee windows and it now looks conservative. A long stay is welcome to include Christmas week. It is not welcome to arrive on the 26th and leave on the 28th at a discounted nightly rate.

  • Letting the rate move with the week

    Serenity Suites moves from $520 on a January midweek to $681 at Christmas. Robin's house moves $616 to $811. Neither is doing anything clever. They are simply not charging Christmas prices in mud season, or mud-season prices at Christmas.

  • Publishing the licence number

    Of the six, only the Old EVL Inn condo states its Village of Ellicottville rental licence in the listing text. It is a small trust signal that costs nothing, and it is the sort of thing that matters more each year as villages tighten short-term rental rules.

  • Watching what Airbnb pushes at you

    Two of the six were carrying Airbnb-generated discounts when we looked, with prompts like save $136 on these select nights and your price is below the 60-day average. Airbnb's nudges optimise for a booking, not for your revenue. They will happily push you to discount a night that never needed help. This is the same failure mode as a flat length-of-stay percentage, arriving through a different door.

The honest caveat about all of them

Ellicottville is a small market. 136 active listings in the village means these tools are working from a thin sample, and their confidence drops accordingly on any single night. They are very good at the shape of a season and only fair at the price of one Tuesday.

More to the point, none of them knows about the café. No pricing engine on the market can tell you that the right move on a soft February Tuesday is a breakfast sandwich from downstairs rather than $40 off the rate. That lever is yours because you own the whole building, and it stays a manual decision no matter what you subscribe to.
