What we did not have in August

The July pricing plan and the August discount analysis were both built the same way: from the shape of the Ellicottville year. Festivals, ski weeks, mud season. That reasoning was sound, but it was reasoning. We had no competitor prices in either document. Now we do, for six listings within a few blocks of you and for the wider village, on four dates chosen to sit at four different points on the calendar.

The short version

The calendar was right. The advice on the 85-night block was too cautious.

The village really does swing about 1.76× from a quiet fall weekend to Christmas week, which is close to the spread the plan already recommended. That part needs no revision.

What we could not see in August was occupancy. The typical village listing runs 27.7% for the year and about 41.8% in its best season. The break-even we published for the winter block was 81%. That gap is large enough to change the recommendation, and Section 04 works through it.

There is no going rate here

On one ordinary fall weekend, two-bedroom units on your block ranged from $354 a night to $1,397. Set aside the flat-rate outlier in the box below and it is still $354 to $762, better than double. Same village, same dates, same size. The listing charging the least has the most reviews. The one charging the most has none.

Presidents' weekend is already gone

Four of the six were not bookable for Feb 12–14 when we checked, roughly six months out. The two you could still book were the two most expensive. This is the clearest evidence yet for the rule the last document ended on: never discount that week.

One listing prices every night the same, and it is instructive

Greg's Old World Charmer quoted exactly $2,794 for two nights on all four dates. Fall shoulder, a random January midweek, Presidents' weekend and Christmas, all identical. It was also one of only two of the six still open on Presidents' weekend. We cannot see anyone's booking calendar from outside, so we will not claim it never sells. What we can say is that the one listing on the block that refuses to move its price is also the one still sitting available on the weekend everybody else is spoken for.

The one place the model looks wrong

Notice the first two bars. A Tuesday to Thursday in ski season prices higher in this village than a Saturday night in October, by about 15%. The July plan has that the other way round: in-season Monday to Thursday sits in the Low tier at $270, below the $350 shoulder anchor.

That matters more than it sounds, because those in-season midweek nights are the same 36 nights the 85-night block discount was aimed at, and the same nights the café perk was designed to close. If the village really pays a premium for a ski Tuesday over a fall Saturday, the Low tier is set too low and the softest nights of your winter are not as soft as we assumed.

Treat this one as a flag, not a finding. The two medians come from whichever listings Airbnb happened to show for each date, and it is not the same set both times, so part of that 15% could be a different mix of properties rather than a real difference in price. It is cheap to settle and expensive to ignore, which makes it worth checking before the winter rates publish.

Read this one carefully

These medians include each host's cleaning and service fees, and your $350 does not. So the two are not directly comparable as levels, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. What is comparable is the shape, because fees fall on every listing alike. The village moves 1.76× between its quiet and busy weekends. You move once, to nothing.

04

The 81% question, answered

August ended on an honest cliffhanger. Pricing the 85-night winter block to demand beats taking it as one guaranteed booking, but only above 81% winter occupancy, and we did not know your occupancy. We still do not. But we now know the village's, and it changes what we would advise.

The village, measured

Across 136 active listings in the Village of Ellicottville over August 2025 through July 2026: average occupancy 27.7%, average daily rate $320, average annual revenue $25,598.

Even the good months

Peak season, meaning December, January and February together, runs 41.8% occupancy at $352 a night. The single best month of the year, January, reaches 48.1%. The worst, April, sits at 16.3%.

Revised recommendation

If the 85-night winter block is a real offer, take it. That condition matters: August worked from Corey's sketch of what a season-long discount would produce, not from a guest with a name. Nothing below argues for going out and finding one. It argues for what to do when one appears. The break-even we published asks you to fill 81% of the winter at rates well above the village average. The best month the typical village listing manages is 48.1%. Beating a guaranteed booking would mean roughly doubling the occupancy of a normal listing here while charging more than one. That is not a bet we would tell you to make on a maybe.

The four fixes in Section 05 still apply, and they matter more now, not less. Take the block, but do not take it at 25%, and do not let the discount reach the marquee weeks untouched.

What would change our mind, and it is still the same thing

That 27.7% is an average across 136 listings, and averages carry a lot of poorly run properties. You are above your own restaurant, in the middle of the village, with a kitchen downstairs. There is a real case that you outperform the average by a wide margin.

  • Your booking history from last winter. It answers this outright and takes about ten minutes. It was the first ask in August and it is still the first ask.
  • Your live listing URL. We could not find your unit in the search results, so every figure here is your neighbours, not you. With the link we can measure yours exactly the same way.
  • Your cleaning fee. It is the missing piece for turning your $350 into a number comparable to the table in Section 02.
05

What the block already does that you don't

Three of the four fixes we recommended in August turn out to be in use within a few doors of you. That is worth knowing before the call, because it means none of this is exotic.

  • Minimum stays on the weeks that sell themselves

    Jake's flat does not sell Christmas by the night at all. It is a seven-night minimum. Greg runs a four-night minimum over Fall Festival. We suggested three nights on your marquee windows and it now looks conservative. A long stay is welcome to include Christmas week. It is not welcome to arrive on the 26th and leave on the 28th at a discounted nightly rate.

  • Letting the rate move with the week

    Serenity Suites moves from $520 on a January midweek to $681 at Christmas. Robin's house moves $616 to $811. Neither is doing anything clever. They are simply not charging Christmas prices in mud season, or mud-season prices at Christmas.

  • Publishing the licence number

    Of the six, only the Old EVL Inn condo states its Village of Ellicottville rental licence in the listing text. It is a small trust signal that costs nothing, and it is the sort of thing that matters more each year as villages tighten short-term rental rules.

  • Watching what Airbnb pushes at you

    Two of the six were carrying Airbnb-generated discounts when we looked, with prompts like save $136 on these select nights and your price is below the 60-day average. Airbnb's nudges optimise for a booking, not for your revenue. They will happily push you to discount a night that never needed help. This is the same failure mode as a flat length-of-stay percentage, arriving through a different door.

The honest caveat about all of them

Ellicottville is a small market. 136 active listings in the village means these tools are working from a thin sample, and their confidence drops accordingly on any single night. They are very good at the shape of a season and only fair at the price of one Tuesday.

More to the point, none of them knows about the café. No pricing engine on the market can tell you that the right move on a soft February Tuesday is a breakfast sandwich from downstairs rather than $40 off the rate. That lever is yours because you own the whole building, and it stays a manual decision no matter what you subscribe to.
