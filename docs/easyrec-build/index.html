Live now · Done

Both tools are on the bench page

They sit on new.easyrecreation.com/easy-stack-bench/, one above the colour grid and one below it. Both are single blocks of HTML in Elementor widgets. No plugin, no licence, nothing to keep updated. Deleting the widget removes the tool and leaves the rest of the page alone.

The 3D viewer

It runs on a bench model that was already in your media library from July, 69543_Depew_Bench_1.glb. We checked the file before using it: valid glTF 2 out of SolidWorks, one mesh, 2,580 triangles, 115 KB, and it measures 77 by 18 by 18 inches, which is the bench. At that size it will open quickly on a phone.

The sixteen colours now drive the model itself. Pick a swatch under the viewer and the bench changes colour while you are turning it. All sixteen come off Conrad's single export, so there is nothing for him to redo and nothing to keep in sync.

To show a different product, change the file name in the widget to another GLB's URL. Everything else stays as it is.

The colour and logo preview

Sixteen swatches, a logo upload, drag to position, slider to size. The logo file never leaves the visitor's browser. Three hidden fields carry the colour, the file name and the placement, so when you point a quote form at them the rep gets all three with the request instead of a phone call.

The sixteen images are your own, read straight off the colour grid further down the page rather than typed in by us, so the pairing cannot drift from what the page already shows.

One limit. Those sixteen files are small, mostly 209 pixels wide, and nothing larger exists on the server. We capped the preview at a size where they still look like a bench rather than blowing them up. The upgrade is a single photograph: a plain white bench with no graphics on it, square on from the side. With that one shot, all sixteen colours come off it and a customer's logo sits on a real surface.

Answering your note · Half of it is live

The configurator question

You asked whether we can keep the 3D file, lay a logo on it and change the colour of the bench. Those two halves have different answers, and it is worth separating them because one of them is already done.

Colour in 3D: yes, and it is running now

The model has one material, which is the easy case. The viewer recolours it live, so the swatches you already had on the page now change the bench you are turning. Go and click a few.

A logo in 3D: not with this file, and the reason is fixable

We opened Conrad's export and read what is inside it. It carries geometry and surface normals, and nothing else: no texture coordinates, no textures, no images. Texture coordinates are the thing that tells a 3D program where on a surface a picture goes. With none in the file, there is nowhere for artwork to land. That is a property of how the file was exported, not a limit of the viewer.

Conrad could re-export with a mapped panel on the side. It would work, and it would need doing again on every model he changes, which is a standing cost on a catalogue that grows.

Which is why your second idea is the better one

You offered a static image with a flat area for the logo, the way Yeti does it. That is the one we would take. A logo only has to look right from one angle to convince somebody, and it looks more convincing sitting on a photograph than on a render.

So: the 3D view for turning the bench and choosing a colour, a photograph for putting a customer's badge on it. Not a compromise. They are good at different jobs.

What the photograph needs to be. All sixteen colours are produced from this one shot, so it is worth getting right once.

  • A white bench, RAL 9016, with no graphics on it.
  • Square on from the side, camera at bench height rather than looking down at it.
  • The whole side panel in frame, evenly lit, with no hard shadow crossing it.
  • Plain background, and as large a file as you have. 2000 pixels wide or better.

There is a near miss already on the live site at 2020/02/IMG_3806-scaled.jpg, 2560 by 1707. It is exactly the right shot except the InterActive Play logo is already printed on the panel, so it cannot carry somebody else's. Same setup, clean bench, and we are there.

Needs a click in wp-admin · Over to you

The one thing we could not do from here

The new site is still open to Google. We checked again after finishing: robots.txt invites crawling and no page carries a noindex tag.

This one switch is not reachable through the WordPress API, so it cannot be done the way everything else here was done. It is a checkbox:

  1. Settings → Reading.
  2. Tick Discourage search engines from indexing this site, and save.
  3. Untick it the day you launch, and not before.

We deliberately did not reach for a workaround. There is a way to force the same result with a code snippet, and it would have left a second switch for somebody to find at launch. A site that stays invisible after go-live because of a forgotten snippet is a worse problem than the one being solved.

The pool measuring form

Parked rather than dropped, since the configurator is plainly the thing on your mind. Still the one we have not built. MetForm keeps its forms out of the WordPress API entirely, so unlike everything else on this page it cannot be built remotely. It is a job in the MetForm screens.

There is a second reason to slow down on it. A quote form that is built but whose email routing is wrong is worse than the Google Form, because nobody notices until a customer asks why they never heard back. Before it gets built, someone needs to say where submissions go and whether they should also be stored in WordPress.

The four things the current form does that generate the phone calls are on the earlier page, and they have not changed: the first water depth point has no label of its own, nothing asks which gutter profile the pool has, multi-point steps arrive as one comma-separated string, and there is no way to attach a photo.

Questions, none of them urgent

  • Who is easyrecreation0mh? There is a third administrator on the new site under that name, on a Gmail address, shown as "Crystal Nova". It may be perfectly ordinary, from the install or the theme purchase. If nobody recognises it, it should come off.
  • Is an SEO plugin part of the plan? There is none on the new site right now, so no page has a meta description and there is no product markup. That is not a problem today. It becomes one the day the site launches, because it is far easier to build in before go-live than to retrofit after.
  • The sitemap answers 404. A curious one, and worth settling before launch rather than after. wp-sitemap.xml returns a perfectly good index, three entries, contents correct and updating. It just returns it with a 404 status attached, and Google reads the status, not the body. Nothing to do about it while the site is closed off, but it wants an answer the week you open it up.
  • Can somebody shoot the white bench? The one photograph described further up is the single thing that would most improve the logo preview, and it needs a camera rather than any of us. A plain white bench, no graphics, square on from the side, against a wall.
