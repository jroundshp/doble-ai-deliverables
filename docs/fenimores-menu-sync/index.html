The three new menus, and what it took to line the website up with them

Breakfast, lunch and dinner all changed. The pages people read were nearly right. The layer underneath them, the part search engines and AI assistants actually quote, was still describing the old card. This is what we found and what we changed.

01

The short version

Every menu page has two layers. There is the copy a guest reads, and there is a block of machine-readable data underneath it that tells Google and ChatGPT what the restaurant serves and what it costs. Both have to say the same thing.

When the three new cards went in, the visible copy was updated and the machine layer was not. So the page a guest saw was showing the new dinner card while the data underneath was still offering Moody Blue Chips, Tuna Nachos, a Pork Belly Banh Mi Bowl and four desserts the kitchen no longer makes. An assistant asked about dinner at Fenimore's would have answered from the old card.

That is now fixed, along with a handful of price and spelling items. The menu page carries 48 dishes in the machine layer and they match the page exactly. The breakfast page, which had no machine-readable menu at all, now carries 37.

05

Everything changed today

Seven items, each verified on the live page afterwards.

  1. Lunch add-protein prices corrected to the 9-8 card, in both places they appear.
  2. Side Salad $4 added to the lunch Sides list, which now matches the card's four.
  3. “Loaded French Toast” capitalised on the breakfast page.
  4. The menu page's machine layer rebuilt from scratch against the live page: 48 dishes across 14 sections, lunch and dinner. Every retired item is gone and every new one is in, including Salmon Ball, both flatbreads, the new dessert four, and Kids Pasta.
  5. The breakfast page given a machine-readable menu for the first time: 37 items across five sections, with the vegetarian dishes marked as vegetarian so a dietary search can find them.
  6. /brunch/ now redirects to /breakfast/ instead of returning a 404. Reversible in one click if brunch ever becomes a page of its own.
  7. A private events page created as a draft, with the structure, the questions and every answer we can already write. It is deliberately not published, so a half-finished page cannot be indexed. Each open item is marked in the page itself, ready for Bob's answers.

06

What Tammy is doing, and what not to change

Tammy is bringing Toast in line with the three cards. That matters here for one reason: where Toast and the website disagree, the card decides, and on the wings the website was already right. Nothing on the site should move to match a Toast figure without checking the card first.

The two menus now agree on the kids section in both directions. Pasta was added to the website and the Hot Dog, which Toast does not carry, is gone from both.

07

Still open

  • Bob's answers on private events. Buyout, lead time, deposit or minimum, outside cake and cutting fee, the parking count, and final copy for the three experiences and the Celebration Brunch. The draft page has a marked slot for each.
  • The capacity split. We have 95 indoors and 140 in summer with the patio. Still needed: whether those are seated or standing figures, which months the patio is open, and what happens to a large booking if the weather turns.
  • Six to ten real photographs of the room. No stock and no generated images.
  • The patio is invisible online. It appears nowhere on the website, in the machine layer, or on the Google profile, and it is the thing that takes a party from 95 to 140.

One thing worth a look rather than an action. The homepage now shows the same salmon photograph twice, in the second and third slots, and the rice bowl that used to sit between them is gone. It may well be deliberate. If it is not, it happened somewhere in today's edits and is a two-minute swap.
