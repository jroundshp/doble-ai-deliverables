01

We agree with the outside read

We are not going to spend this document defending our own. The outside verdict is correct, it matches what our July audit found, and an outsider saying it unprompted is worth more to VPC than us saying it again.

What we can add is the mechanism, because “not findable” is a symptom and there are four specific causes, all of them fixable, and two of them cheap.

The short version: VPC publishes roughly 100 indexable pages across four properties. The pages that describe the actual products are the least machine-readable things the company owns. Not the marketing pages. The products.

Why this is worth real effort at VPC specifically VPC has to find new large buyers more or less continually, which makes discovery a structural requirement rather than a marketing preference. The six-year sales history shows a top of the revenue stack that rebuilds itself. Most of the customers who reach the largest tier in a given year never reach it again, and fewer than half the customers who buy in one year buy anything the next. That is ordinary for project work and nothing here reads it as a fault. It does decide where effort belongs. A business carried by repeat orders can afford to be hard to find. A business that has to source a fresh set of large buyers every year cannot, and the pages a new buyer would land on are the ones a machine can read least.

The thing that makes this harder than a normal site review VPC is not one kind of business, and findability does different work for each half. Some lines are catalog-shaped: Parshall flumes, manhole systems, standard Enduro enclosures, the EasyRec ADA stairs and stackable benches. A buyer already knows roughly what the part is and searches for it by name, so those pages have to exist, carry the product name, and be legible as products. The rest is engineer-to-order: shelters and buildings, ductwork, fans, custom weirs and baffles, and the InterActive Play attractions, where a consulting engineer arrives with a spec and a drawing and no part number exists yet. Those pages are not competing to match a search term, they are competing to prove capability, which means named projects and stated tolerances rather than a catalog entry. VPC currently under-serves both, but it under-serves them in different ways, and a single fix list that ignores the split will over-invest in one half.

Cause one: the catalog speaks, but in the quieter of the two dialects

products.vpcfiberglass.com carries Product markup as microdata and no JSON-LD anywhere, and the microdata is noisy enough that a machine reading it gets the breadcrumb where the product name should be.

This is the ThomasNet-hosted catalog, and it is where the actual products live: FRP fans, louvers and dampers, sound enclosures, ductwork, flumes, manhole systems, staff and tank gauges, troughs, weirs, baffles, ladders, platforms, railings, tanks, catch basins. Every one of the 23 pages on the main site links into it at least 37 times, rising to 46 from the homepage and 50 each from the industries page and the ductwork resource. So VPC is pouring its internal link equity into a subdomain it does not control. Corrected 6 September: an earlier version of this section said that subdomain carried no structured data at all. It does. Item pages declare schema.org/Product in microdata, with a real SKU, a real description, images and a set of PropertyValue attributes that varies per product, between three and twenty on the pages we sampled. Our first check looked only for application/ld+json and found none, which is true and was reported as though it were the whole answer. It was not.

What is actually wrong with it is quality rather than absence. On the pages we read, the name property picks up breadcrumb text rather than the product, so a parser is told the product is called “All Categories Product Type” before it reaches “Customized Fiberglass Buildings”. Promotional items nested inside the same scope contribute their own SKUs. And Offer appears on one of the five item pages we sampled and on none of the other four. So the honest reading is present, inconsistent and hard to trust, not missing. That is a materially different recommendation: not “add markup” but “clean up what is emitted and add JSON-LD, which is the format Google and the assistants most reliably act on.”

The PropertyValue attributes are the find worth carrying into the quoting conversation. They vary per product and they are real specification data, which means the Navigator platform holds a structured attribute model for the catalog-shaped families. That is the same shape of data a first-pass configured quote would need.

This was the July audit's highest-leverage recommendation and it has moved less than it looks. Re-checked 5 September: still 200, still 73,567 bytes, still zero JSON-LD. It is also the one item we have never been able to price, because it depends on a question nobody has answered.

So name the fork rather than carrying it another month. The recommendation actually changes depending on one answer, and the two branches are not the same size of work:

  • If VPC can edit the ThomasNet templates Clean the existing markup and add JSON-LD This is the highest-leverage item anywhere in the estate and it is contained. The names, descriptions, images and per-product attributes are already written, already on the page and already partly marked up. The sitemap exists and lists all 289 URLs. What is missing is a clean Product name that is not the breadcrumb, a consistent Offer, and a JSON-LD block alongside the microdata. That is a template change in one place. Nothing has to be migrated and nothing has to be rewritten.
  • If VPC cannot edit them Move the product truth onto a domain VPC controls Then the honest framing is that the catalog is rented, and no amount of schema advice reaches it. The work becomes building product pages for the traditional lines on vpcfiberglass.com itself, starting with the catalog-shaped ones a buyer searches by name, and letting ThomasNet keep doing what it is good at, which is industrial discovery and RFQ. That is a different size of project and it needs a budget conversation rather than a ticket.

One fact that was available all along and that we had not read. The footer of every page on both vpcfiberglass.com and products.vpcfiberglass.com says: “Site created by Thomas Marketing Services and Powered by Navigator Platform.” So the two properties are not the same kind of thing. The main site is WordPress on VPC's own domain running a theme named for the company, which makes it VPC's to change with a login. The catalog is Thomas's Navigator product wearing a VPC subdomain. That does not settle whether the templates can be edited, which is still the question below, but it does say who to ask.

Two smaller defects sit on the catalog as well, both the platform's rather than VPC's. One of its two search forms posts to a staging hostname, products.vpcfiberglass.stage.thomasnet-navigator.com, written as a relative path so it resolves against VPC's own domain and returns a 404. And its robots file opens User-agent: *,SemrushBot-BA, ..., which is not a valid user-agent token, so the hundred or so Disallow rules beneath it likely match no crawler at all.

The meeting question: who controls products.vpcfiberglass.com, and can anyone at VPC edit its templates? That single answer decides which of the two branches above is the plan. It has been open since 14 August.

04

Cause two: the play catalog has no product markup at all

The play catalog carries no product markup of any kind, in either format.

Re-confirmed 5 Sep

We sampled the play catalog directly. /products/aquatic-play-structures/triwave-100/, /products/water-play-features/jet-stream/, /products/splash-pads/ and the /products/ hub all carry exactly one JSON-LD block, and it contains WebPage, BreadcrumbList and WebSite. Nothing that says this page is a product, who makes it, or what it is for.

So when somebody asks a search engine or an assistant for a commercial splash pad manufacturer, the pages that should answer are structurally indistinguishable from an About page. This is the property where the “no product markup” finding holds without qualification. The traditional catalog is a different case, covered in section 03: there the markup exists as microdata, and the problem is that it is noisy and carries no JSON-LD. That is the whole of the gap the outside firm described. VPC ranks for what fiberglass is because those pages are written like articles. It does not rank for what VPC sells because the pages that sell either carry no product markup at all, as here, or carry it in a form a parser struggles to trust, as on the traditional catalog.

The markup is only half of it, and the cheaper half. A product page also has to say the obvious things in plain sentences, because those are the sentences an assistant lifts and a buyer scans for. Who makes this, and under which company. Who it is for, in their own words, a municipal wastewater plant or a campground operator rather than “our clients”. And which kind of purchase it is: whether the buyer is configuring something that already exists in sizes and options, or commissioning something built to their drawing. That last distinction is the one the estate never states anywhere, and it is the one that tells a buyer whether to expect a price or a conversation. We are not proposing that anyone publish lead times or tolerances we have not been given. State the shape of the transaction, which VPC already knows, and leave the numbers to the people who own them.

10

The decision

Start with the free half, because it needs no budget and no vendor. Items 01, 02, 03, 06 and 10 live inside settings screens and text fields VPC already controls: VPC’s own record on ThomasNet and the five other directories that mirror it, the three unfinished August items, an identity block on the two satellite sites, the questions written down as questions, and a page for the winterization service. Between them they close most of the gap the outside firm identified, and not one of them waits on the catalog question.

Then item 04, the Product schema on the play catalog. It is a template change on a domain VPC certainly owns, it is the first Product markup the company would have anywhere, and it is the first thing that makes VPC answerable to a buying question rather than an educational one. It also produces the template the traditional lines reuse later. Those three reasons are the whole case, and they are enough. This page has argued for the item on revenue grounds twice, in both directions, and section 02a retires that line of argument: InterActive Play leads on a six-year total, has been overtaken in 2026 to date, and books two thirds of its revenue in four months of every thirty-three. Rank was never the right metric here. Ownership of the markup is.

Item 05, the catalog, is a decision before it is a budget. Nobody can price it until somebody says whether VPC can edit the ThomasNet templates on products.vpcfiberglass.com. One answer makes it a contained template change with the largest return in the estate. The other makes it a project to move the product truth onto a domain VPC controls. Both are legitimate. Neither can start while the question is open, and it has been open since 14 August.

Our recommendation on the website itself is a tune-up, not a rebuild, on the reasoning in section 08. If brand or look is the real blocker, say so and that changes, but the findings here are structure, not construction.

And on the two programs in section 01b, the recommendation is a sequence rather than a verdict. We are not the right party to tell VPC whether to spend $48,000 with Growth Marketing Pro or $1,300 a month with Thomas, and this document does not try to. Both are buying new pages, both are reasonable things to buy, and their research agrees with ours. What we would say is that the free half above should go first, and it can go first this week. It costs nothing, it needs no vendor, and every page either program publishes lands on that surface. Publishing new content onto a site whose products carry no product markup, whose satellites do not name their own company, and whose redirects send an enclosure link to a manhole cover is paying full price for a fraction of the return. Do the day of work, then start the program. That sequencing is free to adopt and it is the only thing we are arguing for here.

What used to block all of this was that no single name sat against the website. That was the constraint from July until the 9 September call, and it was the one thing on this page that could never have been bought or built. It is settled now, and what is left is mechanical: a backup held somewhere other than the server, then a named admin account on the three WordPress sites, then the list above worked in order with a change summary after each pass. Everything in the free half can start the day the credentials exist.

One optional ask, only if VPC wants the next round measured rather than argued: Search Console read access on the four properties. It takes a minute to grant, and it converts most of the reasoning in this document into numbers.
