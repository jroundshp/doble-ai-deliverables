Doble AI · Findability review · 1 September 2026

VPC is four websites, and the two carrying the products are the two machines can read least

An outside firm told Corey that VPC is not findable and not searchable. We went and looked, across all four properties this time. They are right, and the mechanism is more specific than the verdict.

Every figure below was pulled on 1 September 2026. Supersedes nothing — the July audit of vpcfiberglass.com stands, and this widens the frame to the three properties it never covered.

00

First, something that has not happened before on this account

Three of the six items from the 17 August fix guide have shipped. Nobody replied to the email. Somebody just did the work, and the site's own timestamps put it between 25 and 28 August.

  • The structured-data bug is fixed. @context now reads https://schema.org without the www, and @type is correctly cased LocalBusiness. It carries the Medina address, the phone number and six authority links including D&B, BBB, ThomasNet and Bloomberg.
  • /locations/test-location/ is gone. It now returns 404.
  • The Enduro duplicates are gone as duplicates. Nothing self-canonicalises any more and only one Enduro page serves content: /vpc-case-studies/enduro-standard-equipment-enclosure-5/. The root-level -2, -3 and -4 copies now 404, and both bare URLs redirect to that surviving page. The redirects were mapped carelessly, though, and that is worth five minutes. See directly below.

That matters more than the three items themselves. It means there is somebody at or around VPC who reads these documents and acts on them, which is the thing we could not establish in August. It also means the argument that follows is not landing in an empty room.

Three URLs still named “enduro-standard-equipment-enclosure” now redirect to three unrelated projects.

Confirmed 1 Sep
/vpc-case-studies/enduro-standard-equipment-enclosure-2/ 301 -> /vpc-case-studies/custom-fiberglass-building/ /vpc-case-studies/enduro-standard-equipment-enclosure-3/ 301 -> /vpc-case-studies/36-inch-parshall-flume-fiberglass/ /vpc-case-studies/enduro-standard-equipment-enclosure-4/ 301 -> /vpc-case-studies/storm-drain-manhole-cover/ /vpc-case-studies/enduro-standard-equipment-enclosure-5/ 200 (the real Enduro page)

Whoever cleaned the duplicates appears to have pointed the leftover numbered URLs at whichever case study came next in the list rather than at the Enduro page. So a link, a bookmark or an old search result for an equipment enclosure now lands a visitor on a custom building, a 36-inch Parshall flume, or a storm drain manhole cover.

A 301 tells a search engine “this page moved here, and it is the same thing.” These three say that about products that have nothing to do with each other. It is a smaller problem than leaving duplicates up, so this is still a net improvement, and it is a five-minute correction: point all three at -5, or let them 404 the way their root-level twins already do.

Still open from that same list No og:image and no Twitter card, so anything shared into LinkedIn or a text message renders without an image. /author/tmswebsiteadminthomasnet-com/ still loads, and still has its own submitted sitemap. /locations/ still has no meta description at all.

01

We agree with the outside read

We are not going to spend this document defending our own. The outside verdict is correct, it matches what our July audit found, and an outsider saying it unprompted is worth more to VPC than us saying it again.

What we can add is the mechanism, because “not findable” is a symptom and there are four specific causes, all of them fixable, and two of them cheap.

The short version: VPC publishes roughly 100 indexable pages across four properties. The pages that describe the actual products are the least machine-readable things the company owns. Not the marketing pages. The products.

02

The map, which the July audit did not have

The July audit graded vpcfiberglass.com and scored it 6.4. That number was honest for what it measured and it covered one of four properties. VPC runs five divisions across three domains plus a catalog subdomain, and three of those four were never looked at.

02b

What is genuinely good, so the list below is not read as a demolition

  • The divisions page does cross-linking properly. It links all five product lines, and the anchor text carries the brand names — “EasyRec”, “InterActivePlay”, “View CFT Product Line”. This is a thing most sites get wrong and VPC gets right.
  • The Google Business Profile is complete and correct. Categorised Fiberglass supplier, 10478 Ridge Rd Medina NY, (585) 735-9668, hours published, website linked. Re-checked 1 September. One change worth flagging: the rating now reads 4.0★, where the July audit recorded 5.0★ and re-confirmed it on 24 August. The count is not visible signed out, so we are not quoting one and we cannot tell you what caused the move. What it does show is that a profile carrying only three reviews has no buffer in it, and a single new review can swing the number a whole star in a week. That makes the July audit's recommendation to ask a decade of customers for reviews more urgent rather than less.
  • The main site is fast and technically clean. Apex redirects to www in a single hop, canonicals are in place, the viewport does not block pinch-to-zoom.
  • The educational writing is good. The /resources/ pages on why fiberglass, ductwork, manhole covers and flumes are real explanatory content. That is the asset the outside firm noticed, and they were right to.

03

Cause one — the catalog cannot be crawled and cannot be parsed

products.vpcfiberglass.com carries zero structured data and publishes no sitemap.

Confirmed 1 Sep
products.vpcfiberglass.com/ 200, 73,567 bytes application/ld+json blocks 0 products.vpcfiberglass.com/sitemap.xml 404 linked from every main-site page 41 to 52 times

This is the ThomasNet-hosted catalog, and it is where the actual products live: FRP fans, louvers and dampers, sound enclosures, ductwork, flumes, manhole systems, staff and tank gauges, troughs, weirs, baffles, ladders, platforms, railings, tanks, catch basins. The main site links into it more than forty times per page, so VPC is pouring its internal link equity into a subdomain that hands a search engine no machine-readable description of a single product, and no map of what exists.

This was the July audit's highest-leverage recommendation and it has not moved. It is also the one item we have never been able to price, because it depends on a question nobody has answered. See section 07.

04

Cause two — a hundred product pages and not one product marked as a product

There is no Product schema anywhere in the VPC estate.

Confirmed 1 Sep

We sampled the play catalog directly. /products/aquatic-play-structures/triwave-100/, /products/water-play-features/jet-stream/, /products/splash-pads/ and the /products/ hub all carry exactly one JSON-LD block, and it contains WebPage, BreadcrumbList and WebSite. Nothing that says this page is a product, who makes it, or what it is for.

So when somebody asks a search engine or an assistant for a commercial splash pad manufacturer, or an FRP equipment enclosure, the pages that should answer are structurally indistinguishable from an About page. That is the whole of the gap the outside firm described. VPC ranks for what fiberglass is because those pages are written like articles. It does not rank for what VPC sells because those pages are not written like products.

05

Cause three — nothing here is built to be quoted by an assistant

  • No FAQ markup anywhere, on any of the four properties. One page comes close: easyrecreation.com/ada-easy-stair/ has the words “frequently asked” on it and no FAQPage schema, no Question schema, nothing. Across roughly a hundred pages that is the entire stock of question-and-answer content.
  • No llms.txt on any of the three domains. All 404. We would not rush this one, and we say why in section 06.
  • The blog stopped four years ago. Five posts, the newest with a lastmod of 5 July 2022. The educational authority the outside firm praised is the residue of work that ended during the last administration.

An AI assistant answering “who makes fiberglass equipment shelters” needs a page that states, in plain sentences, that this company makes that thing, where, for whom, and to what spec. VPC has the knowledge for that in its /resources/ pages and its 15 case studies. It has not been written down in the shape a machine can lift.

06

Cause four — the two satellite sites do not identify their own company

Neither EasyRec nor InterActive Play tells a machine who owns it or where it is.

Confirmed 1 Sep
easyrecreation.com/ 0 JSON-LD blocks. 5 indexable pages. sitemap submits /author/admin/ and /author/aacker/ interactivewaterparks.com/ 1 block: WebPage, BreadcrumbList, WebSite no Organization, no address, no telephone title reads "Homepage - Interactive Play"

Both sites link back to vpcfiberglass.com once, so the relationship exists for a person who goes looking. It does not exist in the markup. Google and the assistants are shown three companies at three addresses with no stated connection, and one of them — EasyRec, five pages — is barely a website at all.

Two smaller things in the same place. EasyRec's sitemap publishes two author URLs, one of them the default admin, which is the same defect flagged on the main site in August and half of a login. And its heading structure is broken: the homepage emits four H1s, and /ada-easy-stair/ emits nine H1s and no H2 at all, which is a page builder setting rather than a writing problem.

07

Two questions block more work than anything on the list

Both have been asked before, on 14 August, to the whole VPC group. Neither has been answered. We are raising them once more because between them they decide whether the biggest item here is a settings change or a project.

  • Who controls products.vpcfiberglass.com? It is ThomasNet-hosted. If VPC can edit its templates, adding Product schema and a sitemap is a contained piece of work with a large return, and it is the single highest-value thing in this document. If VPC cannot, then the recommendation is not “add schema” — it becomes “build an owned catalog”, which is a different size of project and needs a budget conversation rather than a ticket.
  • Who owns the website decision? This is the real bottleneck and it is worth naming plainly. VPC has had findings since July, a specific fix list since 17 August, and an outside firm's assessment since 19 August. Three of six items then shipped with no reply to anyone. That tells us there are capable hands on the site, and no named person deciding what those hands work on. A second agency does not solve that. One name against the website does.

08

What we would do, in order

  1. Finish the three open items from the August fix guideAdd og:image and a Twitter card, turn off author archives so /author/tmswebsiteadminthomasnet-com/ stops resolving and leaves the sitemap, and write a meta description for /locations/. Whoever shipped the other three can do these in the same sitting. Do the same author fix on EasyRec, which is publishing /author/admin/.
  2. Give EasyRec and InterActive Play an identity in their markupOrganization or LocalBusiness on both, with the address, the phone number, and a parentOrganization pointing at Virtual Polymer Compounds. The main site already has a correct block to copy from since August. This is the cheapest way to make three websites read as one company. While in there, retitle Homepage - Interactive Play, and fix the nine H1s on the EasyRec stair page.
  3. Put Product schema on the play catalogTwenty-four product pages already have names, descriptions and images on the page. The markup is a template change in one place, not twenty-four edits. This is the first item that makes VPC answerable to a commercial question rather than an educational one, and it is on a domain VPC certainly controls, which is why it comes before the ThomasNet catalog.
  4. Resolve the catalog question, then act on itSection 07. If VPC can edit the ThomasNet templates, this is the largest return available anywhere in the estate. If it cannot, the honest answer is that the catalog is rented and the strategy has to change. Either way it stops being an open item.
  5. Write the questions down as questionsThe knowledge is already in the /resources/ pages and the 15 case studies. What is missing is the shape: a real FAQ block, marked up as one, on the four resource pages and the products hub. What is the difference between FRP and steel enclosures. What NEMA ratings can you meet. What lead time on a custom shelter. These are the sentences an assistant can quote, and nobody at VPC has to learn anything to write them.
  6. Restart the blog, or retire it honestlyFive posts and nothing since July 2022 reads as abandoned to a crawler and to a buyer. One case study a month, written up as a page with the client, the spec and the outcome, would do more than any of the technical items above over twelve months. It is also the only item on this list that needs a habit rather than a change.

On llms.txt, deliberately not on the list All three domains 404 it, and we would leave it that way for now. An llms.txt is a plain-text summary a site publishes for assistants to read, and it is only worth having once the site underneath it is consistent. Publishing one over an estate where the catalog has no product data and two domains do not name their own company would just state the confusion more confidently. Do items 01 through 05, then it is worth twenty minutes.

09

How we checked, and what we cannot see

  • Everything above was pulled live on 1 September 2026 against all four properties: status codes, redirect chains, robots files, every sitemap and child sitemap, the JSON-LD parsed out of the served pages rather than guessed at, heading counts, titles and meta descriptions, and the Google profile checked signed out.
  • We hold no Search Console or Analytics access for any of the four. So we can tell you what the sites say and what Google's public surfaces show. We cannot tell you what anybody searched, what impressions look like, or which pages are actually indexed. Read access would convert most of the reasoning here into measurement, and it is the cheapest thing on this page to grant.
  • We have not scored the three new properties. The July rubric was built for an industrial site selling to specifying engineers. InterActive Play sells to campground and resort owners making a capital purchase. Running one number across both would flatter the exercise and tell you nothing, so we have described each property's state instead. If a score is useful for comparison we will build a second rubric and say so.
  • The outside firm's assessment reached us through Corey rather than in writing. Where we say we agree with it, we are agreeing with the verdict as relayed. If there is a written version, send it and we will check their specific claims against live the way we checked ours.
  • The play side still rests on assumptions we have not been able to test. Twenty-eight questions went to the VPC group on 14 August about who the customers actually are, and no answers came back. Everything we believe about how a campground owner buys a splash pad is inference, and we have kept it out of this document on purpose.

10

The decision

Items 01, 02 and 05 are free, they live inside settings screens and text fields VPC already controls, and between them they close most of the gap the outside firm identified. Whoever shipped three fixes in late August can ship all three of these.

Item 03 is a template change on a domain VPC owns and is the first thing that makes the company answerable to a buying question. Item 04 is the one that needs a decision before it needs a budget.

None of that is blocked by choosing an agency. It is blocked by one person's name going against the website. That has been the constraint since July, it is the same constraint an outside firm will hit, and it is the only thing on this page that VPC cannot buy.
