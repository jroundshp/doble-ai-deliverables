VPC is four websites, and the two carrying the products are the two machines can read least

An outside firm told Corey that VPC is not findable and not searchable. We went and looked, across all four properties this time. They are right, and the mechanism is more specific than the verdict.

Every figure below was pulled on 1 September 2026 and re-checked live on 5 September 2026. Supersedes nothing. The July audit of vpcfiberglass.com stands, and this widens the frame to the three properties it never covered.

00

First, something that has not happened before on this account

Three of the six items from the 17 August fix guide have shipped, with the site's own timestamps putting the work between 25 and 28 August. It happened quietly, without any correspondence back to us, which is worth knowing in itself: the site is being actively maintained.

  • The structured-data bug is fixed. @context now reads https://schema.org without the www, and @type is correctly cased LocalBusiness. It carries the Medina address, the phone number and six authority links including D&B, BBB, ThomasNet and Bloomberg.
  • /locations/test-location/ is gone. It now returns 404.
  • The Enduro duplicates are gone as duplicates. Nothing self-canonicalises any more and only one Enduro page serves content: /vpc-case-studies/enduro-standard-equipment-enclosure-5/. The root-level -2, -3 and -4 copies now 404, and both bare URLs redirect to that surviving page. The redirects were mapped carelessly, though, and that is worth five minutes. See directly below.

That matters more than the three items themselves. It means there is somebody at or around VPC who reads these documents and acts on them, which is the thing we could not establish in August. It also means the argument that follows is not landing in an empty room.

01

We agree with the outside read

We are not going to spend this document defending our own. The outside verdict is correct, it matches what our July audit found, and an outsider saying it unprompted is worth more to VPC than us saying it again.

What we can add is the mechanism, because “not findable” is a symptom and there are four specific causes, all of them fixable, and two of them cheap.

The short version: VPC publishes roughly 100 indexable pages across four properties. The pages that describe the actual products are the least machine-readable things the company owns. Not the marketing pages. The products.

The thing that makes this harder than a normal site review VPC is not one kind of business, and findability does different work for each half. Some lines are catalog-shaped: Parshall flumes, manhole systems, standard Enduro enclosures, the EasyRec ADA stairs and stackable benches. A buyer already knows roughly what the part is and searches for it by name, so those pages have to exist, carry the product name, and be legible as products. The rest is engineer-to-order: shelters and buildings, ductwork, fans, custom weirs and baffles, and the InterActive Play attractions, where a consulting engineer arrives with a spec and a drawing and no part number exists yet. Those pages are not competing to match a search term, they are competing to prove capability, which means named projects and stated tolerances rather than a catalog entry. VPC currently under-serves both, but it under-serves them in different ways, and a single fix list that ignores the split will over-invest in one half.

Why the flagship looks thin on plant FRP Traditional's catalog depth is not on the flagship. It sits on ThomasNet-hosted products.vpcfiberglass.com, which is why 23 pages is the whole of what a crawler finds for plant and municipal FRP on the domain VPC owns. The products exist and the descriptions exist. They are parked somewhere a machine cannot read them, on a property whose control is still an open question. ThomasNet is a real industrial discovery and RFQ channel and it should stay one, but it is a channel, not a substitute for a product story on a domain VPC controls.

What is genuinely good, so the list below is not read as a demolition

  • The divisions page does cross-linking properly. It links all five product lines, and the anchor text carries the brand names: “EasyRec”, “InterActivePlay”, “View CFT Product Line”. This is a thing most sites get wrong and VPC gets right.
  • The Medina Google profile is complete and correct. Categorised Fiberglass supplier, 10478 Ridge Rd Medina NY, (585) 735-9668, hours published, website linked. Re-checked 1 September. That is the one location represented properly, and the other two are covered below rather than here.
  • The main site is fast and technically clean. Apex redirects to www in a single hop, canonicals are in place, the viewport does not block pinch-to-zoom.
  • The educational writing is good. The /resources/ pages on why fiberglass, ductwork, manhole covers and flumes are real explanatory content. That is the asset the outside firm noticed, and they were right to.

We are saying it did not surface rather than that it does not exist, because a profile can be present and too weak to return. Either way, a third facility that a buyer cannot find in Maps is not findable in the most literal sense this document uses the word.

And the Medina rating has moved. It read 4.0★ from 4 reviews on 1 September, where the July audit recorded 5.0★ from 3 and re-confirmed it on 24 August. For a fourth review to pull a 5.0 average down to exactly 4.0, it has to be a one-star. We have not read it and we are not going to speculate about what it says. On 5 September the 4.0 rating re-confirmed, and the review count was not visible: signed out, Google serves a limited view that suppresses the count, so we are reporting the rating we can see and the count as of 1 September rather than restating a number we could not re-measure. The structural point is the one the July audit already made: a business with three or four reviews has no buffer, one unhappy customer moves the public number a full star, and a decade of satisfied municipal and industrial customers have never been asked.

The fix is one canonical phone story, published once and repeated everywhere. Decide which number reaches which plant, then make the website, the Medina profile and the Lockport profile all say that. If the 888 number is a live routing line, put it on /locations/ so Maps and the site corroborate each other. If it is not, replace it on the Lockport profile with the number that plant actually answers. Then give Amherst a profile of its own, or stop listing it as a facility a buyer can go to. Three plants told three different ways is the version a machine cannot resolve, and this is a form field rather than a project.

03

Cause one: the catalog cannot be crawled and cannot be parsed

products.vpcfiberglass.com carries zero structured data and publishes no sitemap.

Re-confirmed 5 Sep

This is the ThomasNet-hosted catalog, and it is where the actual products live: FRP fans, louvers and dampers, sound enclosures, ductwork, flumes, manhole systems, staff and tank gauges, troughs, weirs, baffles, ladders, platforms, railings, tanks, catch basins. Every one of the 23 pages on the main site links into it at least 37 times, rising to 46 from the homepage and 50 each from the industries page and the ductwork resource. So VPC is pouring its internal link equity into a subdomain that hands a search engine no machine-readable description of a single product, and no map of what exists.

This was the July audit's highest-leverage recommendation and it has not moved. Re-checked 5 September: still 200, still 73,567 bytes, still zero JSON-LD, sitemap still 404. It is also the one item we have never been able to price, because it depends on a question nobody has answered.

So name the fork rather than carrying it another month. The recommendation actually changes depending on one answer, and the two branches are not the same size of work:

  • If VPC can edit the ThomasNet templates Product schema and a sitemap on the existing catalog This is the highest-leverage item anywhere in the estate and it is contained. The product names, descriptions and images are already written and already on the page. Marking them up is a template change in one place, and publishing a sitemap is a setting. Nothing has to be migrated and nothing has to be rewritten.
  • If VPC cannot edit them Move the product truth onto a domain VPC controls Then the honest framing is that the catalog is rented, and no amount of schema advice reaches it. The work becomes building product pages for the traditional lines on vpcfiberglass.com itself, starting with the catalog-shaped ones a buyer searches by name, and letting ThomasNet keep doing what it is good at, which is industrial discovery and RFQ. That is a different size of project and it needs a budget conversation rather than a ticket.

The meeting question: who controls products.vpcfiberglass.com, and can anyone at VPC edit its templates? That single answer decides which of the two branches above is the plan. It has been open since 14 August.

04

Cause two: a hundred product pages and not one product marked as a product

There is no Product schema anywhere in the VPC estate.

Re-confirmed 5 Sep

We sampled the play catalog directly. /products/aquatic-play-structures/triwave-100/, /products/water-play-features/jet-stream/, /products/splash-pads/ and the /products/ hub all carry exactly one JSON-LD block, and it contains WebPage, BreadcrumbList and WebSite. Nothing that says this page is a product, who makes it, or what it is for.

So when somebody asks a search engine or an assistant for a commercial splash pad manufacturer, or an FRP equipment enclosure, the pages that should answer are structurally indistinguishable from an About page. That is the whole of the gap the outside firm described. VPC ranks for what fiberglass is because those pages are written like articles. It does not rank for what VPC sells because those pages are not written like products.

The markup is only half of it, and the cheaper half. A product page also has to say the obvious things in plain sentences, because those are the sentences an assistant lifts and a buyer scans for. Who makes this, and under which company. Who it is for, in their own words, a municipal wastewater plant or a campground operator rather than “our clients”. And which kind of purchase it is: whether the buyer is configuring something that already exists in sizes and options, or commissioning something built to their drawing. That last distinction is the one the estate never states anywhere, and it is the one that tells a buyer whether to expect a price or a conversation. We are not proposing that anyone publish lead times or tolerances we have not been given. State the shape of the transaction, which VPC already knows, and leave the numbers to the people who own them.

05

Cause three: nothing here is built to be quoted by an assistant

  • No FAQ markup anywhere, on any of the four properties. One page comes close: easyrecreation.com/ada-easy-stair/ has the words “frequently asked” on it and no FAQPage schema, no Question schema, nothing. Across roughly a hundred pages that is the entire stock of question-and-answer content.
  • No llms.txt on any of the three domains. All 404. We would not rush this one, and we say why in section 06.
  • The blog stopped four years ago. Five posts, the newest with a lastmod of 5 July 2022. The educational authority the outside firm praised is the residue of work that ended during the last administration.

An AI assistant answering “who makes fiberglass equipment shelters” needs a page that states, in plain sentences, that this company makes that thing, where, for whom, and to what spec. VPC has the knowledge for that in its /resources/ pages and its 15 case studies. It has not been written down in the shape a machine can lift.

07

Two questions block more work than anything on the list

Both have been asked before, on 14 August, to the whole VPC group. Neither has been answered. We are raising them once more because between them they decide whether the biggest item here is a settings change or a project.

  • Who controls products.vpcfiberglass.com? Section 03 sets out the two branches this answer selects between, and they are not the same size of work: a contained template change on the catalog as it stands, or moving the product truth onto a domain VPC controls. Both are legitimate and we are not arguing for either one sight unseen. What we cannot do is price it, sequence it, or recommend it while nobody has said which world we are in. This is the single highest-value unanswered question in the document.
  • Who owns the website decision? This is the real bottleneck and it is worth naming plainly. VPC has had findings since July, a specific fix list since 17 August, and an outside firm's assessment since 19 August. Three of six items have since shipped, so there are capable hands on the site and they are quick. What has not happened is an answer on who decides what those hands work on next. A second agency does not solve that. One name against the website does.

08

Website tune-up sequence, not a rebuild

Our recommendation is to tune this estate up rather than rebuild it, unless VPC names the brand or the look as the actual blocker. The main site is fast, correctly configured and well written, and somebody at VPC is demonstrably able to ship changes to it. Nothing found across four properties is a construction problem. It is missing structure, missing product pages and an unresolved catalog. A rebuild would spend the budget on the one layer that is already working and would restart the clock on everything below. If brand or look is the real complaint, that is a legitimate reason to rebuild and it changes this recommendation, but it is a different conversation and nobody has said it yet.

  1. Finish the three open items from the August fix guideAdd og:image and a Twitter card, turn off author archives so /author/tmswebsiteadminthomasnet-com/ stops resolving and leaves the sitemap, and write a meta description for /locations/. Whoever shipped the other three can do these in the same sitting. Do the same author fix on EasyRec, which is publishing /author/admin/.
  2. Give EasyRec and InterActive Play an identity in their markupOrganization or LocalBusiness on both, with the address, the phone number, and a parentOrganization pointing at Virtual Polymer Compounds. The main site already has a correct block to copy from since August. This is the cheapest way to make three websites read as one company. While in there, retitle Homepage - Interactive Play, and fix the nine H1s on the EasyRec stair page.
  3. Put Product schema on the play catalogTwenty-four product pages already have names, descriptions and images on the page. The markup is a template change in one place, not twenty-four edits. This is the first item that makes VPC answerable to a commercial question rather than an educational one, and it is on a domain VPC certainly controls, which is why it comes before the ThomasNet catalog. It is also the first Product markup VPC would own anywhere in the estate, so it doubles as the template for the traditional lines later. While the template is open, make each page state in plain sentences who makes it and under which company, who it is for, and whether the buyer is configuring something that exists in sizes and options or commissioning something built to a drawing. No lead times, no tolerances, nothing we have not been given: just the shape of the transaction, which VPC already knows.
  4. Resolve the catalog question, then act on itSection 07. If VPC can edit the ThomasNet templates, this is the largest return available anywhere in the estate. If it cannot, the honest answer is that the catalog is rented and the strategy has to change. Either way it stops being an open item.
  5. Write the questions down as questionsThe knowledge is already in the /resources/ pages and the 15 case studies. What is missing is the shape: a real FAQ block, marked up as one, on the four resource pages and the products hub. What is the difference between FRP and steel enclosures. What NEMA ratings can you meet. What lead time on a custom shelter. These are the sentences an assistant can quote, and nobody at VPC has to learn anything to write them.
  6. Two doors in navigation and IASplit the estate the way the buyers already split: plant and municipal FRP on one side, EasyRec and InterActive Play on the other. Then give each door product-named hubs a person would actually type, Parshall flume, scum baffle, FRP shelter, splash pad, ADA stair, so that traffic arriving from ThomasNet, from Google or from an assistant lands in the right hallway instead of on a homepage that has to serve a wastewater engineer and a campground owner with the same sentence. This is the item that turns the two previous ones into traffic, because schema makes a page eligible and a product-named hub is what there is to be eligible for.
  7. Restart the blog, or retire it honestlyFive posts and nothing since July 2022 reads as abandoned to a crawler and to a buyer. One case study a month, written up as a page with the client, the spec and the outcome, would do more than any of the technical items above over twelve months. It is also the only item on this list that needs a habit rather than a change.

On llms.txt, deliberately not on the list All three domains 404 it, and we would leave it that way for now. An llms.txt is a plain-text summary a site publishes for assistants to read, and it is only worth having once the site underneath it is consistent. Publishing one over an estate where the catalog has no product data and two domains do not name their own company would just state the confusion more confidently. Do items 01 through 05, then it is worth twenty minutes.

How we checked, and what we cannot see

  • Everything above was pulled live on 1 September 2026 and re-checked on 5 September 2026 against all four properties: status codes, redirect chains, robots files, every sitemap and child sitemap, the JSON-LD parsed out of the served pages rather than guessed at, heading counts, titles and meta descriptions, and the Google profiles rendered and read signed out. On the second pass every technical finding in this document still held, and nothing had been fixed in between. One figure was corrected: the homepage carries 46 links into the catalog subdomain rather than the 50 first reported, which does not change the point it supports.
  • We hold no Search Console or Analytics access for any of the four. So we can tell you what the sites say and what Google's public surfaces show. We cannot tell you what anybody searched, what impressions look like, or which pages are actually indexed. Read access would convert most of the reasoning here into measurement, and it is the cheapest thing on this page to grant.
  • We have not scored the three new properties. The July rubric was built for an industrial site selling to specifying engineers. InterActive Play sells to campground and resort owners making a capital purchase. Running one number across both would flatter the exercise and tell you nothing, so we have described each property's state instead. If a score is useful for comparison we will build a second rubric and say so.
  • The outside firm's assessment reached us through Corey rather than in writing. Where we say we agree with it, we are agreeing with the verdict as relayed. If there is a written version, send it and we will check their specific claims against live the way we checked ours.
  • The play side still rests on assumptions we have not been able to test. Twenty-eight questions went to the VPC group on 14 August about who the customers actually are, and no answers came back. Everything we believe about how a campground owner buys a splash pad is inference, and we have kept it out of this document on purpose.

10

The decision

Start with the free half, because it needs no budget and no vendor. Items 01, 02 and 05 live inside settings screens and text fields VPC already controls: the three unfinished August items, an identity block on the two satellite sites, and the questions written down as questions. Between them they close most of the gap the outside firm identified. Whoever shipped three fixes in late August can ship all three of these, and none of it waits on anything decided in this meeting.

Then item 03, the Product schema on the play catalog. It is a template change on a domain VPC certainly owns, it is the first Product markup the company would have anywhere, and it is the first thing that makes VPC answerable to a buying question rather than an educational one. It also produces the template the traditional lines reuse later, so it is worth doing first even though the play side is not where the revenue concentration is.

Item 04, the catalog, is a decision before it is a budget. Nobody can price it until somebody says whether VPC can edit the ThomasNet templates on products.vpcfiberglass.com. One answer makes it a contained template change with the largest return in the estate. The other makes it a project to move the product truth onto a domain VPC controls. Both are legitimate. Neither can start while the question is open, and it has been open since 14 August.

Our recommendation on the website itself is a tune-up, not a rebuild, on the reasoning in section 08. If brand or look is the real blocker, say so and that changes, but the findings here are structure, not construction.

What is actually blocking all of it is that no single name sits against the website. That has been the constraint since July. It is the same constraint any outside firm will hit, and it is the only thing on this page that cannot be bought or built. One name, with the authority to say what the capable hands work on next, unblocks every item above.

One optional ask, only if VPC wants the next round measured rather than argued: Search Console read access on the four properties. It takes a minute to grant, and it converts most of the reasoning in this document into numbers.
