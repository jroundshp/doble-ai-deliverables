05 Priority Recommendations
01 Claim & Build a Google Business Profile (Highest Priority)
This is the single biggest lever and it's free. Create and verify a Google Business Profile with the real yard address, hours, service area, product categories, and photos of the yard and trucks. It's what puts Patriot Stone on Maps, in the local "3-pack," and in "gravel near me" results.

But the local-search win is only half of it, and the smaller half. A verified profile is the record every other signal resolves back to. The LocalBusiness schema in recommendation 04 asserts who and where this business is; the Google profile is what corroborates it. Directory listings, Apple and Bing Maps, and the AI assistants all check that the claims agree. When they do, the business becomes one confident entity. When there is no verified profile, there is nothing to agree with, every source is a separate unverified claim, and the engine falls back on the strongest match to the name — which today means Patriot Mining and Materials in Denton, or one of the unrelated Patriot Stone companies in Georgia and Florida.

Reviews compound it further, because they live on the profile and they are among the signals AI engines weigh most heavily when recommending a local supplier. So the sequence matters: verify the profile first, then add the schema, then gather reviews. Done in that order each step reinforces the last. Done out of order, the schema has nothing to point at. Nothing else in this list matters as much.
02 Publish the Address & a Consistent NAP Everywhere
Put the yard's Name, Address, and Phone on the website (header, footer, and a real Contact page with an embedded map) and make it match exactly across Google, Apple Maps, Bing, and Yelp. Decide whether to move to a local Denton (940/469) number or clearly present the current one as the business line — the out-of-state area code muddies the local signal. Consistent NAP is the bedrock of local ranking.
03 Fix the SEO Foundation (Titles, Descriptions, H1s)
Replace the duplicated title with a real, location-keyword title on every page ("Crushed Stone, Gravel & Aggregate Delivery in Denton, TX | Patriot Stone Supply"), add unique meta descriptions and Open Graph tags, and give each page a specific H1. This is a couple of hours of work with an SEO plugin and it's the difference between being readable by search and being skipped.
04 Add LocalBusiness & Product Schema
Add LocalBusiness (or Store) JSON-LD with the address, hours, geo-coordinates, and service area, plus Product schema for the material types. This is how Google and AI tools read the business as structured data — and it directly helps disambiguate Patriot Stone from the unrelated "Patriot Stone" companies in other states that currently muddy its search results.
05 Turn On a Review Engine
Start from zero deliberately: text a Google review link to every satisfied customer, add a QR code to invoices and the counter, and enable recommendations on Facebook. Even 10–15 genuine reviews would leapfrog the current "no social proof at all" position and feed both Maps ranking and AI citations. For a supplier that lives on reliability, reviews are the trust currency.
06 Publish Materials, Delivery-Area & "We Buy Concrete" Content + FAQ (GEO)
Start from an asset that already exists. The homepage already publishes per-ton prices on nine materials, which almost nobody in this industry does — and it wastes them by stacking all nine on one page. Give each material its own indexable page carrying that price plus coverage, uses and typical load size, and build out the concrete-recycling service as its own real page; it's the least-contested, most ownable message in the market. Extend the existing We Buy Concrete FAQ to the material side (delivery zips, minimum loads, coverage per yard) and add FAQPage schema to the answers already written, which is a markup job rather than a writing one. Published, specific numbers are exactly what AI engines quote. (Revised August 13, 2026: the original asked them to add "pricing guidance" without noting the pricing was already there.)
07 Fix Mobile Zoom & Add Alt Text
Remove maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=0 from the viewport so contractors on phones can pinch-to-zoom — an accessibility requirement and a usability win for a mobile-first, on-the-jobsite audience. Add descriptive alt text to the product and yard photos. Fast, cheap, and good for both users and search. (Worth fixing across all of the owner's sites, since they share this flaw.)
08 Point Instagram at the Funnel + Add an AI Quote Concierge
Put the website and a "call/text for a quote" link in the Instagram bio, tag the Denton location on every reel, and cross-post to Facebook so the one active channel actually drives leads. Then add an AI chat concierge to the site that answers material, coverage, delivery-area, and "we buy concrete" questions and captures quote requests 24/7 — the exact after-hours questions a phone-only yard misses. This is Doble AI's signature layer.
09 Run the AI Test Yourself — Ten Minutes
Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, Grok and a plain Google search. Set each one up properly, because "use a private window" is only correct for two of the four. For Google and Perplexity, an incognito window is the right control, though on Google it will not change your location, which it reads from your internet connection. ChatGPT needs Temporary Chat instead: its Memory is attached to your account rather than your browser and is switched on by default, so a private window does not touch it. Grok requires a sign-in, so incognito only gives you a login screen; sign in and start a brand new conversation rather than continuing an existing thread. If you can, have a few different people run the same set, ideally five, since these systems are not deterministic and one person running it once is a sample of one. Never search your own company name until the last prompt: searching the name only proves the name exists, while the earlier prompts test whether anyone who does not already know you can find you. Then type what a customer would type: "gravel delivery near Denton Texas" · "where to buy crushed stone in Denton County" · "concrete recycling drop off Denton TX" · "landscape rock suppliers north of Dallas" · "what do you know about Patriot Stone Supply in Denton Texas". Note who gets named and whether you appear at all. That last prompt is the revealing one. Everything in this audit is an explanation of what you will see.
10 Verify the Domain With Bing
Bing is the second index that has to stop confusing this company with Patriot Mining and Materials, and at the moment nobody here can see into it. patriotstonesupply.com has no msvalidate.01 tag and /BingSiteAuth.xml returns a 404, so the site is not verified in Bing Webmaster Tools. That matters more than it used to, because ChatGPT's search runs on Bing's index, not Google's, and the name-collision work above has to land in both. One meta tag, free, five minutes, then submit https://patriotstonesupply.com/wp-sitemap.xml.
