Where this lands

This is an option, not an asset. The contract guarantees no volume at all, and TDS’s own contract history shows exactly what that is worth: the same agency, the same “up to 1,500 travel trailers,” awarded twice, three years apart, at figures 91 times apart.

What was actually awarded Doc

NC Emergency Management ran a sealed-bid Invitation for Bid to establish a standing disaster contract for travel trailers and recreational vehicles used as temporary housing for disaster survivors. Award went to the lowest responsive price. TDS won it, and the term runs to 19 March 2028.

The contract carries no floor. Section 1.0 and Section 4.2 both say it plainly: “No maximum or minimum quantities are guaranteed.” Quantities in the solicitation are annual estimates for information only. Revenue appears when NC Emergency Management issues a Task Work Order and not before, and §5.7 says the Division does not intend to “lease/purchase” all units at once. That wording is worth holding onto: the agency itself left both structures on the table.

So the award is a position on a list, priced and pre-qualified, waiting on weather.

I tried to break this figure before leaning on it, because the package it comes from has demonstrable number problems elsewhere: career volume appears as both “almost 12,500” and “over 11,400” units, and one quantity reads “144-2,800.” A single mistyped zero would destroy the comparison. It survived. The Navy RFI response, written twenty months later for an entirely different customer, carries the same two entries with the same two figures, the same solicitation numbers and the same procurement contact.

That is consistency, not independent confirmation, since both documents have the same author. Open North Carolina’s own procurement records would settle it outright, and that is a cheap check for whoever has an hour.

For the upper bound, the same history lists a Florida Division of Emergency Management turnkey housing contract at base estimates of $131.6M, $138.2M and $145.1M across three years, renewable and open to piggyback award by four additional southeastern states. That is what an activated version of this kind of contract looks like.

Two structures are in the same folder Doc

The awarded contract is a purchase. Section 5.6 requires TDS to title every trailer to the State of North Carolina and mail the original Certificate of Title to Raleigh. The vendor retains no asset and no residual.

The lease proposal in the same package is the opposite structure. TDS and RV Wholesalers offer monthly rentals on three unit types, with the lessor keeping the fleet, backed by an unnamed party the document calls only “a Government Leasing Company.”

Purchase and lease are mutually exclusive economics, and the award letter references only the purchase solicitation. Which one is live is the first thing to settle, because the lease rate card has no bearing on the awarded contract.

This is a field-services contract wearing a trailer contract’s clothes Doc

Sections 5.4 through 5.8 put far more than delivery on the vendor. Before a unit lands: a site visit to assess power, water and sewer, and any repairs those need. At placement: leveling and blocking, six tie-downs to ground, helical anchors, a 50-amp panel with power pole and meter loop, potable water with freeze protection, and a rigid three-inch PVC sewer line with cleanouts, sloped to code. Transportation permits across federal, state, tribal and local jurisdictions are the contractor’s to obtain.

Then it stays. The vendor owns preventative, routine, major and emergency maintenance for the life of the deployment: plumbing, electrical, HVAC, appliances, windows and egress, steps and ramps, re-leveling, propane refills, smoke and carbon monoxide detectors, and monthly pest control. Written status reports go to the State weekly. Freeze protection for external components is called out as the contractor’s problem, with NC Emergency Management free to reject any proposed method until it approves one, in mountain counties that hold below freezing for long stretches.

The margin question is therefore not the spread on a trailer. It is the cost of standing crews up in western North Carolina and keeping them there for as long as a Task Work Order leaves a unit on the ground. The eighteen-to-sixty-month range quoted around this deal comes from the lease proposal, not the award; the award is a 36-month purchase vehicle, and each unit’s maintenance tail is set by its own work order.

The 48-hour commitment Doc

Most dangerous number in the package

The lease proposal’s Quality Assurance Surveillance Plan commits to units deployed within 48 hours of a state directive at a 95% acceptable quality level, utilities connected within 24 hours at 98%, and emergency repairs completed within 6 hours at 100%.

Three statements in the same set of documents contradict it. The proposal’s own background section says units reach the staging area within 15 days. The capability package says production commences within 72 hours with deliveries arriving within 15 days. And the staging area named for both intake and end-of-deployment return is Hudson, Florida, roughly 600 road miles from the western North Carolina counties this was written for.

A 48-hour clock measured against a 15-day supply chain and an out-of-state yard is a written performance standard nobody has costed. If any version of this QASP survives into a signed document, it is the clause that creates liability.

Who actually delivers Vendor

TDS is Charles “Mike” Ferrall. The named team runs to about seven people, one of whom shares his surname, and every capability beyond coordination belongs to a partner: RV Wholesalers for units and transport, S&M and Associates for setup and installation, an unnamed leasing company for capital. Forest River is named as the production source at 100 units per day, which is a claim about a supplier relationship, not a contract in this set.

The partner bench is real. S&M and Associates carries two decades of federal construction work and USACE Blue Roof past-performance letters that read genuinely well, including a survey form scoring them at the top of the scale with “would award another contract.” That is the strongest third-party evidence in the package.

Ferrall’s own bio puts him in the Navy at 17, out after 22.5 years, running TDS for 28 more, married 51 years. Whatever the exact figure, the single point of failure in this contract is also the person carrying the Navy RFI response described below.

Two more mismatches sit in the same file. And David Durnell appears as RV Wholesalers on the cover of the lease proposal and signs its contact block as president of RVing Solutions LLC. Which entity carries the obligation is worth knowing before it matters.

The Navy question, which is capacity and not a second contract Doc

The same principal is also pitching the Navy, and it is important not to read that as a funded job. TDS has prepared a response to RFIUH-2026, a NAVFAC Request for Information on unaccompanied housing, in a letter dated 23 August 2026 and a completed response workbook. Nothing in the files confirms submission or receipt, only that the documents exist and carry that date. The offer is 2,000 berthing units plus 500 MWR units on a turnkey lease. An RFI is market research at the earliest stage of federal procurement. There is no award in that packet, no contract number and no period of performance.

The response workbook is explicit about where TDS stands. Units currently available: zero. All 2,500 units sit in the to-be-built column. Every ROM cost cell is left empty, with monthly cost per bed answered as “Varies.” The 1 October 2026 date that has been circulating is the workbook’s Earliest Ready for Occupancy field, which is TDS’s own estimate of when it could first house someone, not a start date and not a contract term.

So the collision is conditional rather than scheduled. If that RFI becomes a solicitation and TDS wins it, and North Carolina activates in the same season, both draw on one principal, one installer bench and one set of trucks. That is a capacity exposure to hold open in the portfolio comparison, not two funded jobs to sequence.

Presentation problems worth an hour of somebody’s time Vendor

The information package went to the Director of NC Emergency Management and still says the trailers are “immediately available to the State of Florida,” thanks the reader for the opportunity to support “the FEMA response for the Florida Housing Mission DR-70FBR425Q00000037,” and misspells North Carolina on the first page of the lease proposal.

  • Sections 2 and 4 of the lease proposal repeat four subsections verbatim, and the table of contents swaps the conclusion and the executive summary against the body.
  • The setup partner is “S&W Associates” in his own bio line and S&M and Associates everywhere else; the principal is Darren in one place and Darron in the USACE letters.
  • The president’s email address is printed with a comma in place of the period, and his office number with an extra digit.
  • Career volume is stated as “almost 12,500 THU units” on page one and “over 11,400” in the bio four pages later.
  • A quantity appears as “at least 144-2,800 travel and destination trailers.”

None of this moves the economics. All of it is what a procurement officer sees first, and on a lowest-price award the only thing standing between TDS and a responsiveness challenge is the care in the file.

What this brief has not seen Open

Stated plainly so the group can weigh it, and so anyone who knows better can correct us.

  • No bid prices. Attachment A is in the set with all four line items blank. Everything about margin is therefore unanswerable, not estimated.
  • No executed contract. We have the IFB as issued and a one-page award letter. The signed agreement is not here.
  • The binding terms are links, not documents. The IFB incorporates the Instructions to Vendors and the North Carolina General Terms and Conditions by reference to two URLs. Termination, liability, remedies and the detail behind the insurance tier all live in documents nobody in this analysis has opened.
  • No task work orders, invoices or delivery records. Nothing here shows whether a single unit has been ordered since March 2025.
  • No teaming or joint venture agreement. How RV Wholesalers and S&M and Associates are bound to a contract awarded to TDS alone is not visible.
  • No financials. §4.12 requires a signed Certification of Financial Condition as a condition of award. It is not in this set, and neither is anything else about TDS’s balance sheet.
  • The contract history is unaudited. Every prior contract value on this page is TDS’s own account of its own past. None of it has been checked against a state or federal procurement record.

Three questions, in order

  1. What did TDS bid on Attachment A?

    Four line items: standard and short length, each in stick-and-tin and laminate. Without them there is no margin analysis and no valuation, only narrative. This is the one missing document that changes the answer.

  2. How many Task Work Orders has NC Emergency Management issued since 20 March 2025, and for how many units?

    Seventeen months in. If the answer is none, this is a pre-qualification worth holding and nothing more. If units are in the field, the maintenance tail is already running and the real question becomes crew cost.

  3. Is the lease model live, or was it declined in favor of the purchase award?

    The award letter cites only the purchase solicitation. If the lease was passed over, the rate card and the QASP are dead paper and should stop circulating with the package.

Source and method
Read from four documents: the 32-page IFB, the 21 March 2025 award letter, the North Carolina Information Package dated 9 December 2024, and the Temporary Housing Lease Proposal. The two copies of the lease proposal in the folder were diffed and are identical. The Navy section rests on two further documents outside this folder: TDS’s 23 August 2026 response to RFIUH-2026 and the RFI response workbook filed with it, both read directly rather than summarised. Section references are to the IFB as issued, and every quoted requirement was re-read at its source line before publishing. Every dollar figure is quoted except the lease term totals, which are marked as ours. The headline pair was adversarially checked against a second, later document by the same author before it was given a section. Where the documents disagree with themselves, the disagreement is shown rather than resolved.
