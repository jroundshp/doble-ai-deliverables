What we found first

All 24 product pages already carry the “Got Questions? We’ve Got Answers!” panel, and every one of them holds exactly three questions with full answers. We checked all 24 individually rather than sampling.

They are the same three. Not similar, identical, word for word, on every page:

QuestionAppears on
How will installing a multi-level play structure benefit our facility?24 pages
What maintenance is required for a multi-level play structure?24 pages
Can the multi-level play structure be customized to fit our facility’s theme or branding?24 pages

This is why we did not simply mark up what was there. Google’s FAQ guidance excludes question and answer content duplicated across many pages. Publishing the same three pairs 24 times is the one version of this job that reliably earns nothing.

There is also a copy issue underneath it. Fifteen of those pages are single water play features, not multi-level play structures. The Cannon, the Rain Maker, the Wee Womper and twelve others are currently answering questions about a product they are not.

What we suggest instead

The three questions you have are good ones, and they are true of the range as a whole rather than of any single model. They belong on the Aquatic Play Structures category page, marked up once, where nothing competes with them. That page carries no FAQ panel today.

The nine structure pages then get questions only they can answer, drawn from the specification table already on each one. A buyer sizing a deck asks about footprint and capacity, and those numbers are different on every page, so the markup stops being duplicate content and starts being the thing an assistant can actually quote.

  • Every answer below uses figures already published on that product’s page. Nothing new is claimed.
  • Google requires the questions to be visible to a reader on the same page, so these go into the existing accordion as well as into the markup.
  • Reword anything that does not sound like InterActive Play before it goes up.

How much space does the TriWave Model 100 need?

The TriWave Model 100 requires a minimum footprint of 4,300 sq ft / 399.4 sq m. The unit itself measures 60’ x 45’ x 16’ 7” (L/W/H), with a top deck height of 108 in / 2.74 m.

How many guests can the TriWave Model 100 serve?

Up to 172 water players. The structure carries 20 interactive water features and 2 slides – closed flume tube slides (8 ft and 9 ft high).

What kind of pool does the TriWave Model 100 install over?

Shallow depth pool 6-8 inches deep. All models can be customized with your selection of water play toys and designed to a footprint and scale that fits your facility.
