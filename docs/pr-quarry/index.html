The Project

What is actually being bought

One family — Santiago R. Varela Rosario and his wife, married under Puerto Rico's community-property regime — owns a vertically integrated chain that runs from rock in the ground to concrete in a truck. The deal is to buy the whole chain, plus the land under it.

Opportunity

The case, and what it rests on

The thesis has three legs: a funded demand cycle, a supply position that is hard to replicate, and a business currently running at a fraction of what its own permit allows.

  • PRIOR Demand is named and funded, not speculative. Western PR has an estimated $7–10B of infrastructure investment identified for 2026–2030 — the $20B LUMA grid rebuild, $4.566B in CDBG-MIT mitigation, the $1.298B City-Rev municipal program, PR-10 expansion at $540–552M, PR-2 freeway conversion, $142M+ at Aguadilla airport and Ramey, and roughly 1 GW of solar. The concrete and aggregates slice is put at $300–500M over five years.
  • PRIOR Supply is genuinely thin. CEMEX is the island's only cement producer. The named western aggregate quarries amount to Productora de Agregados, Cantera Mundo Real, and Rodriguez Aggregates. New quarry permits in karst are slow and contested — so the existing Arenales and Cotto permits are the moat, not the equipment.
  • OURS Is the quarry really running at a fifth of its permit? The permit allows 442,000 m³/yr, ~600,000 tons DOC. At the $14/ton the models assume, full permitted output is roughly $8.4M of aggregate revenue; EMR actually booked $1.6M in 2024 DOC. That arithmetic gives ~20% — but the $14/ton is a model assumption, not a verified market price, and at $10/ton the same sum gives ~27%. The question for the group: what does aggregate actually sell for at the gate in western PR, and how much of that gap is demand versus trucks, crew and working capital? The upside case rests entirely on this answer.
  • PRIOR An asphalt plant is the swing factor. A 1,700 t/day hot-mix plant sized to match the quarry's permitted output would be fed internally (asphalt is ~95% aggregate by weight). It requires a separate permit that has not been granted, and it is the single largest driver in every projection.

The $7.5M question DOC

Counsel's memo calls this "the single largest open economic term." The parties' 3 August summary is ambiguous about whether the bank and SBA debt sits inside the $15M — seller nets roughly $7.5M — or on top of it, making enterprise value roughly $22.5M. Those are two completely different deals and neither side has signed a term sheet resolving it.

The debt figure is itself unreconciled: the drafts total roughly $4.5M (FirstBank + SBA + LOC), counsel's memo cites ~$6.3M of bank/SBA debt, and the August 3 summary's "$7.5 million in bank notes" appears to describe total liabilities rather than bank debt. All three numbers are in circulation. None is a payoff letter.

One open item we can close now DOC

Counsel lists the identity of Chaparro's creditor as unresolved — Banco Popular on the 2020–2022 notes, FirstBank on the 2023–2024 notes. The 2024 appraisal in the set is a full appraisal report commissioned by FirstBank's Credit Lending Administration (effective 25 April 2024, addressed to their appraisal review manager). That is a genuine independent third-party document and it puts FirstBank in the lender seat as of April 2024. It does not replace a payoff letter, but it narrows the question.

What the earnings actually support DOC

Combined 2024 net earnings were $201,739. The JV's own operating agreement concedes in writing that debt service on a $14M note plus assumed bank debt is "substantial relative to the Company's equity capitalization of $1,000,000," and that capital calls are "reasonably likely to occur, potentially within the first twelve months following the Closing." Whoever funds the $1M should assume it is not the last $1M.

Five-year history: revenue $3.42M (2020) → $4.61M (2021) → $3.76M (2022) → $3.77M (2023) → $5.75M (2024). Net swung from +$180K in 2021 to −$192K in 2023 before recovering to +$202K. Gross margin is healthy at ~48%; the 3.5% net margin is interest and G&A, not production cost. EMR alone carried ~$275K of interest in 2024 and sits on negative equity of −$978K.

Blockers

The seven that would stop me signing

  • The permit expires before the note matures. The Arenales extraction permit was approved 27 December 2023 for five years — through 2028, at 442,000 m³/yr, mechanical only. The seller note runs five years from closing, to roughly 2031. There is a three-year gap with no confirmed renewal path. Its companion permit (PGO 2017-159059-PGO-004749) has already expired, and EMR's Permiso Único was reported expiring 11 November 2025 with no renewal in the file.
  • Foreclosure on Arenales would forfeit the business. Permits attach to and follow ownership of the parcels. If the seller takes a mortgage over Arenales and later forecloses, the extraction permit goes with it — and with it the aggregate supply for both batch plants and the proposed asphalt plant. The drafts flag excluding Arenales from the collateral as the highest-priority negotiating term. Not yet agreed.
  • Undisclosed pre-closing tax exposure. Varela's own 19 December 2024 letter describes cash-inclusive monthly sales materially above what the books show. In a share purchase the buyer inherits every dollar of pre-closing tax liability. EMR paid $987 of income tax on $108,716 of 2024 net earnings — about 0.9%, which reads more like loss carryforwards than the Act 60 decree the seller cites.
  • The financials have never been audited or reviewed. All statements are compilation-basis. The Chaparro 2021 balance sheet has a visible assets-vs-liabilities inconsistency in the source. EMR's 2024 statement omits the cash-flow statement entirely. The CPA of record changed in 2023–24. A quality-of-earnings review by an independent firm has to precede any closing.
  • The Act 60 decree may not survive the sale. Case 20-60-I-060, held by EMR, reportedly "remains in effect until transfer of ownership" — which is precisely the event contemplated. DDEC written consent to the change of control is a condition precedent in both drafts. Nobody has obtained the executed decree and read the transfer clause. The CPA's notes still describe the predecessor Act 135 regime.
  • Equipment is claimed but not documented. ~$1.5M of quarry heavy equipment with no inventory, no titles and no invoices produced. Fifty-two vehicles across four registered holders — including one personal vehicle and 21 units under an ambiguous "H.M.R." — with contradictory mixer inventories and no lien search by VIN.
  • There is no NDA between buyers and sellers, and the data room contains unredacted Social Security numbers and payroll data. That is a live exposure today, before anyone decides anything.

One inconsistency worth chasing early DOC

The figure $535,797 appears twice with two different creditors: the 27 August PSA calls it a shareholder loan owed by EMR to the Varelas (to be capitalized, forgiven or paid), while the other draft calls it an intercompany payable owed by EMR to Chaparro (to remain outstanding). Those cannot both be right, and the treatment differs materially in each case.

Decision

What Corey is actually being asked

Two questions, and they are separable.

  • OURS Is the asset good? On the evidence here, probably yes. A permitted limestone quarry with a 32-year reserve, in an opportunity zone, in a region with a documented multi-billion-dollar funded pipeline and almost no competing aggregate supply, running at a fifth of its own permitted output. That is a real position and it is hard to replicate.
  • OURS Is this deal good? Unknowable today. The largest economic term in the transaction — whether $4.5M–$6.3M of bank debt sits inside or on top of the price — has never been agreed. The interest rate on $14M is blank. Neither the land area, the equipment, the debt balance, the permit renewal, nor the tax exemption has been verified against a primary source. Every number in this brief comes from the seller's package or from unaudited compilations built on it.

The gap between those two answers is a diligence budget, not an investment decision. Before committing capital, the sequence counsel lays out is: sign an NDA and get the SSNs out of the data room; form the acquisition entity; agree a term sheet on the price-versus-debt question; then pay for four things — a quality-of-earnings review, title studies on all three parcels, an independent reserve study, and written confirmation from OGPe/DRNA on permit renewal and change of control.

Corey's specific question — whether to put resources here — turns on one thing this document set cannot answer: on which side of the table would he sit? The $1M of equity is currently SGC's obligation, the operating role is currently KBN's, and no teaming agreement between the two buyers exists yet. That is the conversation to have before the diligence conversation.

Scope

What this brief has not seen

Stated plainly so the group can weigh it, and so anyone who knows better can correct us.

  • The six spreadsheets are unopened — including the consolidated financial ledgers, which are the closest thing to primary transaction data in the set. Everything financial here comes from the compiled statements, not from the underlying ledgers.
  • The Act 60 decree itself is a scan with no readable text. Everything said about it comes from counsel's description and a DDEC cover email, not from reading the decree's transfer clause.
  • The limestone analysis is also an unreadable scan, so the 94.6% purity figure is unverified.
  • Both appraisals were identified but not read line by line — the FirstBank-commissioned 2024 report on the quarry and the 2021 Chaparro appraisal. They are the strongest independent documents in the set and deserve a proper read before any valuation argument leans on them.
  • No figure here has been checked against a government source — not the Property Registry, OGPe, DRNA, DDEC, CRIM, Hacienda, the SBA or either bank. That is exactly the work counsel's 35-item list describes, and none of it has been done.
  • The market case has not been independently sourced. The infrastructure figures are reproduced from analysis already in the set; we have not traced them to LUMA, CDBG-MIT or DTOP program documents.

One number was spot-checked against a primary record as a sanity test: Empresas Mundo Real's 2024 sales, net earnings and income tax all match the accountant's statement exactly ($1,597,692 / $108,716 / $987). The statements are legible and in English, so the rest of the financial history is verifiable the same way — it simply has not been done yet.
