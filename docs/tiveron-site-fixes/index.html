Six items to confirm or correct

Ordered by return for the time spent. The first two are single edits with site-wide effect.

1

robots.txt points search engines at a sitemap on the old domain, which 404s

2 minutesOne lineFully reversible
What we found

The last line of tiveronlaw.com/robots.txt reads:

Sitemap: https://www.hoganwillig.com/sitemap_index.xml

That URL returns 404. Meanwhile the site's own https://www.tiveronlaw.com/sitemap_index.xml works perfectly, returns 200, and lists all 964 URLs across eight child sitemaps. It is simply never declared.

Why it matters

robots.txt is the first file a crawler reads, and the sitemap line is how a site says "here is the complete list of everything I have." Tiveron currently points that instruction at a dead address on a domain the firm no longer trades under. Every crawler that follows it finds nothing and has to discover 964 URLs by following links instead.

Of everything in this document this is the smallest edit with the widest reach. It is one line.

How to verify it
  1. Open https://www.tiveronlaw.com/robots.txt in a browser and read the last line.
  2. Open https://www.hoganwillig.com/sitemap_index.xml. It returns Not Found.
  3. Open https://www.tiveronlaw.com/sitemap_index.xml and confirm it loads a full index.
The correction, if you agree
  1. In Yoast, go to Yoast SEO → Tools → File editor, where robots.txt can be edited directly.
  2. Change the sitemap line to the working address:
Sitemap: https://www.tiveronlaw.com/sitemap_index.xml
  1. Save, reload robots.txt in a browser, and confirm the new line is live.
  2. While you are there, submit the working sitemap in Search Console so the discovery is not left to chance.
2

The blog still tells search engines it belongs to HoganWillig

15 minutesOne templateFully reversible
What we found

We sampled 25 blog posts at random from the sitemap. 24 of the 25 carry a title tag ending:

... : HoganWillig Law Firm - Amherst, Buffalo, Ellicottville, Lancaster, Lockport New York

There are 689 posts in the sitemap. The rebrand was November 2022, so this has been the case for nearly four years. The visible page is correctly branded; it is the title tag, which is what a search engine displays and reads, that was never updated.

Why it matters

The title tag is the strongest single on-page signal of what a page is and who it belongs to. Across roughly 689 pages, the site is telling search engines that the firm's largest body of content belongs to a firm that no longer exists under that name. It is also the headline a searcher sees before deciding whether to click.

Because it is generated from a template rather than typed per post, the fix is one edit, not 689.

How to verify it
  1. Open any blog post and look at the browser tab, which shows the title tag.
  2. Or search Google for site:tiveronlaw.com HoganWillig and read the blue headlines in the results.
  3. Or View Page Source on a post and search for <title.
The correction, if you agree
  1. In Yoast, go to Search Appearance → Content Types → Posts.
  2. The SEO title field will contain a template with the old firm name in it. Replace the HoganWillig portion with the current branding, keeping the variable that inserts the post title.
  3. Save, then reload a post and check the tab. The change applies to every post at once.
  4. Check the other content types in the same panel, since practice pages, FAQs, attorneys and offices each have their own template and may carry the same text.
  5. One post slug also still reads hoganwillig-pllc-attorneys-receive-super-lawyers-recognition. Leave it. Changing a live URL costs more than it gains unless it is redirected properly.
3

The site's structured data names the old firm's social accounts as official, and one of them is dead

10 minutesSettings onlyFully reversible
What we found

The Organization block in the homepage structured data declares these as the firm's official accounts:

"sameAs": [
  "https://www.facebook.com/hoganwillig/",
  "https://x.com/hoganwillig"
]

The same old account is declared again in the page's article:publisher tag. x.com/hoganwillig returns 404. The current Tiveron accounts appear in the visible footer, but not in the structured data.

One qualification: Facebook blocks automated checks, so we could not determine the state of facebook.com/hoganwillig from outside. We are reporting what the markup declares, not what that page currently contains.

Why it matters

sameAs is how a site tells search engines and AI systems which other profiles on the internet are the same entity. It is the main mechanism for consolidating a firm's identity across the web. Tiveron currently uses it to point at the previous firm's accounts, one of which no longer exists.

The pattern is the same one running through this whole document: a human reading the site sees Tiveron everywhere, and a machine reading the same page is told HoganWillig.

How to verify it
  1. Open validator.schema.org, enter https://www.tiveronlaw.com/, and look at the Organization block's sameAs values.
  2. Or View Page Source and search for sameAs.
  3. Open https://x.com/hoganwillig and confirm the account is gone.
The correction, if you agree
  1. In Yoast, go to Settings → Site representation (older versions call it Social). The social account URLs entered there populate sameAs and article:publisher.
  2. Replace the HoganWillig entries with the current accounts already listed in the site footer: the Tiveron Facebook page, the Tiveron X account, and the Tiveron LinkedIn company page.
  3. Save, then re-run the schema validator on the homepage and confirm the new values appear.
  4. If any HoganWillig social accounts are still under the firm's control, rename rather than delete them, so their history and any inbound links survive.
4

All five office listings declare an image that does not exist

10 minutesNew since the auditFully reversible
What we found

Each of the five office blocks on the site carries structured data marking it as a business location. Inside each one is a hidden image declaration:

<img class="hide" alt="" itemprop="image"
     src=".../themes/paperstreet/images/logo.png" />

That file returns 404 Not Found. Because the tag is marked class="hide" it is invisible on the page, so nothing looks broken to a visitor. It appears five times on the homepage, once per office.

Why it matters

The image is the declared visual identity for each office in the firm's machine-readable record. Every office currently points at a file that is not there, so the one image each location offers a search engine is missing. It is a small thing, but it is five out of five, and it is invisible precisely because it was designed to be hidden.

How to verify it
  1. Open https://www.tiveronlaw.com/wp-content/themes/paperstreet/images/logo.png directly. It returns a 404 page.
  2. View Page Source on the homepage and search for images/logo.png. It appears five times.
The correction, if you agree
  1. Either restore a logo.png at that path in the theme's images folder, or update the template to point at a logo file that exists. The site already serves working SVG logos, so the asset is available.
  2. This lives in the PaperStreet theme template that renders the office blocks, so it is a developer edit rather than a settings change.
  3. Afterwards, load the URL again and confirm an image returns instead of a 404.
  4. Worth pairing with the note in the closing section about giving these office blocks proper structured data, since the same template is involved.
5

108 FAQ pages, each answering a real question, and none of them marked up as one

Varies, start with the templateBiggest opportunity hereReversible
What we found

The site has 108 FAQ pages. We sampled ten. All ten showed the same three things:

What we checkedResult across 10 sampled
The <h1> heading"Frequently Asked Questions" on all 10, identical. The actual question is only an H2
Meta descriptionLiterally .. on all 10
FAQ structured dataNone on any of the 10
Why it matters

This is the most valuable asset on the site and the one being wasted most completely. Someone at the firm wrote 108 answers to questions real clients actually ask, which is exactly the material an AI assistant looks for when someone types a legal question. Three things stop it being usable.

Every page claims to be about "Frequently Asked Questions" rather than about its own question, so 108 pages compete as near-duplicates instead of each owning its topic. The meta description is two full stops, which is what a search engine shows under the link. And without FAQ markup, nothing tells a machine that this page is a question with an answer, which is the format most likely to be quoted directly.

How to verify it
  1. Open any FAQ page and look at the largest heading. It reads "Frequently Asked Questions" rather than the question.
  2. View Page Source and search for name="description". The content is ...
  3. Run the page through Google's Rich Results Test. No FAQ type is detected.
  4. Search Google for site:tiveronlaw.com/faqs/ and look at how many results share the same headline.
The correction, if you agree
  1. Start with the template, not the 108 pages. In Yoast under Search Appearance → Content Types, find the FAQ post type and set the SEO title to use the page's own question rather than a fixed string.
  2. Fix the heading level in the theme template so the question is the H1 and "Frequently Asked Questions" becomes a label or is dropped.
  3. The meta descriptions are stored per page and will need writing, which is the one genuinely slow part. The first sentence of each answer is usually a serviceable description, so this is editing rather than authoring.
  4. Add FAQ structured data. A schema plugin can generate it from the question and answer already on the page, which avoids touching 108 pages by hand.
  5. Do a handful first and re-test with the Rich Results Test before running the whole set.

Every Google profile lists the Amherst main line, while the site lists local numbers

15 minutesBroader than the audit saidFully reversible
What we found

The audit reported this for the Buffalo office. Re-checking on 17 August shows it affects every branch, and that a Lockport profile exists which the audit said did not.

One further detail worth a decision rather than a fix: the Amherst profile's address resolves to Getzville, which is the postal designation for that building, while the site and the office name both say Amherst.

Why it matters

Name, address and phone consistency is one of the few things local search treats as a direct trust signal, and the phone number is the field it weighs most. When a profile and the website disagree about how to reach an office, a search engine has to decide which to believe, and the confidence it has in both drops.

There is a practical side too. Someone finding the Buffalo office in Maps calls the Amherst switchboard, and someone finding the same office on the website calls a Buffalo number. Whether that is intended routing or drift is something only the firm can say, which is why this is a confirm-or-deny rather than a defect.

How to verify it
  1. In a private browsing window, search Google Maps for Tiveron Law 155 Summer Street Buffalo NY and read the phone number on the profile.
  2. Compare with the Buffalo entry in the website footer.
  3. Repeat for Tiveron Law 770 Davison Road Lockport NY.
  4. Note that the Buffalo office page itself publishes the address without a phone number; the local number appears only in the footer office list.
The correction, if you agree
  1. Decide first whether each branch should publish its own line or route everything through the main number. Either is defensible. What costs you is publishing both.
  2. Once decided, make the Google profile and the website agree, office by office.
  3. Add the office's phone number to its own page, not only the footer, so each location page states its full details.
  4. Settle the Amherst and Getzville question the same way, and use the chosen version everywhere including the directory listings.

Once these are cleaned up, what actually makes the firm findable

The six items above are hygiene. They stop the site working against itself, and none of them will put Tiveron in front of someone searching for a lawyer. That is this list, and it is deliberately separate because most of it is not a quick edit.

WhatWhy it matters hereSize
Every deep URL on hoganwillig.com returns 404 The old homepage redirects correctly, but /amherst/, /lockport/, /real-estate-law/ and /attorneys/ all 404, checked again on 17 August. Those addresses accumulated links and citations over decades under the old name, and every one of them is currently a dead end rather than a path to the equivalent Tiveron page. A redirect map from the old URLs to their current counterparts is the single largest recoverable asset here. Real project, needs the old URL list
The firm's structured data is a stub The Organization block carries only name, url, logo, image and sameAs. There is no address, no telephone, no service area, and it is typed as a generic Organization rather than as a legal practice. In AI answers it is losing to small shops that publish specific, structured, plainly priced answers, which is exactly what the 108 FAQ pages already are before they are marked up. Follows from the rest

Worth stating plainly, since it is the thread running through both lists: the underlying work here is not missing. The answers are written, the offices are real, the reviews were earned, the attorneys have credentials. What is missing is the layer that tells a machine any of it exists, and in several places that layer still names a firm that closed in 2022.
