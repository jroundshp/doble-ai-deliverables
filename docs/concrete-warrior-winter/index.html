Concrete Warrior · New Town, North Dakota

Winter at New Town

Whether North Dakota earns its keep from November through April, or whether the equipment and the attention are better spent in Texas. This brief does not answer that. It narrows it, and it lists what we still have to know.

29 August 2026 For Corey Hogan Decision brief · not a recommendation

The question

This is a resource call, not a weather call

New Town is one of several places the same money and the same attention could go. So the honest question is not “can we operate in a North Dakota winter,” which is a question about equipment. It is whether five months at New Town beats the next best use of the same resources, including moving the emphasis south.

Framed that way, there are three doors, and they cost wildly different amounts.

DOOR 1

Stay north, full production

Crushers, plant and crew running through the winter at New Town.

DOOR 2

Go dark, shift to Texas

Close the yard at the end of October as planned and put the season into Patriot.

DOOR 3

Keep a presence, not a plant

Stop producing, but hold a yard, a stockpile and a phone number through the winter.

Almost every conversation about this collapses doors 1 and 3 into “staying open,” and they are not the same decision. One is a crushing spread and a crew. The other is a loader and somebody answering the phone.

What is already decided for us

The state sets the calendar

One part of this is not a judgment call. Reclamation topsoil is the lead product, and when a customer can take delivery is fixed by regulation, because a well site is not released from its bond until topsoil is respread and native grass is established. Native grass has two seeding windows a year: before May 24, or after October 20.

On top of the spring window sit the NDDOT spring load restrictions. In 2026 Burleigh County posted on March 5 and Ward County did not lift until May 28.

Two things follow, and both are structural rather than opinions. The spring window is largely unhaulable, because the road ban covers most of it. And the fall window opens eleven days before the current plan sends the crushers home. Whatever door gets picked, it should be picked knowing that the one stretch of the year when a reclamation customer can freely take delivery is the stretch we currently spend leaving.

One number to be careful with

The 2,160 is the whole state, and the rule expects an operator to respread the site's own topsoil, salvaged eight inches deep when the pad was built. Our market is the share where that stockpile is short, lost or contaminated. It is the denominator, not the opportunity, and it should not be quoted as ours.

What we do not know

The gate

This is the real content of the brief. Nothing above decides anything on its own, because the cost side is missing entirely and so is the Texas comparison. The unknowns sort into three piles by who can close them.

You already know these

Corey and Richard · no research required · answer in a conversation

  1. What does it cost to keep the New Town yard staffed, plowed and insured for a month with no production running? Sets the floor that door 3 has to clear. Without it, door 3 cannot be evaluated at all.
  2. What does the Texas emphasis actually earn from November through April? This is the number North Dakota is competing against. Everything else is secondary to it.
  3. Is the batch plant winterized, and what would it cost to make it so? Decides whether ready-mix is even on the table in door 1, or whether door 1 is aggregate only.
  4. What does it cost to mobilize the crushers south and back again? If moving iron twice a year is expensive enough, it may quietly favor staying put regardless of demand.
  5. Is there stockpile capacity and yard space at New Town to hold winter inventory? Door 3 is a stockpiling strategy. No room, no door 3.

We can get these

Doble AI · roughly a week · public records and phone calls

  1. Who are the reclamation and plugging contractors working the Bakken? They order topsoil by the load. They are the actual customer and we do not have their names yet. NDIC contract awards list them.
  2. How many of the 2,160 sites awaiting bond release sit in Mountrail, McKenzie, Dunn and McLean? Turns a statewide number into a radius number. DMR does not publish it, so this means asking them directly.
  3. What do the counties and the MHA Nation buy for winter road maintenance, and on what schedule? The only hard test of the door 3 assumption. Public budgets and bid records.
  4. Do the local competitors run through winter, and what do they sell off the pile? If Knife River holds a winter yard in New Town, that answers the demand question without us guessing.
  5. Does the Nation's TERO preference change who wins reclamation work on the reservation? Sitting in New Town may be worth more than the seasonal math on its own.

Nobody has built these yet

Not started · a separate piece of work · scope before committing

  1. The Texas side of the comparison. Door 2 is being treated as the safe default, but Patriot's winter market, cost base and capacity have not been evaluated the way North Dakota now has. Right now we are comparing a measured option against an assumed one.
  2. What a season at New Town actually produces and sells. The site still carries this as an open gap. Yards per week, what a typical reclamation order looks like, and how far the haul really goes.
  3. Whether any customer will commit to fall or winter volume in advance. One signed commitment changes this from a forecast into a decision. It is also the cheapest thing to go find out.

The shape of the answer

What each door costs if it is wrong

The three doors are not symmetric, and that asymmetry probably matters more than the demand numbers.

Cheap to test

Run to freeze-up

Oct 20 → late Nov

An extension of a season already staffed, into a window with no road bans and firm ground. Not a new commitment, just a later end date.

If wrong: a few weeks of payroll and fuel against thin demand. Recoverable inside one season.

Expensive to be wrong

Full winter production

Dec → Feb

Wash water freezes, so washed rock stops. Ready-mix demand falls once cold-weather pour rules apply. Frozen wet feed is slow and hard on the plant.

If wrong: a winter of fixed cost with no revenue, and iron in the wrong state when Texas needs it.

Uncosted

Hold the yard

All winter

Scoria, Class 5, Class 13 and gravel sell off the pile without the crusher running. Also pre-positions material ahead of the March road bans, which is the only way to serve spring at all.

If wrong: the carrying cost of a yard nobody calls. Small, but genuinely unknown until question one is answered.

Next

The cheapest questions first

Two of these cost a conversation and would move the decision more than another month of research. Question one on your list sets the floor for door 3. Question two is the number North Dakota has to beat, and until it exists, the Texas option is being compared on instinct.

If those two come back and North Dakota still looks close, the work in the second pile is worth doing and takes about a week. If they come back and Texas wins outright, this stops here and nothing further gets spent on it.

The one thing worth saying plainly: door 2 is currently the default rather than a conclusion. It may well be right. It has just not been measured against the same standard North Dakota has now been held to.
