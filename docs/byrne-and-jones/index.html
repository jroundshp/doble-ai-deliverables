02 · Baseline

What Is Already Right

This section is longer than it usually is, because most of the fundamentals are genuinely handled. Anything below this line does not need work, and knowing that changes what the rest of the audit is worth spending money on.

Verified working · August 16, 2026
  • Every page is readable by machines. Service copy, FAQ answers and project descriptions all appear in the served HTML rather than being painted in by JavaScript. This is the floor that everything else in AI search depends on, and plenty of well-designed sites fail it.
  • Canonicals are clean across all 152 URLs. Zero mismatches, zero status errors, zero noindex pages sitting in the sitemap. Nothing on the site is quietly declaring itself a duplicate of something else.
  • robots.txt is hand-tuned, not stock. Someone deliberately blocked search parameters, ad-click IDs, oEmbed endpoints and the SDS PDF library while whitelisting the employment application. That is careful crawl-budget work.
  • All six Google Business Profiles are claimed and owner-managed. Each one links to its own market page with a ?utm_campaign=gmb tag, so the attribution was set up on purpose. Ratings run 4.5 to 5.0, with Hilliard showing 5.0 across 31 reviews.
  • Schema is rich and mostly correct. GeneralContractor, Place, PostalAddress, Service, Offer, VideoObject and BreadcrumbList, plus real FAQ markup on 23 pages.
  • The sports pages are exceptional. Running tracks carries 26 question and answer pairs, turf field installation 24, tennis courts 13. This is the depth that gets a page quoted by an AI assistant, and it is already paying: you rank first for track and turf queries in Missouri and second nationally for Midwest pickleball court construction.
  • The 404 page is custom and on-brand. Which is how we could tell the EV button was broken rather than the server being down.
03 · One Finding Worth Its Own Section

Twenty-eight pages have no internal links pointing at them, and they are the sales pages

We crawled every page in your sitemap and built the internal link graph from the served HTML. Twenty-eight pages came back with zero inbound links. A crawler can still reach them through the sitemap, so they are not invisible to Google. But a human being cannot reach them by clicking, from anywhere on your site.

Twelve of twenty-eight shown. The remaining sixteen are blog posts and award pages. The full list, with a suggested parent page for each, is in the free repair pack at the bottom of this audit.

Read that list as a sales problem rather than an SEO problem. Every case study you have is unreachable. The page explaining cooperative purchasing, which is how a school district or parks department buys from you without running a competitive bid, is unreachable. Your press coverage is unreachable. The page where an existing customer requests maintenance is unreachable.

Someone wrote all of this. Someone approved it. It is good material. A buyer comparing you against ATG Sports or Mid-America Sports Construction cannot find any of it without a direct link from a salesperson.

Why this matters more than it looks

Internal links do two jobs at once. They carry ranking signal, and they carry people. The SEO half is real but modest. The sales half is the expensive part: you are paying to produce proof material that never reaches the person deciding whether to shortlist you.

This is also the cheapest finding in the audit to fix. No new content, no new pages. It is a set of links from pages that already exist to pages that already exist.

06 · Legacy Debt

Two migrations left residue that is still live

The HubSpot blog is still online and still ranking. blog.byrneandjones.com returns a 200 with the title "Byrne & Jones Construction Blog" and zero posts. Its RSS feed is empty. Its footer reads 2019 Byrne & Jones Construction. Its navigation is the pre-migration company: Rhino Pave Asphalt, and Missouri, Nebraska and Ohio divisions, with no Nashville, no Kansas City, no Illinois and no Parks division. It carries no canonical tag and no robots directive, so nothing tells Google to ignore it.

It surfaced third in our brand search, above your own About page. A prospect checking you out can land on an empty 2019 shell of your company. Redirect the subdomain to /blog/ and the problem is gone permanently.

Retired URLs are still linked from live pages. The 2024 WordPress migration reorganized the site and the internal links never fully followed. Nine legacy paths are still linked from pages people read today. Two live blog posts link to /sports/tennis-court-pickleball-court-construction/, which now takes two redirect hops to land. Your sports hub links to /sports/landscape-turf/, which redirects to playground installation. Your About page links to /Ohio/ with a capital O. One blog post has an href with a trailing space inside the URL. None of this breaks a page, but all of it is real and all of it is mechanical to fix.

A Nebraska phone number is sitting in the Ohio page's HTML. /ohio/ contains a text block reading "Free Estimates -> (531) 201-6959," which is the Omaha and Lincoln number. Worth being precise about the impact: we rendered the page and that block does not display to a visitor, so nobody is calling Nebraska by mistake. But it is in the served document, which means crawlers, AI engines and screen readers see a Nebraska number on your Columbus page. For a business whose local ranking depends on consistent name, address and phone data, that is a small pollutant in the exact place it hurts. Delete the leftover module.

One item for you rather than for us: /ohio/asphalt-maintenance-and-repair/ shows (614) 957-2083 while every other Ohio page shows (614) 662-4572. That is very often a deliberate call-tracking line, so we are flagging it as a question rather than scoring it as an error.

08 · What We Would Do

Ranked by Return, Not by Effort

01Fix the EV page button10 minutes
Change the href from the old absolute URL to #EVContact. The form it should reach is already on the page. This is the only finding in the audit that is actively losing a lead today.
02Link the 28 orphaned pagesDone for you
Case studies belong on the service page for the work they describe and on the sports hub. Cooperative purchasing belongs in the main navigation, because it removes a procurement barrier for exactly the school and municipal buyers you want. The press page belongs on About. The full map, with a suggested parent for each page, is in the free pack below.
03Redirect blog.byrneandjones.comOne DNS or HubSpot change
301 the whole subdomain to /blog/. It removes an empty 2019 version of your company from your brand results permanently, and consolidates whatever authority the old domain still holds.
04Rewrite St. Louis and Nashville around buyer language
Bring both up to the depth of the Omaha page, which is your own proof that it works. Take the questions from the miss list: what a commercial lot costs per square foot, how long a resurface closes a lot, who handles ADA compliance, what happens to the business during the pour. Two FAQ pairs on the headquarters page against 26 on the running track page is the gap in one number.
05Wrap the 21 existing question headings in FAQ schema
The copy exists and reads well. Rank Math can emit FAQPage JSON-LD per page, so this is a markup pass rather than a writing project. Start with the homepage, the asphalt hub and the six question-structured blog posts.
06Clear the legacy link debtList provided
Nine retired paths, one two-hop chain, one capital-letter URL, one href with a space in it, and twelve site-wide patterns that drop the trailing slash. All 32 are listed with source page and replacement target in the pack below.
07Re-optimize the migrated blog
Around 30 posts came across in May 2024 with HubSpot-era metadata. Descriptions run to 301 characters and get truncated, one post has no H1, and 12 posts have no inbound links from anywhere. Rather than rewriting them, decide which ones still earn a place, consolidate the rest, and give the survivors current descriptions and a link from the service page they support.
08Measure AI visibility monthly
Run a fixed set of buyer prompts across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Grok, screenshot the answers, and track who gets recommended and which third-party sources the engines are citing. Your sports pages are already winning, which gives a clean baseline to measure the paving work against.
