Puerto Rico Quarry

Isabela and Aguada · $15M asking

A permitted limestone quarry on the northwest coast, running at 20–30% of its own permitted output, in an opportunity zone with a 4% tax decree to 2034 and very little competing aggregate supply nearby. The asset is genuinely hard to replicate. The transaction around it is not yet a transaction: four unexecuted drafts, counsel’s 35 open items, and a price structure whose largest term was never agreed. Nothing is signed and nothing is verified against a primary record.

jroundshp.github.io/doble-ai-deliverables/pr-quarry/
Questions to resolve
  1. How much rock is actually left? The seller’s package implies a 32-year reserve. An external appraiser, in the company’s own accounting notes, implies closer to twelve, with some of it already extracted. The entire asset case turns on which is right.
  2. Does the bank debt sit inside the price or on top of it? Between $4.5M and $6.3M, it is the largest economic term in the deal and it has never been agreed. The interest rate on the $14M seller note is still blank.
  3. Which side of the table is Corey on? The $1M of equity is currently SGC’s obligation and the operating role is currently KBN’s. No teaming agreement between the two buyers exists. That conversation comes before the diligence conversation.

North Carolina Trailer Contract

Temporary Disaster Shelters · awarded, runs to Mar 2028

North Carolina Emergency Management awarded TDS a three-year disaster contract for travel trailers and RVs on lowest responsive price. The award is real and it guarantees nothing: the solicitation says twice that no maximum or minimum quantities are guaranteed, so revenue exists only when the state issues a task work order. TDS’s own history shows what that is worth in practice, listing two earlier North Carolina contracts written in identical terms, one at $37.4M and the next at $409,050. It is also a field-services contract rather than a trailer sale: utilities, tie-downs, permits, then maintenance for as long as a work order leaves a unit on the ground.

jroundshp.github.io/doble-ai-deliverables/nc-tds/
Questions to resolve
  1. What did TDS bid on Attachment A? The pricing form in our copy is blank across all four line items. Without those numbers there is no margin analysis and no valuation, only narrative. This is the one document that changes the answer.
  2. How many task work orders has the state issued since 20 March 2025, and for how many units? Seventeen months in. If the answer is none, this is a pre-qualification worth holding rather than a business.
  3. Is the lease model live, or was it declined in favor of the purchase award? The award letter cites only the purchase solicitation, and the two structures have opposite economics.

Navy Unaccompanied Housing

Temporary Disaster Shelters · RFI response, no award

This one needs its status corrected before anything else. It is a response to a Request for Information, which is market research at the earliest stage of federal procurement. There is no award, no contract number, no period of performance and no dollar figure, and the workbook records zero units currently available. The 1 October 2026 date attached to it is TDS’s own estimate of when it could first house someone. The offer itself is substantial on paper, 2,000 berthing units and 500 MWR units across ten installations on a turnkey lease, and the packet leaves the housing entirely unpriced while pricing the catering to the cent.

jroundshp.github.io/doble-ai-deliverables/navy-tds/
Questions to resolve
  1. Where does the $300,000,000 North Carolina figure come from? The packet’s contract history states that value for the same award whose letter sets no value at all. Ask TDS directly, and ask it before anything else here moves.
  2. What does a berthing unit cost per month? Every housing cost field in the packet is blank. Without a rate there is no way to compare this against the other opportunities.
  3. Was a completed workbook filed? In the copy we have, the company tab carries no company name and the locations tab has no installation selected.

One thing that cuts across two of them

Temporary Disaster Shelters is the counterparty in both the North Carolina and the Navy files, and its self-reported contract history is the main evidence available about its track record. That history contains one entry we could test against primary documents, and it failed: the North Carolina award is listed at $300,000,000 against an award letter that sets no value and a solicitation that guarantees no quantities.

The practical consequence is narrow but real. Figures sourced from TDS across both briefs are marked as vendor-asserted and should be treated as leads to verify, not as evidence. That includes the $37.4M and $409,050 pair, which does cross-check across two separate packages twenty months apart, and which the North Carolina page now carries a caveat against for exactly this reason.
