One — What we believe we understand Doc

TDS responded to RFIUH-2026, a Request for Information issued by NAVFAC, with a packet dated 23 August 2026 addressed to Ms. Lita Staskey. An RFI is market research: the agency is finding out what industry can do before deciding whether to buy anything, so there is no award here, no solicitation number beyond the RFI reference, no contract number and no period of performance. We say that only because the opportunity has been described to us in other terms elsewhere, and we wanted to be sure we had the stage right.

The offer is 2,000 berthing units and 500 MWR units, 2,500 in total, across ten installations from Great Lakes to Pearl Harbor, structured as a turnkey lease rather than a sale. The cover letter is explicit about that choice: "Although we can sell all the equipment, we do not see any advantage to the Government in purchasing these items." Scope is roughly 11,900 Navy personnel.

The capability is assembled from named partners rather than held in-house: Forest River for berthing units, CAVCO Industries for MWR, Government Leasing, LLC for the lease structure, and food service under two names that both appear in the packet, MCM Solutions, LLC and Morgan Chase Management. Vendor

Two dates we noted. Earliest ready for occupancy is 1 October 2026, which the workbook records as TDS's own estimate of when it could first house someone rather than any start date set by the Navy. And pricing is stated as holding "for a period of one year from August 24, 2026."

Open We have your answer but not the Navy's question. The RFI itself is not in what reached us, so the requirements, the evaluation approach and the timing all sit in a document we have not read. Nothing in our files confirms the packet was submitted or received either, which is why this note says "prepared for NAVFAC" throughout rather than "sent."

Two — What the submission consists of Doc

The 39-page packet carries a cover letter, a contract history, the catering subcontractor's submission, a lessor's rental schedule at pages 29 to 32, and camp and suite floorplans at pages 25 to 28. Alongside it is the Navy's own response workbook, which the Navy describes as existing "to collect consistent, complete, and analyzable data," with instructions on its first tab to complete Company_Info first, then Locations_Master, then a solution tab for each proposed solution.

This is question 1 below and it is the first thing we would like to clear up, because if a completed workbook went in then most of this paragraph is moot.

Five — Our questions

Twenty-one, grouped and numbered so they can be worked through in order or answered by number. If we only get through five, we would pick 1, 2, 3, 5 and 11. Those are the ones where an answer changes how we read everything else.

Group A — The submission and the offer

  1. Was a completed workbook filed, with Company_Info and Locations_Master populated?

    Our copy has both tabs blank and is a download named "(1)", so we may just be holding the wrong save. If a fuller copy exists we should be reading that one.

  2. Is the offer 11,900 beds or 15,000?

    Total Units holds a live formula and returns 2,500. Total Beds has a typed 11,900 where the formula was. At six beds per unit those do not agree, and 11,900 is also the cover letter's Navy headcount.

  3. What does a berthing unit cost per month, and was leaving housing unpriced a deliberate choice at RFI stage?

    We assume it was. But every housing cost field is blank while the caterer priced six tiers to the cent, and without a monthly figure there is no way to size this against anything else.

  4. What does the Development Status field mean here?

    It reads "Operating" on the same tab where units currently available reads zero. We suspect it refers to the company rather than the fleet.

  5. What stands behind Forest River and CAVCO — supply agreements, letters of intent, or working relationships?

    The whole capability rests on them, and no agreement with either is in what we received. The same goes for the claimed 250 units a day and the ramp from 50 a day at ten days to 300 a day at thirty.

  6. Are the 6-hour and 48-hour response times a considered position?

    Both are above the Navy's stated desire of two and twenty-four, and both match what TDS already offers on disaster work. Would they be revised if this became a solicitation, or held?

  7. Was Government Leasing's Rental Schedule 26281 meant to be part of the response, or attached for reference?

    It reads as a proposed deal inside market research, and its title, risk-of-loss and renewal-commitment clauses are the sort that get struck. Its period of performance also says 60 months over a table ending at month 48.

  8. What is the basis for serving Hawaii by ocean barge from San Diego?

    Transit time and cost are the parts an evaluator would press on, and neither is in the packet.

  9. What does the 1 October 2026 ready-for-occupancy date assume?

    With zero units built, that date implies a production and delivery sequence we cannot see.

  10. Does the one-year price validity from 24 August 2026 still hold if a solicitation lands later than that?

    RFI to solicitation often runs longer than a year.

Group B — The contract history

  1. Where does the $300,000,000 figure in entry 49 come from?

    The award letter and IFB we hold state no value for that contract. Is there a separate North Carolina agreement we have not been sent, or does the history draw its figures from a different source than the award file?

  2. Is the contract history maintained by hand?

    The entry immediately above 49 reads $24,755,00.00, which looks like a digit dropped in typing. If the list is manual, that would explain the term and contact differences too, and it would tell us how to weight the rest of it.

  3. Is company tenure "over 32 years" or "over 28 years"?

    This packet says 32 and the North Carolina package says 28, describing the same company about twenty months apart.

  4. Is there a receipt or portal confirmation for the submission?

    We have a dated letter and a workbook, and nothing that evidences filing. It is the reason we have been careful to write "prepared for" rather than "sent."
