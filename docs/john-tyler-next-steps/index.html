02 · The Main Ask

Your listing pages, and the one reason Google will not index them

The assessment said your listing pages are too thin. That was true but imprecise, so here is what we actually measured this morning.

We pulled two of your live listing pages and compared them word for word. One runs 276 words, the other 285. Of those, 242 words are byte-identical between the two pages. The navigation, your bio, the contact block, the footer, the photo credits. All of it repeats on every listing.

That leaves 34 words on a listing page that are actually about that property, and twenty of those are placeholder text we wrote from the MLS facts.

So Google is not looking at a thin page. It is looking at fourteen pages that are 88 percent the same page, and declining to index them for that reason. That is what "Discovered, currently not indexed" means in Search Console, and it is what your words fix.

One note on the number seventeen

The assessment said seventeen listing pages. That counts pages, and your site is bilingual, so each property has an English page and a Spanish one. Seven properties. You write once per property, we translate it, and both pages are fixed. Nothing here is asking you for Spanish, and nothing is asking you to write anything twice.

What we need, part one: three or four sentences per property

Why: it replaces the twenty placeholder words with something that exists nowhere else on the internet. Right now the page repeats the MLS description that Google has already read on Zillow and on the Slifer site, so yours is the last copy it has any reason to show.

What we need, part two: five answers per property

Why: these become a questions-and-answers block on each listing page. Your neighborhood pages already have this and it is the single biggest reason they get picked up by AI assistants. Not one of your listing pages has it. Five answers takes each page from 34 unique words to roughly 250, and it puts the answer on your page when someone asks an AI what the dues cover at Ridgepoint.

The same five questions, every property

  1. What does the HOA cover, and what does it run? Dues, what is included, and anything assessed.
  2. Can it be rented short term, and does the building allow it? The first question most second-home buyers ask and the one they cannot look up.
  3. Where is the parking, is it deeded, and how many spaces? In Vail this is frequently the deciding factor and it is rarely in the listing.
  4. What floor, which exposure, and what do you see out the windows?
  5. What conveys with the sale? Furnished, partly furnished, anything excluded.

04 · Finished And Waiting

The short-term rental post is written and sitting on the shelf

Both languages, nine questions and answers on each side, built and committed. It is not live because of two figures we could not confirm at the source.

The post is "Short-Term Rentals in the Vail Valley: What to Confirm Before You Count on the Income." It is structured around the three gates a buyer has to clear, in the order that actually decides the outcome: the HOA and the covenants first, because Vail's short-term rental chapter does not override private CC and Rs, then the jurisdiction, then licensing and taxes.

1Confirm the Town of Vail license fees
What we have: $260 for a standard short-term rental license, and $50 for a property in a building with 24/7 on-site management. One license per property, renewal at the end of January, expiry at the end of February.

Why we need you: vail.gov blocks automated access, so we could not read those numbers off the town's own page. They come from secondary sources. We will not publish a fee figure we could not confirm at the primary source, because if it is wrong it is wrong on your page under your name. One call to the town clears it.

One thing to watch while you are asking: there is a widely repeated "$1,200 per bedroom" Vail fee that is not real. A per-bedroom fee was proposed at $1,800 and struck before adoption. If anyone quotes you a per-bedroom number, that is where it comes from.
2Confirm the Ballot Issue 2A margin
What we have: Vail's 6 percent short-term rental excise tax failed in November 2025 by 32 votes. It would have taken lodging tax from about 10.8 percent to 16.8 percent.

Why we need you: the sources disagree. Vail Daily reported 32 votes as the final margin, the Colorado Sun reported 916 to 881 on election night. We would rather say nothing than say the wrong number about a local vote your clients followed.
3Tell us the legal framing is fine with Slifer
