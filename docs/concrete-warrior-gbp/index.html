02

Why the listing comes first

It moves faster than the website will

Type "gravel near New Town" into a phone and the map results sit above the website results. A business with no profile is not in that list at all, and no amount of work on the website changes that. The listing is a separate and faster channel than organic search, and it is the one that gets a call from somebody standing in a pit at 7am.

It is also the cheapest thing on the whole project. The profile costs nothing. The mailbox under it runs about $170 a year.

Or add all four from the terminal

npx vercel dns add $D '@' TXT "google-site-verification=PASTE" $S
npx vercel dns add $D '@' MX smtp.google.com 1 $S
npx vercel dns add $D '@' TXT "v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all" $S
npx vercel dns add $D '_dmarc' TXT "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:admin@concretewarriorsupply.com" $S

On the name — this is still open

There is one rule and it decides everything else: the listing name has to be the real-world name, meaning what is on the sign, the invoices and the truck doors. Google checks it during verification, and it is what the video is looking at.

As far as anyone knows there is no sign at the yard yet, and vinyl door magnets for the trucks are being discussed for the 24 August shoot. So the order runs the other way around from usual. Richard and Roger decide what goes on the magnets and the sign, and the listing copies it.

The recommendation, if a sign is being ordered anyway

Concrete Warrior Supply. If the sign has to be made regardless, this is the cheapest moment there will ever be to fix the supplier-versus-contractor problem, and it costs one extra word. Put it on the sign, the invoices and the truck doors, file the trade name, and the listing matches everything.

If a sign already exists that nobody has told us about, that settles it instead. Copy the sign.

What is wrong either way

Do not add the town or the materials. Not "Concrete Warrior New Town," not "Concrete Warrior Ready Mix & Aggregate." Padding a listing name with keywords is the most common cause of an edit or a suspension, and the words earn nothing there that the categories do not earn properly. Whether "LLC" goes on the end is also decided by the sign rather than by preference.

On the categories, which matter more than the name

Google's category list changes, so type each phrase into the field and pick what appears rather than trusting my spelling. If a phrase is not offered, take the nearest supplier category and move on.

The one to stay out of is concrete contractor. That is where Warrior Concrete LLC and every other Warrior-named outfit in the country already sits, and it describes somebody who pours a driveway rather than somebody who sells material by the ton. Since the name cannot differentiate this business, the category has to.

Drag the pin by hand

Google's guess at 9702 ND-23 carries the warning "the location shown is not precise," which means it interpolated a point from the road. Zoom into satellite view and move the marker onto the actual yard entrance where a truck would turn in. Get this right the first time; corrections after verification are slow.

08

The New Town video

Step 8 · forward this section as is

This is the only part of the project that has to happen on site. It is a phone task rather than a computer task, and the whole thing takes about ten minutes including the walk.

Who films it

Amanda or Richard, whoever is at the yard when the link arrives. Forward this section to them as written.

What Google needs to see

Four things, in one continuous take. Do not stop the recording and do not edit it afterward. Google is watching for cuts, because cuts are how people fake this.

  1. The sign, and the road it sits on. Start here. Hold on the sign long enough to read it, then pan to the road so the surroundings are visible. If there is a street sign or a mile marker nearby, catch it.
  2. The yard, walking. Keep moving and keep filming. Stockpiles, the crusher, the batch plant, loaders, whatever is standing. This is what proves it is a materials operation rather than an empty lot.
  3. Something that shows you run it. Climb into the cab and start a machine, or open the office and show paperwork with Concrete Warrior on it, or unlock a gate with your own keys. Google calls this proof of management and it is the part people usually forget.
  4. End wide. One slow pan across the whole operation before you stop recording.

Practical notes for whoever is filming

  • Shoot landscape, phone turned sideways.
  • Two to five minutes is plenty. Under thirty seconds looks thin.
  • Retake it as many times as you want. Nothing is submitted until you press submit, so a bad take costs nothing.
  • Do not narrate for Google's benefit. Nobody is scoring the commentary.
  • Windy day is fine. Audio does not matter.

If there is no sign

Stop and tell Buffalo before filming. A missing sign does not make verification impossible, but it makes it considerably harder, and it may be worth ordering one first. This is also the question that has been open since 20 August and still has no answer.

The handover to Corey

What moves, what already lives there, and when

Once the website is built and dialed in, John transfers the domain to Corey's organization and they hold the whole thing. That is the plan and it has been since the domain was bought on 14 August.

Set up the way this document describes, that handover is close to a non-event. Most of what would normally have to change hands never sits with us in the first place.

AssetWhere it sitsWhat the handover takes
Google Business Profileoffice@concretewarriorsupply.comNothing. It is already inside Corey's Workspace and Breanna already owns it.
Email and WorkspaceCorey's billing, BuffaloNothing. Settled, on his billing from day one.
DomainJohn's Vercel accountA transfer. This is the real one.
Website hostingVercel project concretewarriorsupplyMoves with or after the domain, depending on who maintains the site.

Timing on the domain

ICANN locks a newly registered domain against a registrar transfer for 60 days, so a move to Corey's own registrar cannot happen before roughly 13 October 2026. Corey already knows and has said it is not a problem.

If a faster route is ever wanted, a team-to-team transfer inside Vercel has no lock at all. That gets Corey's organization holding it immediately while leaving the site where it is.

The thing to decide before, not after

DNS control moves with the domain. Right now John holds it, which is the whole reason he appears in this document at all. After the transfer, anything that needs a DNS record, a new mail alias on a different provider, a subdomain, a verification string for some other service, goes through whoever administers Corey's account rather than through him.

That is fine and it is the point of handing it over. It is worth saying out loud once, because the failure mode is nobody realizing it until something needs changing on a Friday.

Two things worth keeping after the transfer

Doble AI should stay a Manager on the Google Business Profile if there is ongoing findability work, since a Manager cannot delete anything or change ownership. And the Workspace should keep admin@ as a super admin account distinct from anyone's daily login, for the same reason it was created that way: people change jobs and role accounts do not.
