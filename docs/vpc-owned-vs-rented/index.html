What VPC owns, and what VPC rents

The web estate as it actually stands. Who holds what, what it is doing for VPC today, where the holes are, and the questions that decide which path is the right one.

Checked live on 6 September 2026. This replaces the 17 August fix guide, three of whose six items VPC has already shipped. It sits alongside the findability review of 1 September rather than replacing it: that document is the technical read, this one is the ownership map.

00

The short version

The finding is not that VPC’s website needs work. The site is well built, and our July audit said so before we knew any of this.

The finding is that VPC does not own its own identity in the places buyers and machines look it up. The company description on the industrial directory was written by a vendor. The product catalog, where every product page lives, runs on that vendor’s software. And VPC’s own website carries markup telling Google and the AI assistants that the vendor’s directory page is an authority on VPC. The one property VPC fully controls is 23 pages and has almost no product information on it, because the products were moved onto rented ground.

In a sentence: the products are the business, and the product data is not VPC’s.

01

How it is actually set up

There are four websites and a directory record, and they are not the same kind of thing. That distinction is the whole document, because it separates the work that needs a login from the work that needs a negotiation.

The footer of every page on both vpcfiberglass.com and products.vpcfiberglass.com states it in public: “Site created by Thomas Marketing Services and Powered by Navigator Platform.” So this is not a discovery, it is a fact that has been on the page all along and that nobody had cause to read closely.

One sentence for the whole architecture: the marketing site and the product catalog are two different systems, on two different platforms, with two different owners, joined by a subdomain and a lot of links. Each page of the main site sends between 37 and 50 internal links into the catalog it does not control.

02

What VPC is getting from it today

This part is real, and it needs saying before the list of holes, because a document that only complains gets discounted and should be.

  • A genuinely well-engineered site. Fast, measured between roughly 0.05 and 0.16 seconds to first byte across checks in August and September, valid SSL on both hostnames, with the apex redirecting correctly to www in a single hop from https and in two from a bare http address, canonical tags in place, a working sitemap, no cloaking, and pinch-to-zoom left enabled where every sibling business in the portfolio disables it. Substantially better built than anything else we have audited in the group.
  • A product catalog that exists and is organised. 289 pages with names, descriptions, images and a category tree, plus a configurator, attribute model and CAD publishing behind it. Building that from nothing is a real project. VPC did not have to run it.
  • Educational content that actually ranks. The /resources/ pages are the residue of genuine work and they perform. That is the asset the outside firm noticed and praised.
  • A claimed directory profile with broad category coverage on the largest industrial directory in North America, listing VPC across dozens of product categories from adapters and baffles through to weirs.
  • Six authority links in the structured data including Dun & Bradstreet, BBB, Bloomberg and two trade bodies. Most manufacturers this size have none, and this was fixed correctly in late August.
  • A BABA compliance page with its own place in the navigation. For infrastructure-funded municipal and utility work this is a procurement eligibility, and it is the strongest credential on the site.
  • Capable hands. Three of six items from a written fix guide shipped inside three days in late August, with no meeting and no correspondence. Whatever else is unresolved, execution is not the constraint.

None of that is nothing. The problem in this document is not quality of work. It is where the work landed.

03

Where the holes are, in order of what they cost

  1. The product data is hard for a machine to trust. The catalog does describe its products in markup, but as microdata only, with no JSON-LD anywhere, and the markup is noisy: the product’s name property picks up breadcrumb text, and a price offer appears on one sampled item page and none of the other four. So a parser gets something, and what it gets is unreliable. Cleaning that up and adding JSON-LD is the most valuable single item in the estate, and it has been the top recommendation since July.
  2. The product data is rented. Which means fixing the item above may not be VPC’s to do. This is the one genuine fork in the document and it is unresolved.
  3. The best product writing VPC has is on somebody else’s website. VPC’s directory listing describes shelters by dimension and by application, naming the sizes and a dozen situations a buyer would be searching from. Nothing like it exists on vpcfiberglass.com. The content is written. It is just not VPC’s to keep, mark up or rank with.
  4. The entity record is incomplete and VPC’s own markup endorses it. The directory profile reports annual sales under $1 million, one to nine employees and no quality certifications. Those size figures look like a field nobody filled in rather than a claim, since several competitors on the same page show identical values. Other public business directories carry different ranges again for VPC, none of them agreeing with each other, which is the ordinary state of scraped firmographic data and another reason to set the record straight in the one place VPC’s own markup points at. But VPC’s LocalBusiness markup names that page as an authority on VPC, so whatever it says is what an assistant reads.
  5. The best credential is in the weakest position. BABA compliance is a procurement eligibility that decides whether VPC can be bought at all on federally funded work. It does not appear in the parts of the directory profile we read, it is absent from structured data, and on the site itself it sits one level down inside an About submenu.
  6. Two unrelated businesses share one front door. A wastewater engineer specifying a Parshall flume and a campground owner buying a splash pad land on the same homepage, which is written for neither of them in particular.
  7. The two satellite sites say who owns them only to humans. Both carry “Division of VPC” in visible copy, and InterActive Play publishes VPC’s own main number, 585-735-9668. Neither states any of it in markup, so a machine reading either site finds no parent company, no address and no telephone.
  8. The blog stopped in July 2022. The post sitemap’s most recent entry is 5 July 2022. The rest of the site is not frozen: page entries carry modification dates as recent as 28 August 2026, and the case studies were touched on 25 August. It is the publishing habit that lapsed, not the maintenance.
  9. None of it is measurable. No Bing verification on any of the three domains, and we hold no Search Console access. Every statement about assistant visibility in our documents is currently being taken against an index nobody at VPC can see into.

04

The questions, sorted by who can actually answer them

Nothing below is rhetorical. Each one changes what the right next step is, and none of them can be answered from outside the company.

For the web vendor

  • Can Product markup be added to the Navigator templates on products.vpcfiberglass.com, and at what cost? This is the single highest-value unanswered question in the estate.
  • If not, can VPC publish a crawlable, marked-up version of the catalog on a domain it controls?
  • If VPC ever moves, does the product data export, and in what format?
  • Who holds the WordPress administrator credentials, and can VPC hold its own set?
  • What has the current arrangement covered over the last twenty-four months, and what does it cost?
  • Two specific defects: one of the catalog’s search forms posts to a staging hostname and returns a 404, and the catalog’s robots file opens with an invalid user-agent token.

For VPC

  • What are the real revenue and headcount figures? Needed before anyone edits the directory record. We are not assuming the published numbers are wrong, only that they should be looked at.
  • Does VPC hold any quality certification it is not publishing? We searched all 23 pages on 6 September and found no ISO 9001, ASME RTP-1, NSF, AWWA, ANSI, UL or AISC. For contrast, Augusta Fiberglass publishes ASME RTP-1 fabricator in its own directory copy. If VPC holds one, it is invisible everywhere. If it does not, that is a competitive fact worth knowing rather than a marketing problem.
  • Does a specification name VPC before it goes out to bid, or a competitor with “or approved equal” after? This decides whether the traditional site should be a specification writer’s toolkit or a lead generation site. They are two different builds. Asked on 14 August and still open.
  • Is the catalog configurator used internally, and does it hold real sizes and options? If it does, VPC may already own a structured description of its own catalog-shaped products.

What those answers decide: the catalog fork, whether the traditional site is built for specifiers or for buyers, and whether the aquatic side needs a structure of its own.

05

The sequence

Ordered so that everything requiring no budget and no vendor happens before the one decision that requires both.

  1. Correct VPC’s own record on the directoryRevenue, headcount, primary company type, the categories that do not belong, and BABA, which we did not see in the parts of the profile we read. The profile is claimed and carries a “Suggest an Edit” control. About an hour, and it corrects the page VPC’s own structured data tells search engines to trust. Two fields need an answer from inside VPC first.
  2. Do the day of work that needs only a loginRoughly fifteen items across the main site and the two satellites: the social sharing tags, the author archives publishing a vendor admin username, a meta description on /locations/, three misrouted redirects, identity markup on EasyRec and InterActive Play, the broken heading structure on the EasyRec stair page, and the Product markup pack that is already built and waiting. None of this needs the vendor. It needs WordPress credentials.
  3. Get measurement in placeVerify all three domains in Bing Webmaster Tools, one meta tag each. ChatGPT search runs on Bing’s index rather than Google’s, so without this every claim about assistant visibility stays an argument instead of a number.
  4. Then decide the catalogIf the templates can be edited, this is the largest return available anywhere and it is contained: the names, descriptions, images and per-product attributes are already written, already on the page and already partly marked up. The work is to clean what is emitted, so the product name is the product rather than the breadcrumb, and to add JSON-LD alongside the existing microdata. If they cannot be edited, the honest answer is that the product truth has to move onto a domain VPC controls, and that is a project with a budget rather than a ticket.
  5. Separate the two businessesTwo doors in the navigation, then product-named hubs a buyer would actually type. This is the item that converts the technical work into traffic.
  6. Only then evaluate paid placementDirectory advertising decides where VPC ranks among several hundred suppliers in a category. It does not decide what the record says about VPC. Do not buy visibility while the record still reads as a nine-person company. Fix step one, get step three measuring, then judge the spend on what it returns.

06

What we are not recommending, and why

Not a rebuild. The site is fast, correctly canonicalised, structurally sound and accessible. A rebuild spends money to arrive roughly where VPC already is and discards years of accumulated authority in the process. The findings here are structure and data, not construction.

Not a change of vendor. Thomas built something competent and the evidence is in section 02. The observation worth making is structural rather than personal: one vendor currently holds three roles, as the builder of the site, the landlord of the catalog, and the seller of directory placement. Those roles have different interests. A landlord has no particular reason to make a tenant’s data portable, and a placement product is not the answer to a data problem. The owner’s job is not to fire anyone. It is to know which of the three is speaking when advice arrives, and to ask the questions in section 04 plainly.

The only fair test of any vendor is whether the assets get more valuable or more rented over time. That is a question to put to them, with the record in front of both parties, rather than a conclusion to reach without them.

07

How this was checked

  • All four websites were pulled live on 6 September 2026: status codes, robots files, sitemaps, the structured data parsed out of the served pages rather than guessed at, and the page source read for platform fingerprints. The main site’s 23 pages were fetched in full and searched with word-boundary matching, because a plain search returns false positives inside stylesheet keywords. Catalog item pages were sampled and parsed for both JSON-LD and microdata, after an earlier pass looked only for JSON-LD and wrongly reported the catalog as carrying no structured data.
  • The directory profile was read visually in a browser, because thomasnet.com answers automated requests with a captcha. The figures quoted from it are transcribed from that page and have not been independently corroborated.
  • VPC does appear in the browsable category listings. Confirmed on 6 September: in the FRP Shelters category, 609 suppliers, VPC sits around eighth on the first page, unpaid, below two sponsored entries. So the directory listing is doing real work and this document should not be read as an argument against it.
  • Revenue, headcount and certifications are reported here as what the record says, not as corrected facts. They need an answer from inside VPC before anything is edited. An earlier version of this page treated the size figures as evidence VPC was being misrepresented; on checking the category listing, competitors including the top sponsored result carry the same values, so they read as unpopulated rather than wrong.
