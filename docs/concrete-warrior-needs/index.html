Credentials and paper

What procurement verifies before a supplier gets named

Estimators and procurement officers check material on paper before it goes into a bid. No supplier in this market publishes any of it, which is why the site has a credentials section at all. One row of five is confirmed today.

CredentialStatusWhat we need
MHA TERO license Confirmed Nothing. Confirmed by Breanna on 6 August. It is a license, not preference standing, and the site says so.
MSHA Part 46 Unconfirmed The written training plan, the named competent person, and confirmation that new miner and annual refresher training is running. Mandatory for sand and gravel operations, so a buyer assumes it exists and will ask for the plan.
NDDA weed-free, NDCC 4.1-47 Unconfirmed Which certification Richard actually holds. He holds something. Nobody here can say which, and source inspection is written into reclamation specifications.
NDDOT approved source Unconfirmed The Pit ID. Without it the pit is not listed on the state register as far as any reader can tell.
Buyer prequalification Not registered A decision on which platform first, then the registration itself. This is the biggest gap on the list and it is not a website problem.

The gate that sits ahead of the quote

Most operators in the basin will not open a supplier account until you are on the prequalification platform they use. ISNetworld has the widest coverage among Bakken operators. Veriforce dominates pipeline and DOT-regulated work. Avetta is common across construction and mining. Picking one is a business decision with a real cost attached, and it belongs to Richard and Corey rather than to us.

Reservation standing carries some work without it. It does not carry the operator work, and the operator work is the volume.

Seven slots are empty and render as a marked box. The full brief went out on 29 August; this is the same list, compressed.

Empty on the site right now

  • Class 5 base stockpile, shot tight enough to read the gradation.
  • Class 13 base stockpile, same.
  • A mixer truck discharging on a real pour. There is not one photograph of a mixer truck in any library we hold. Single biggest hole on the site.
  • The trommel running, dirt moving through the drum. Topsoil is the largest thing the yard produces and there is no picture of it being made.
  • A person at work, operator in the cab or somebody on the ground in a hard hat. The site has zero faces on it.
  • A second crew or task shot, so the About page is not carrying one lonely portrait.

Replacing the Texas stand-ins

  • The crusher running at New Town. Currently a Texas crushing and screening spread.
  • A wide establishing shot of the New Town yard. Stockpiles, haul road, the whole operation in one frame.
  • The screened topsoil stockpile at New Town. Currently a Texas pile.
  • The washed rock stockpile. Currently rock in a Texas loader bucket.

Not a website slot, but they unblock the launch

  • The yard sign or entrance gate. This one gates the Google listing. A profile with no exterior photograph of the location ranks worse and reads as unverified to a buyer checking you out.
  • A truck door, close enough to read the decal. It also settles a question we cannot answer from Colorado: whether Texas iron is running north still wearing Patriot livery.
  • The scale house or yard building, whatever a customer sees pulling in. Buyers want to see a staffed yard rather than a field.
  • A loader filling a customer's truck. The picture most producers lead with.
  • One clean horizontal frame we can crop for the social share card, the image that appears when somebody texts the link.

Upgrades and depth, if the camera is already out

None of these block anything and all of them get used: an aerial of the yard for the hero, the crusher with rubble actually feeding into it, the batch plant running, concrete removal in progress, topsoil being spread at a well site, a before and after pair from one reclamation job, the pit with an open working face, a loaded haul truck leaving, the equipment lineup, a tight close-up of roughly a square foot of each material, and anything at all from winter or early season work.

How to send them

Originals only. Not screenshots, not saved out of a text message. Both strip resolution and the date and location data we use to sort them. iPhone HEIC files are fine exactly as they come off the phone. AirDrop, Dropbox, Drive or a shared album all work.

Label each batch on the way in: which yard, roughly when, who shot it. That context is not recoverable later. We already have a good rock pile photograph we cannot use because nobody can say what it is or where it was taken.

One safety pass before it leaves your hands. Anything showing a person without a hard hat cannot go on a site courting ISNetworld and MSHA Part 46 buyers. Those buyers read photographs as evidence of how the operation is run.

05

Google Business Profile

The one thing on this list that pays off before the website does

Type "gravel near New Town" into a phone and the map results sit above the website results. A business with no profile is not in that list at all, and no amount of work on the website changes that.

The listing is a separate channel from organic search and a faster one. It is also the cheapest item in the entire project: the profile is free, and the mailbox underneath it runs about $170 a year. It is what gets the call from somebody standing in a pit at seven in the morning, and it is the reason to finish this before anything else on the page.

What is already done

  • Google Workspace has been live since 24 August and admin@concretewarriorsupply.com is the super admin role account. What is not confirmed is office@, which is meant to own the profile, and the quotes@ and info@ aliases the website prints as its contact addresses. One test email to quotes@ settles it, and it is question 1 in the playbook.
  • The field has been checked in a browser rather than guessed. There is no Concrete Warrior profile in New Town and Google has not auto-generated one, so this is a build from zero, which is the easy case.
  • The neighbors are known. Strata sits at 8790 39th St NW and its listing is unclaimed. West Dakota Ready Mix is a permanently closed pin. That is the entire field of concrete suppliers Google returns for New Town.

What is still needed to get it over the line

  • The phone number. Landed 3 Sep (701) 627-2200. Enter it exactly as written, because the profile and the site have to agree character for character.
  • Whether a sign exists at the yard, and its exact wording. The sign and the truck magnets together decide the listing name, and Google checks the name against what a verifier can see on the ground.
  • Yard hours, and whether it is staffed during them. Same item as the site needs, used twice.
  • The material list with correct names, which becomes the services list on the profile and is a real ranking input.
  • The verification video, recorded at the yard. It has to be filmed through a link Google generates after the profile is submitted, so nothing already shot can be sent in as it. Someone at New Town walks the yard on a phone when Breanna texts the link.
  • Exterior and yard photographs from section 04, particularly the sign, the building and the loader working.
  • The pin dragged to the real entrance. Google's current panel for 9702 ND-23 carries the warning "the location shown is not precise," because it is interpolated from the road rather than surveyed.

Why the verification video carries so much weight here

Google has no business record at that address today. There is no street imagery of a yard, no interpolated listing to correct, nothing to lean on. That means the video is doing the entire job of proving a real operation stands there. A sign in frame, a building, iron working and a person on the ground is the difference between verified in days and a rejection loop that can take weeks to get out of.

The name will not carry this listing, so the rest has to

Search "Concrete Warrior New Town ND" today and Google returns Warrior Concrete LLC, the words reversed, a claimed service-area contractor with an 801 number out of Utah. Widen it and there is a whole field of Warrior-something concrete companies. None of them are in North Dakota, so none of them block the filing. What it means is that this profile has to win on categories, on distance from the searcher and on completeness, never on the name. Every unanswered field is a place where a thinner competitor gets picked instead.

The step-by-step version, including who does what and the nine things not to do, is in the Google Business Profile playbook. Nothing in that document has changed, and it is no longer waiting on the phone number. Breanna can start it whenever she has twenty minutes.
