The Google listing

Verified first time, with no existing Google record at that address to lean on. Wired to the site the same day.

The name is settled. The business filed as Concrete Warrior Supply, which the logo and domain already said. The website was the one place still reading "Concrete Warrior," so the website was changed to match the filing. The name is the single field on a Google listing that is hardest to fix later, and it now agrees across the filing, the logo, the domain, the site and the listing.

Underneath the website, the parts that decide whether Google and an AI assistant can read it: the www version redirects to one address instead of splitting the credit between two; a machine-readable record of the business carrying the address, phone, hours and the four counties served; ten answered questions marked up as an FAQ, generated from the same source as the visible words so the two cannot drift apart; robots and sitemap files serving correctly with nothing accidentally hidden; an llms.txt file, which is how you tell an AI assistant what a business does in a form it can quote. The phone number is identical in all of them and identical to the listing, which is one of the things Google actually checks.

On the listing, since verification: the website linked, the 750-character description in, ten products and services entries in, and the service area set to Dunn, McKenzie, Williams and Mountrail, matching the site. Google retired its questions and answers section in December 2025 and replaced it with Ask Maps, which answers from the profile, the website and the reviews, so the eight answers written for it became profile updates and the three the website did not already cover were added to its FAQ.

Email runs on the domain: Workspace since 24 August, with quotes@ and info@ receiving. A richard@ address is available on the same domain at no extra cost whenever Richard wants one.

One gap, and it is ours to close

There is no Google Search Console property for concretewarriorsupply.com yet. The site is indexable and the sitemap is live, but nobody has submitted it to Google or can see whether Google has actually indexed the pages. The domain and its DNS both sit on our side until the handover, so this is our job rather than an ask, and we are doing it. It is the difference between assuming the site is in Google and knowing it.

The three that matter now

In order. None of them can be done from a keyboard in Colorado

1

Reviews on the Google listing

The listing is complete and has none. When Google has two suppliers the same distance from somebody searching, reviews decide which one it shows first, and then they decide which one gets the call. Four or five would change the picture.

The ask that works is a phone call to somebody who has already taken a load and was happy with it, not a mass email. It is worth more than the next month of work on the website. Owner: Richard and Amanda.

2

Photographs from New Town

The listing shows one image today and it is one Google found, not one of ours. The website still carries four stand-ins from the Texas operation, tagged as placeholders so no reader is misled, and seven slots are empty.

Ranked, so a partial batch still lands the right frames: the yard sign or entrance · a wide shot of the yard · the crusher running with rubble feeding in · a mixer truck discharging on a real pour · the trommel screening topsoil · a loader filling a customer's truck · somebody at work in a hard hat · scoria, Class 5 and Class 13 stockpiles.

Originals off the phone, not screenshots, and label each batch with which yard and roughly when. Hard hats on anyone in frame, because the buyers this site is aimed at read photographs as evidence of how an operation is run. The full brief went out 29 August and has not changed. Owner: Amanda.

3

The USDOT number for the truck magnets

The artwork is drawn, proofed and ready to print, held on one number. The rule is that the operating carrier's name and its USDOT number have to be readable at fifty feet, and nobody has confirmed which number belongs on these trucks. Patriot holds 4232633 and Patriot is a Texas entity. The line is already reserved in the artwork, so it is one change and then straight to print. We will not print a borrowed number.

Worth settling in the same breath: are the truck doors steel? Ford has built aluminum bodies since 2015 and a magnet will not hold on one. The same artwork prints as stick-on vinyl, so the answer changes the order rather than the files. Owner: Richard or Corey.

04

Decisions that sit with Corey

Three calls, none of which we can make

  • Terms actually offered, and to whom. Oil field work runs on terms, and what a supplier will carry decides who gets asked as often as price does. No supplier in this market says anything about it on a website, which makes it an easy advantage and an unusable one until somebody states the policy.
  • Buyer prequalification, and which platform first. ISNetworld has the widest coverage among Bakken operators, Veriforce dominates pipeline and DOT-regulated work, Avetta is common across construction and mining. Reservation standing carries some work without any of them; it does not carry the operator work, and the operator work is the volume. It is off the website entirely until there is a registration to point at, because an estimator reads an unanswered prequalification question as a no.
  • Domain handover to Corey's organization whenever he wants it. The transfer lock lifts 13 October.
