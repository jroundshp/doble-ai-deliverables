What is already built and running

Written down because most of it is invisible, and invisible work is the work people assume did not happen

Two things a buyer can see: a website on its own domain, and a verified Google listing that points at it. Underneath those sit a set of decisions and plumbing that do not show up in a screenshot and are most of the reason the first two work at all.

The website

concretewarriorsupply.com is live on the apex domain and open to search. Before 10 September the domain returned an error. Four pages are published and in the sitemap: the homepage, reclamation topsoil, ready-mix concrete and the about page. Every page is pre-rendered as static HTML rather than assembled in the browser, which is why it loads fast on a phone in a pit with two bars of signal, and why a search crawler and an AI assistant both get the full text on the first request instead of an empty shell.

Under the hood, in plain terms

  • One address, not two. The www version now redirects to the apex with a permanent 308. Before, both answered separately, which quietly splits the credit for everything the site earns between two addresses instead of building it up in one. Canonical tags on every page say the same thing.
  • The site tells Google what kind of business this is, in machine-readable form. A LocalBusiness record carrying the name, the address at 9702 ND-23, the phone, the opening hours as structured data, and the four counties served. A WebSite record alongside it. This is the layer that decides whether a search engine understands the page or merely reads it.
  • Ten answered questions marked up as an FAQ. Seven at launch, three added on 11 September. The markup is generated from the same source as the visible words, so the two cannot drift apart, which is the usual way this kind of markup turns into a penalty rather than a benefit.
  • robots.txt and sitemap.xml are live and correct, and the search block that sat on the preview has been lifted on every layer. Nothing is accidentally hidden.
  • An llms.txt file, which is the emerging convention for telling an AI assistant what a business does in a form it can quote. Most competitors in this market do not have a website at all, let alone this.
  • The phone number appears identically everywhere: header, footer, quote section, topsoil page, the structured data and llms.txt. Identical strings across the site and the listing is one of the things Google actually checks.
  • Email is live on the domain. Google Workspace since 24 August, with quotes@ and info@ confirmed receiving on 4 September, and admin@ as the super admin account. A richard@ address is available on the same domain whenever Richard wants one, at no extra cost.

The name, which was the decision with the longest tail

The business filed as Concrete Warrior Supply. The logo and the domain already said that; the website was the one place still reading "Concrete Warrior," so the website was changed to match the filing rather than the other way round. That matters more than it sounds: the name on a Google listing is the single field that is hardest to correct after the fact, and it now agrees across the filing, the logo, the domain, the website and the listing.

The Google Business Profile, up and wired to the site

  • Created and verified on 10 September by Breanna. That is the slowest and least predictable step in the whole project, and there was no existing Google record at that address to lean on, so the verification did the entire job of proving a real operation stands there.
  • Wired to the website the same day. A verified listing and a matching website are treated as evidence for each other, so the pairing is worth more than either piece alone.
  • Filed as a storefront with the address showing, at 9702 ND-23, with the category set to ready mix concrete supplier and hours of Monday to Friday 8 to 5, Saturday 8 to 12, closed Sunday.
  • The 750-character description and the products and services list went in on 11 September. Those two are the half of a profile that decides how it performs, as distinct from whether it merely exists.
  • The service area now covers Dunn, McKenzie, Williams and Mountrail, which is the same footprint the website claims. Before that the listing was describing the business as being in McKenzie County while the yard sits in Mountrail.
  • The questions and answers moved rather than died. Google retired that section in December 2025 and replaced it with Ask Maps, an AI feature that answers from the profile, the website and the reviews. The eight answers written for the listing were rewritten as profile updates, and the three the website did not already cover were added to the FAQ there. The work survived the feature being switched off.

Written down, so nobody has to hold it in their head

A build spec for the site, a Google Business Profile playbook with the step-by-step and the nine things not to do, a market and seasonality read, a sales and pipeline brief, a launch brief, a photo brief, and this punch list. All of them live at links that stay current rather than in an email thread. The logo and the print artwork were built as a parametric type system, which is why a phone number could be added to the truck magnets in the right weight and spacing without redrawing anything.

What none of this does

It does not get the business reviewed, photographed or specified. A complete, fast, correctly marked-up website with a verified listing is the floor rather than the ceiling. It is what makes the business findable and legible. What makes it chosen is reviews, photographs of the real yard, and numbers an estimator can check, and all three of those live at New Town. That is what the rest of this page is about.

Credentials and paper

What procurement verifies before a supplier gets named

Estimators and procurement officers check material on paper before it goes into a bid. No supplier in this market publishes any of it, which is why the site has a credentials section at all. One row of five is confirmed today.

CredentialStatusWhat we need
MHA TERO license Confirmed Nothing. Confirmed by Breanna on 6 August. It is a license, not preference standing, and the site says so.
MSHA Part 46 Unconfirmed The written training plan, the named competent person, and confirmation that new miner and annual refresher training is running. Mandatory for sand and gravel operations, so a buyer assumes it exists and will ask for the plan.

Prequalification: important to the business, and not something we are waiting on

Most operators in the basin will not open a supplier account until you are on the prequalification platform they use. ISNetworld has the widest coverage among Bakken operators, Veriforce dominates pipeline and DOT-regulated work, and Avetta is common across construction and mining. Reservation standing carries some work without any of them. It does not carry the operator work, and the operator work is the volume, so this has real commercial weight and we are not treating it as optional.

The plan, as we understand it today: launch without it. Registration costs money and takes time, and picking a platform is a business decision that belongs with the owners rather than with us. So it comes in as soon as possible, or whenever it makes business sense, rather than holding up a website and a Google listing that are ready now.

It is also off the website entirely as of 3 September. The homepage used to name all three platforms and say plainly that none were set up, and the answers section asked the question and left it blank. Both are gone. An estimator reads an unanswered prequalification question as a no, and there is no reason to volunteer that before anyone has asked. The section goes back on the site the day there is a registration to point at, and it is a five-minute change when that happens.

  • A person at work, operator in the cab or somebody on the ground in a hard hat. The site has zero faces on it.
  • A second crew or task shot, so the About page is not carrying one lonely portrait.

Replacing the Texas stand-ins

  • The crusher running at New Town. Currently a Texas crushing and screening spread.
  • A wide establishing shot of the New Town yard. Stockpiles, haul road, the whole operation in one frame.
  • The screened topsoil stockpile at New Town. Currently a Texas pile.
  • The washed rock stockpile. Currently rock in a Texas loader bucket.

Not a website slot, but they unblock the launch

  • The yard sign or entrance gate. This one gates the Google listing. A profile with no exterior photograph of the location ranks worse and reads as unverified to a buyer checking you out.
  • A truck door, close enough to read the decal. It also settles a question we cannot answer from Colorado: whether Texas iron is running north still wearing Patriot livery.
  • The scale house or yard building, whatever a customer sees pulling in. Buyers want to see a staffed yard rather than a field.
  • A loader filling a customer's truck. The picture most producers lead with.
  • One clean horizontal frame we can crop for the social share card, the image that appears when somebody texts the link.

Upgrades and depth, if the camera is already out

None of these block anything and all of them get used: an aerial of the yard for the hero, the crusher with rubble actually feeding into it, the batch plant running, concrete removal in progress, topsoil being spread at a well site, a before and after pair from one reclamation job, the pit with an open working face, a loaded haul truck leaving, the equipment lineup, a tight close-up of roughly a square foot of each material, and anything at all from winter or early season work.

How to send them

Originals only. Not screenshots, not saved out of a text message. Both strip resolution and the date and location data we use to sort them. iPhone HEIC files are fine exactly as they come off the phone. AirDrop, Dropbox, Drive or a shared album all work.

Label each batch on the way in: which yard, roughly when, who shot it. That context is not recoverable later. We already have a good rock pile photograph we cannot use because nobody can say what it is or where it was taken.

One safety pass before it leaves your hands. Anything showing a person without a hard hat cannot go on a site courting ISNetworld and MSHA Part 46 buyers. Those buyers read photographs as evidence of how the operation is run.
