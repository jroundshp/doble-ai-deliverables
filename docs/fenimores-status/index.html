Fenimore's Table, where things stand

The kitchen has moved quickly. In the last week Fenimore's has published a breakfast service, a lunch card and a full dinner card, and the website has taken on all three without changing anything about how a search engine or an assistant reads them. This is a snapshot of both halves on one page.

For Corey Hogan and the Cooperstown side. Internal only, not a public page.
Every figure below was checked against the live site and Google on 9 September 2026. Nothing is carried forward from the July audit without a fresh look.
Menu content is the kitchen's call and this readout takes no position on it. What follows is about whether the website and the profiles say the same thing the kitchen is actually serving.
For the detail behind any of this: the Fenimore's Table site audit grades the whole digital presence channel by channel, and the dinner card readout of 2 September carries the menu comparison this page summarises.

01

The short version

Three menus are live where there was one. A breakfast service now opens at 6:30AM, which is a genuinely new daypart and the most interesting thing on this page. The site publishes all of it as readable text, which is worth more than it sounds.

What has not moved is the layer underneath. There is still no structured data on any page, no meta descriptions and no Open Graph tags, and the sitemap declared in robots.txt does not resolve. The gap between what the restaurant is doing and what a machine can see about it is wider now than it was in July, because there is more to see and the same amount of it is readable.

04

What a search engine and an assistant can actually read

Working

The menu is real HTML text, with every price next to its dish. This is the single most valuable thing on the site. It is why this comparison was possible at all, and it is what most restaurant sites lose by posting the menu as a PDF or an image. Whatever the new card looks like on paper, the web version needs to stay text.

Working

The Google profile is claimed, current on hours, and linked to the website.

Open

No structured data on any page. Zero JSON-LD on the homepage, the menu page, the breakfast page and the drinks page. No Restaurant, no Menu, no MenuItem, and no address, hours or phone in markup, even though all three sit in the page text.

Open

The declared sitemap does not resolve. robots.txt points at wp-sitemap.xml, which returns 404, as do /sitemap.xml and /sitemap_index.xml.

Open

No meta description and no Open Graph tags on any of the four pages checked. Shared in a text message or on Facebook, the link renders without a title card. The homepage title is the business name plus the full tagline sentence, which Google cuts off well before the end.

Open

The menu page now carries three h1 headings: Menu, Lunch Menu and Dinner Menu. One page gets one. This is a Divi setting rather than a rebuild, and it is worth doing in the same pass as the schema.

Improved

The dinner section is now a real heading. Entrees reads as an h3, level with Starters and Sandwiches. The “after 5pm” qualifier beside it is still plain text, so nothing machine-readable ties the section to the time it applies.

Unanswered

/brunch/ still returns 404, and the word brunch does not appear anywhere on the site. What was built is breakfast. Those are two different searches, and the site currently answers one of them. Worth a decision either way rather than leaving the old URL dead.

Where the menus sit against the neighbours

The competitive set is Corey's and was settled in the dinner readout. Nothing in the dinner comparison has changed: the $37 New York strip sits below Upstate at $40 and Mel's at $38 and above Council Rock at $27.95, with 1909 at the Otesaga in a different tier entirely at $48 to $140. Five formats appeared on none of the cards we could read, and four of them are on the proposed card.

Breakfast opens a daypart the set was never built for. The comparison work was done on dinner. A 6:30AM service on the Route 28 corridor is a different fight, and it is one where the location reads as an advantage rather than the drawback it is at dinner: families heading to the tournament complexes pass the door before the village is awake.

Two consequences worth naming. Stagecoach Coffee was filed in the dinner readout as running against lunch rather than dinner; at 6:30am to 6pm it is now a direct breakfast comparison. And the Main Street breakfast rooms overlap a service they were not graded against.

What we have not checked is when any of them open. No claim that Fenimore's has the earliest door in Cooperstown is supported yet. That is one morning of verification and it should happen before the early slot is used in any marketing.

06

What to do next, in order

  1. Settle the names and the prices

    One name each for the blue cheese chips and the hot honey pepperoni, one price for the wings and one for the banana bread, across the printed card, the website and Toast. Everything below this line depends on it, because markup that disagrees with the page is worse than no markup.

  2. Fix the headings

    One h1 on the menu page, and a real heading for the after-5 section so a machine can see where dinner starts. A Divi setting, not a build.

  3. Mark up the restaurant, then the menu

    Restaurant on the homepage carrying the address, phone, current hours and the reservation link, then Menu with a section per group and an item per dish, each with its name, description and price. This is the step that turns “where can I get schnitzel near Cooperstown” into a question the site can answer.

  4. The three small ones

    Make the sitemap in robots.txt resolve, write meta descriptions for a person rather than a keyword, add Open Graph tags so shared links render properly. Worth doing while somebody is already in the site.

  5. Then an llms.txt, and not before

    There is none today. It is only worth publishing once the menu page underneath it is stable and the markup matches, otherwise it states the confusion more confidently. Twenty minutes, after the five above.
