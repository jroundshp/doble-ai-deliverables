07

How an inquiry actually reaches a person

This part is invisible on the page and it is where event inquiries are most often lost, so it is worth being direct about it.

The website's current contact form has three fields and sends an email to restaurant@cooperstownexperience.com. It keeps no copy of anything. For a question about opening hours that is fine. For a wedding inquiry it means that if the message is filtered, misread or missed, there is no record it ever arrived and no way to know it happened.

Four things fix it, all inside the current arrangement and all done from our side:

  1. Every submission stored on the website itself

    A dated, searchable list of every inquiry, exportable to a spreadsheet, independent of anybody's inbox. Email becomes the notification rather than the record.

  2. Notifications sent through an authenticated mail route

    Form mail sent the default way from a web host is filtered often, and silently. This is the single most common reason a restaurant never hears about an inquiry that was made.

  3. An automatic reply to the guest, within seconds

    Confirming what they sent and roughly when somebody will call. Costs nothing and is the difference between a lead who waits and a lead who sends the same form to the next venue.

  4. One named person who owns the inbox, and one backup

    Inquiries sent to everybody get answered by nobody. This is a name, not a system, and it is the only item on this list that we cannot put in place from our end.

08

What we need back

Answers to these can come as a reply to the email this arrived with. Nothing here needs a meeting, and photographs can follow after the pages are live.

  1. Capacity. How many seated, and how many for a standing reception? Is there a separate private room, or is it the full dining room? This is the first question every inquiry asks and the pages cannot go live without it.
  2. Buyouts. Is a full restaurant buyout available, and on which days or times? Say no if it is no.
  3. Booking terms. Minimum notice you want, whether there is a deposit, and whether there is a food and beverage minimum. Rough is fine.
  4. Two small ones. Can guests bring their own cake, and is there a fee? And roughly how many parking spaces are on site?
  5. Who owns an inquiry. One name, one backup, and the email address the notifications should go to.
  6. Brunch. Is there a walk in brunch on weekends, or is brunch private events only? This decides whether /brunch/ becomes a real page or a redirect. Either is fine, but right now it is a dead link and answers nothing.
  7. The copy in §03. Approve it, or mark it up. Changes to dish names, package descriptions and anything about the food are yours to make and we will publish exactly what comes back.
  8. Photographs. Six to ten real pictures: the dining room set for a party, a table dressed, a plated dish, the bar. Phone photographs are completely fine. We will not use stock or AI generated imagery on this page.

09

What happens after the answers

First part of next week is achievable. The build itself is about a day.

  • Both pages built in the site's existing design, published unlisted first so the group can look at them before anything is public.
  • Links added from the homepage, the menu page, the breakfast page and contact, so the events page is reachable from anywhere on the site.
  • Page titles, descriptions and social preview cards written, and both pages added to the sitemap so Google finds them.
  • The questions section marked up so assistants can quote the answers directly.
  • The form built, stored, tested end to end with a real submission, and confirmed to arrive.
  • The Google Business Profile updated to point at the events page, which will reach people faster in the first weeks than the page will on its own.

Of that list, only the photographs and the capacity number are outside our hands. Everything else runs from here.

Worth being honest about what a web page will and will not do in week one. A new page does not rank quickly. On its own it will take weeks to start turning up for someone searching for a Cooperstown venue, and the first bookings will almost certainly come from the Google profile, the banners Bob has coming, and people who already know Fenimore's. The page is what makes all of those land somewhere. It is worth building now and it is not the thing that fills the room in October.
