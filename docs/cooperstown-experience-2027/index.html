Answer the questions families actually ask

1 to 2 hoursWebsite, with answers from Corey
Why it matters

When a parent asks an AI assistant "what youth baseball tournaments are there in Cooperstown," the answer is built from pages that respond to exactly that kind of question in plain sentences. Clear written questions and answers are one of the most effective things you can put on your own site to get named in those answers. The site has four right now, and much of what a coach wants to know is scattered across other pages or not written down at all.

The correction, if you agree

Add these to the For Parents page, each as a question heading with a short answer below it:

  • What age groups do you offer? (12U and 13U boys' baseball, 16U and 18U girls' softball)
  • Where is the complex, and how far is it from the Hall of Fame?
  • How many games will my team play? (At least seven, weather permitting)
  • What's included in the price?
  • Does my team have to bring an umpire?
  • Where do families stay? (Link to the Lodging page)
  • Where do teams eat? (The Clubhouse and Fenimore's Table, on the grounds)
  • What happens if it rains?
  • What does a tournament week look like? (The day-by-day list on the schedule page)

Most of these answers are already on other pages, so this is mostly moving them onto one page. We can draft the text if that would help.

The Google listing, Bing, and image descriptions

About an hourWhoever manages the Google listing
What we found

Google Business Profile. As of September 21, the listing has one review, rated 1 star, which is the rating anyone sees when they search the name. It has no hours (Google shows "Add hours" to visitors) and four photos. The address, phone and website are all correct.

Bing. We found no sign the site has been added to Bing Webmaster Tools. ChatGPT's web search draws on Bing, so this matters more than it used to.

Image descriptions. 95 images across the site have no alt text, the short description that screen readers read aloud and search engines use. Many of these are the same logo and banner repeated on every page, so the number of distinct images is much smaller.

The correction, if you agree
  1. On the Google listing, add office hours (or tournament-season hours) and the real photos from item 6.
  2. Once teams have played, ask a few of their coaches for a Google review. With only one review on the listing, a handful of new ones would change the rating people see. Until then, the photos and hours are the part you can fix.
  3. Add the site to Bing Webmaster Tools. It can import the site straight from Google Search Console if the site is already set up there.
  4. Add a short description to each image when you're already editing a page for the items above. Something plain like "Championship game at the Cooperstown Experience stadium field" is enough.
