Cooperstown Experience · Website Direction

What the site is today, and what it could be now.

For the next several months, the people most likely to open cooperstownexperience.com aren't coaches booking a week. They're banks that might finance the complex, investors and potential partners. The opening season also isn't set yet, so a site built around dates and prices is working against itself right now.

This page puts the site as it stands next to a recommendation for what it could become in the meantime. It's written for Corey and Amanda to look at together and decide whether this is the direction they want. Nothing has been changed on the site.

The short version

Today the site is a tournament booking site for a season that hasn't opened, and much of it still describes 2026 as the season coming up.

The recommendation is to keep the same site and the same address, but lead with the project: what's being built, what's already built, who's behind it and how to get involved. The tournaments become the plan, described without dates or prices. Coaches can still add their team to the no-cost Pencil Me In list.

Either way, a short list of corrections is worth making now (step 1 below), because they're wrong whichever direction is chosen.

2. What it could be now

Same site, same address, same SportsEngine account, with the order turned around: the project first, the tournaments second. Six sections.

A

Home: the vision

One clear paragraph on what Cooperstown Experience is going to be, a strip showing what's already built and what's under way, and two buttons: "Partner with us" first and "Coaches: pencil your team in" second. No schedule table, no prices.

A starting point for the wording, using only what's already on the site:

"Cooperstown Experience is a baseball and softball tournament complex being built three miles south of Cooperstown, New York, for boys' baseball and girls' fast-pitch softball, with championship ceremonies at the National Baseball Hall of Fame. Town approvals are in place, field construction is under way, and two restaurants and an arcade are already built on the grounds."

B

The Project

This builds on the current Complex Development page and becomes the most important page on the site. It covers the site-plan video, the approvals and permits already listed there, a progress timeline with dates, and a new photo from the same spot each month. For a bank, a dated record of steady progress says more than anything else on the site.

How much of the longer-term plan for the property to show here is Corey's call.

C

The Program (planned)

The boys' and girls' programs and the two special event weeks, written as the plan: the age groups, the week-long format, what each team gets and the Hall of Fame elements. No dates, prices or payment schedules. It closes with "Tournament dates and pricing will be announced here. Pencil your team in to hear first."

This keeps what makes the program distinctive in front of partners, without committing to a season that isn't set.

D

Who We Are

About Us and Our Team combined: Corey, Bob and anyone else who should be named, with a photo and two or three lines each. For an investor or a lender, this is often the first page they open, and today it has nobody on it.

E

Partner With Us

A new page. It says what kinds of involvement are open (sponsorship, partnership, investment or financing), gives a named person to contact with a direct email and phone, and links to The Project. It doesn't need financial figures. Those belong in a private conversation, not on a public page.

F

Visit Now

What someone can do on the grounds today: Fenimore's Table, the Cooperstown Clubhouse and the arcade, plus lodging and directions. It should say the Clubhouse is closed for the season and reopens in spring 2027, as its own website does, and link to each restaurant's site for hours rather than repeating them, so this page can't fall out of date.

What happens to each existing page

Nothing is deleted. Pages that only matter once there's a season are hidden from the menu, so they can come back unchanged when the date is set.

Three things for Corey to decide first

1. Keep the financial details off the public site. A bank or investor will want numbers: budget, phases, financing needed, projected revenue. Those belong in a private brief sent only to the people you choose, not on a website anyone can read. The site's job is to make the project look real, current and well run.

2. Confirm the Hall of Fame arrangement is still in place. The site promises every player and coach a Hall of Fame membership, a championship ceremony inside the Museum and a private artifact spotlight for coaches. A coach reads that as a perk. A lender reads it as a claim about the business. Before the site leads with it, it's worth confirming it's still current.

3. Who is the contact for partners and investors? The Partner With Us page needs one named person with a direct line.
