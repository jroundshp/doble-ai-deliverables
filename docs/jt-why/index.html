What the website is for

A fair question came up on Monday: if the site isn't producing leads, what's the point? Here is the answer, and what we think it should produce from here.

Prepared for: John Tyler By: Eagle River Base Camp / Doble AI Date: September 29, 2026
01

The leaders run two platforms

The top producers in Vail and Aspen don't pick between the brokerage and their own site. They run both, and each one does a different job.

The brokerage site

vailrealestate.com

  • Listings, IDX search and the firm's reach
  • The Slifer name and its reputation
  • The same template for every broker at the firm
  • Stays with the firm
The platform you own

johntylerrealestate.com + social

  • Your voice, your writing, your point of view
  • Your analytics: who comes, from where, to read what
  • English and Spanish, on every page
  • Stays with you

This is the model at the top of both valleys. The Stockton Group (Compass, the #1 team in the Vail Valley by its own count) runs its own site with neighborhood pages and a live mountain camera. Tom Dunn at LIV Sotheby's, #5 in the valley in 2025, has his own site and newsletter. In Aspen, Saslove & Warwick and Carrie Wells publish their own reports, blogs and guides under their own names. Tim Estin has published his own Aspen market report for twenty years. Each of them also sits inside a strong brokerage.

Rankings and volumes are the agents' own published figures.

02

What your own platform does that the brokerage site can't

1

It sounds like you

No template, no approval queue. The blog is where your judgment shows: the 1031 walkthrough, water rights on the Eagle River, what "membership available" really means at a private club. A buyer who reads three of those knows how you think before the first call.

2

You own it

The domain, the articles, the followers and the traffic history belong to John Tyler Real Estate. That keeps your options open, whatever the next twenty years bring. An agent page on the brokerage site stays with the brokerage.

3

It shows you what the market wants

Every visit is counted: where people came from, what they read, how long they stayed. That is market intelligence you can't get from the brokerage site, and it tells us what to write next (see below).

4

It reaches people others don't

Every page has a Spanish twin. As far as we can see, no other top producer in the valley has a full bilingual platform. Spanish-speaking buyers, sellers and families, here and in Mexico and Latin America, can read about the valley and about you in their own language.

5

It earns trust before the first call

Referrals look you up before they call, and so does anyone weighing a $3 million decision. What they find should confirm what they were told: a Branch Manager with $900M+ in career volume who knows this valley street by street.

6

It reaches the next generation

The son, daughter or grandchild of someone who bought through Carroll or Rick twenty or thirty years ago has never met you. They will search before they ask. The site, and the pages for Carroll and Rick, make sure the search ends with you.

7

It gets you found in Google and AI answers

More buyers now ask Google or ChatGPT "who knows the Vail market?" before they ask a friend. Those answers are built from specific, local, first-hand content, which is exactly what your articles are. A template page that looks like every other broker's gives them nothing to quote.

8

It shows Slifer you're leading

As a Branch Manager, you're showing the firm where the business is heading instead of waiting to be told. A bilingual platform, a monthly market report and a working content engine are things the firm can point to.

One thing the numbers don't show: much of the value lands offline. When your July email went out, the visits it sent showed up as "direct" traffic with no label on them. A past client who reads the market report and calls you a month later looks like a phone call, not a website lead. Judging the site by form fills alone would miss most of what it does.

04

Where it fits while you focus on your database

Putting your energy into your current database over the next few months is the right call. That list is the one marketing asset you own outright, and for a referral business at your level, the lead that matters most is an email address from someone who wants to hear from you. The site doesn't compete with that focus. It's what gives the database something worth opening, and it keeps working on its own while you do.

Attention

Social, QR, email

Posts, business cards, open-house sign-in and the newsletter all point to one page you own.

Proof

The site

The market report, the guides and the blog show them why you're the one to call.

The goal

An email address

"Send me the market report every month." Now they're on your list, not the firm's.

Result

A conversation

When they're ready to buy or sell, the first name they think of is yours.

Send your database to the market report and the guides, and the site will show you which of them are reading and what they care about. When you take another look at the digital side in 2027, the simplest test is how many people it added to your list, alongside calls and inquiries. A sign-up on the site and the Connect page is the one piece it doesn't have yet, and it's worth weighing then.

The arithmetic is short.

Your average transaction is around $3 million. If the platform keeps one past-client family from drifting to another agent, or brings in one buyer who found you through it, it has paid for itself. Everything after that is return.
