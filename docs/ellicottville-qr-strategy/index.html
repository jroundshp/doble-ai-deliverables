The idea, in one paragraph

People standing in your line are, most of the time, in the middle of deciding what to do with their day. So is the guest who just dropped their bags upstairs. A card lets them scan and land on a page built for exactly that question: today's events, the weather, trail and creek conditions, resort webcams, and the local list of what to do in every season. The card carries your name. The visitor gets a better weekend out of it, and a visitor who gets a better weekend comes back to Ellicottville, and back to you.

01 The Question Behind the Counter

Your staff already answers this question, over and over, every weekend. Where's the trailhead. Is the lift running. What's going on tonight. Is there anything to do with the kids if it rains. Where do we park for the festival.

They give good answers, because they live here. But the answers get improvised during a rush, they change depending on who is working that shift, and the person asking is standing between you and the next customer in line. Nobody has time to draw a map on a napkin at 9:15 on a Saturday in October.

The card does not replace that conversation. It gives your staff something to point at, and it gives the visitor something to keep looking at after they walk out with their coffee.

Why this matters more here than in most towns

Ellicottville does not sell one thing. It sells a weekend. Nobody drives two hours for a single stop, and nobody decides to come back because of a single stop either. They come back because the whole weekend worked: they found the trail, they caught the festival, the timing made sense, they were not stuck in the rental scrolling through five different websites trying to figure out what was open. Every business in the village is holding one end of that same weekend.

02 What They Land On

The code does not open a homepage. It opens a single page built for the moment somebody is standing at your counter with a day to fill.

Right now, outsideLive conditions from the National Weather Service, plus links to both resort webcams so people can see the mountain before they drive to it.
Today and this weekendThe events calendar, checked daily against the resort and chamber listings. Festivals, races, live music, rodeo weekends, the whole run of the season.
Where to go outsideMountain biking, hiking, the Finger Lakes Trail, Griffis Sculpture Park, Nannen Arboretum, disc golf, the skatepark, Sky High. Written up properly, not just listed.
Water conditionsLive gauges on Cattaraugus Creek and the Allegheny, so anglers and paddlers know before they load the truck.
The villageEvery restaurant, bar, cafe, and shop in town, with links to their own pages. Where to stay, from slopeside to the campground.
Nothing in the wayNo app to download, no account, no email required, no ads. It opens on any phone and it is free to read.

The hook on the card changes with the season, since the question changes with the season. In February it is conditions and webcams. In July it is trails and the creek. In October it is foliage and the Fall Festival. The printed card never has to change, because only the page behind it does.

Size4 by 6 inches flat, scored and folded into a 4 inch tent. The rental card can go larger, around 5 by 7, since it sits still.
Stock and finishHeavy cover stock, 14 pt or similar, matte rather than gloss. Glossy cards catch the light over a counter and get hard to scan.

QuantityA couple dozen per spot covers a season. They do not wear out, but they do get spilled on and walk off in someone's bag.

The apartment above Alpine is the best placement of the three, and the easiest one to overlook.

The only question is whether they plan it from a page that walks them down to your restaurants, or from a search that sends them somewhere else. Unlike the counter cards, this one gets read for three days instead of thirty seconds.

The card is only part of it. The link should reach the guest three times before they ever see the printed card, because each moment catches a different kind of planning.

1. Right after they bookToo early to plan, but the right moment to plant it. One line: this is the guide we point everyone to. Note that Airbnb is strict about outside links before a booking is confirmed, so this belongs in the message after it is locked in, not in the back and forth before.
2. Three days out, the important oneThis is when they are packing, watching the forecast, and deciding whether the bikes come. A link with one specific reason to open it beats a generic "here's a guide" by a mile.
3. The house manual and guidebookBoth live permanently in the app, so they work without you resending anything. The house manual is what guests actually open in the first ten minutes after they walk in.
4. The card on the counterFor the second and third day, when the messages are buried and somebody is standing in the kitchen with a coffee asking what they should do now.

Why this one closes the loop

This is the placement where all three of your businesses feed each other. A guest sleeping above Alpine, holding a guide that already lists both of your rooms in its Fuel Up section, is the shortest path from a booking to a breakfast to a second visit. It is also the natural place to deliver the coffee and breakfast-sandwich perk from the rental pricing work, since the welcome message is already in their hand.

05 What's In It for All Three
01A better visit is a repeat visit
This is the whole argument. People do not come back to Ellicottville because of one great morning bun. They come back because the weekend worked. Anything that makes the weekend work is marketing for every business in the village, and it lands hardest on the two rooms people start their morning in.
02It answers the question so your staff does not have to
During a rush, "there's a card right there with everything happening this weekend" is a complete answer that takes three seconds. Your staff can still add the local advice on top when there is time for it, which is the part visitors love anyway.
03Your name is on the thing that helped them
The card says who handed them their day. The shop that solved the "what do we do now" problem is the one that gets remembered, gets recommended to the next carload of friends, and gets talked about in the review.
04It makes you the local, not just the coffee
Plenty of places sell a good breakfast. The one that knows the town is a different kind of business. This is a low-effort way to be visibly that place, all season, without anybody having to memorize the events calendar.
05The traffic goes both directions
You are already the two Fuel Up spots on the guide's front page, and the trail and lodging guides point people to the Bake Shop before the ride and Alpine after it. The card sends people to the guide, and the guide keeps sending them back to your counters. With the rental in the mix, all three of your businesses end up pointing at each other through the same card.
06The cost is a small print run
We design them and hand you the finished files for nothing. You print them, which keeps them yours: you decide the stock, the quantity, and when to reprint, and a run this small usually rides along on an order you are already placing. There is nothing to sign and nothing to cancel. If they clutter the counter or nobody touches them, they come off the counter and that is the end of it.
07You get to see what your customers are asking
We can see when people scan and what they look at once they land. If Saturday mornings at the Bake Shop are all events and Sunday afternoons are all trails, that is worth knowing for both of us. We will share it back either way.
06 Straight Talk on How This Works

Worth saying plainly, because a card at your register should not come with a hidden agenda.

We own EllicottvilleOutside.com. It is our site, an independent guide to the town that we built and pay for, not affiliated with Holiday Valley, HoliMont, or the Chamber. More people reading it is good for us, which is the honest reason we would like a card on your counter. That is the entire ask.

In return, the guide works for you. Both restaurants are already featured on it, both of your websites already link back to it, and the card puts that same relationship where the visitors actually are: standing at your register or unpacking upstairs, rather than searching at home three weeks earlier.

Nothing is sold on the card or the page. No email required, no signup wall, no ads, and nothing on it competes with you. If we ever want to change that, we would come ask you first.

You can stop it any time, for any reason or no reason. Hand the cards back and we are done.

07 What Happens Next
01You tell us what your customers actually ask
This is the part we cannot guess at and you already know. The five questions your staff hears most should be the five things on that page, and the ones your rental guests message you about should be right behind them. If we build it around the wrong questions, the card is just clutter.
02We build the page
A single mobile page that answers those questions and nothing else. Most of the pieces already exist on the guide, so this is assembly rather than a build from scratch.
03We send you the artwork, you print one proof
Three print-ready designs, one per location, each with its own code. Run a single proof first and stand it on the actual counter before you order the rest. Wrong size, wrong colors, wrong wording, too big for the space: better to find that out for the price of one card than after a box shows up.
04Cards go out at all three spots
Two counters and the rental, each with its own name on it. The rental also gets the link added to the welcome message, the house manual, and the guidebook, which costs nothing and reaches the guest before the card does. Later we can look at a window cling or a sticker on the to-go bags, which travel with people all day, but these three are the place to start.
05Thirty days later, we show you the numbers
How many scans, which shop, what days, what hours, and what people looked at. Then you decide: keep it, change the wording, or pull it. No part of this needs to be permanent to be worth trying.
