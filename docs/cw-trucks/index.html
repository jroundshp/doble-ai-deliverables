01 · Settle this first

What preference Concrete Warrior actually gets

Every option below depends on one answer from the TERO office, and the rules changed this summer.

The TERO Ordinance (2022) gives preference to firms that are more than 50% Indian-owned. The Regulations the TERO Commission approved on 12 August 2026 go further. They rank certified firms in tiers. Tier 1 is a firm owned by an MHA member, and inside Tier 1 the top level, Self-Performer, requires that the firm be "100% owned and controlled by enrolled members of the Three Affiliated Tribes" (§4.2(A)) confirmed.

Three other tests in the same section matter for any partnership:

  • Profits. "The Indian owner(s) will receive all profits." Any provision giving a non-Indian owner a greater share, "such as through management fees, equipment rental fees, or bonuses tied to profits," means certification is denied (§4.2(A)(3)) confirmed.
  • Management. An Indian owner must be "substantially involved, as a senior level official, in the day-to-day management of the firm as his or her primary employment activity" (§4.2(B)) confirmed.
  • Equipment. On any preference contract the certified firm "must own the equipment," or be buying it with financing or a capital lease "from a disinterested third-party entity" at market terms (§3.2(B)). Equipment leases, financing, staff sharing, revenue sharing and similar agreements with another company "may require submission to and approval by TERO" (§3.2(C)) confirmed.

The Regulations also define a front: "an enrolled Tribal member that, for a fee, uses Indian Preference on behalf of a non-Indian entity" (TERO Regulations, Definitions). Their integrity test asks whether the firm "would have been structured in the manner it is ... if there were no Indian preference program," and looks hard at a non-Indian partner so much larger that it has no reason to take a junior role (§4.1, §4.2(C)) confirmed.

The question to put to TERO in writing: at Concrete Warrior's current ownership, which tier and preference level does it qualify for, and does TERO treat ready-mix as manufacturing (which brings in SBA 8(a) standards under §4.2(D))? ask

The answer decides how much the preference is worth to any partner. The TERO Expo, 19 to 21 October in New Town, opens with an "Update on TERO Regulations" session.

Separate federal rules apply on BIA work, and IHS has a parallel HHS rule (HHSAR 326.6). The Buy Indian Act test is 51% Indian ownership, 51% of the earnings on each contract and Indian control of daily operations, held from bid through the end of the contract confirmed. That is easier to meet than TERO's top level.

02 · The other side of the table

What Knife River is, and what it would want

  • It buys whole companies. Its 10-K says it has "acquired and integrated over 90 complementary businesses" since 1992. It bought four companies for $119.0M in 2024 and five for $622.2M in 2025, $454M of that for Strata. It does hold minority joint venture stakes: its 2025 10-K reports 25 percent and 33 percent interests in joint ventures formed to pool resources on construction contracts. No filing shows a minority stake in a supplier confirmed.
  • It is already licensed on Fort Berthold. Strata, its division, is on TERO's 3 September 2026 list as a non-Native Prime General contractor, licence 27-9089, valid to 29 April 2027, covering construction, reclamation and material supply confirmed. Knife River does not need Concrete Warrior to work on the reservation.
  • Trucks and drivers are its constraint too. The 10-K names "truck, railcar or barge shortages, including shortages of truck drivers" as a risk confirmed.
  • Demand is rising. The 10-K quotes NDDOT's estimate of $745M to $810M in 2026 lettings, against $345M in 2025 confirmed.
  • Decisions are made in the region. Its CFO said on the Q3 2025 call that deal diligence is "done at the local regional level" confirmed.
  • One loose end. When it closed on Strata, Knife River sold four Strata ready-mix plants for $14.5M to a buyer its filings do not name confirmed. Which plants were sold is not public. Worth knowing before any meeting.

Read together: Knife River's minority positions on record are construction joint ventures, not supplier stakes, and it has no need for a reservation licence. What it could use from Concrete Warrior is material, haul capacity and a certified local partner on jobs where preference decides the award.

04 · Trucks for 2027

How many trucks the work needs

The count depends on distance more than on volume. A truck near the plant makes twice the trips of one sent to Watford City.

Assumptions to confirm: 10 yards per load (8 to 9 under spring load limits), 43 minutes per cycle for loading, pouring and washout, plus driving at about 45 mph, and a 10 hour day.

A 1,000 yard job is 100 loads. With four trucks near New Town that is about three days. With five trucks it is just over two days. Pouring it in one day would take about 11 trucks and a plant batching about 100 yards an hour.

Rule of thumb: trucks needed = yards per day ÷ 10 ÷ loads per truck per day, plus one spare.

What a stack could look like

Five used rear-discharge mixers at about $110,000 each is roughly $550,000. That is an assumed price; get two or three dated dealer quotes for rear-discharge trucks that clear the plant (the used quote on file is $110,000 a truck). One version: a bank lends 60% ($330,000) with a BIA or Bank of ND guarantee behind it, the Development Fund lends 30% ($165,000) at 2%, and Concrete Warrior puts in the remaining 10%. The BIA route needs closer to 20% equity, which the MHA loan could help cover. This is an illustration of how the pieces fit, not a quote.

At $330,000 the Bank of North Dakota guarantee drops to 50 percent. The Development Fund charges a 1 percent origination fee confirmed.

Three details a lender will raise

  • Winter. Ready-mix slows sharply in winter. Ask every lender for seasonal or skip payments; PNC Equipment Finance advertises both for mixers through dealer and manufacturer finance programs confirmed.
  • Title. To satisfy TERO's §3.2(B), the trucks should be titled to Concrete Warrior and financed by an unrelated lender at market terms, not leased from a partner or affiliate.
  • Tax. Property acquired after 19 January 2025, used trucks included, qualifies for 100% bonus depreciation confirmed. Used trucks qualify only if not bought from a related party. The older reservation-specific depreciation and employment credits expired after 2021.
06 · Recommended order

What to do, in this order

  1. Get TERO's answer in writing on Concrete Warrior's tier and preference level (section 01). TERO's main line is 701-627-3634. Use the Expo if the call doesn't settle it.
  2. Have the paperwork a lender or partner will check ready: ownership and operating agreement, equipment titles, and signed agreements for the material sites.
  3. Start the truck financing now so the trucks arrive before the 2027 season. Call Native American Bank about a BIA-guaranteed equipment loan. Email the Development Fund. Ask MHA Finance about the business loan and the collateral support program.
  4. Then open Knife River with a commercial offer: aggregate or topsoil supply, overflow hauling, or teaming as the certified local sub on tribal jobs.
