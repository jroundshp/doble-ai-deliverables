01 · Settle this first

What preference Concrete Warrior actually gets

The financing routes and the tribal work both depend on one answer from the TERO office, and the rules changed this summer.

The TERO Ordinance (2022) gives preference to firms that are more than 50% Indian-owned. The Regulations the TERO Commission approved on 12 August 2026 go further. They rank certified firms in tiers. Tier 1 is a firm owned by an MHA member, and inside Tier 1 the top level, Self-Performer, requires that the firm be "100% owned and controlled by enrolled members of the Three Affiliated Tribes" (§4.2(A)) confirmed.

Three other tests in the same rules affect how the trucks are bought and how the company is run:

  • Profits. "The Indian owner(s) will receive all profits." Any provision giving a non-Indian owner a greater share, "such as through management fees, equipment rental fees, or bonuses tied to profits," means certification is denied (§4.2(A)(3)) confirmed.
  • Management. An Indian owner must be "substantially involved, as a senior level official, in the day-to-day management of the firm as his or her primary employment activity" (§4.2(B)) confirmed.
  • Equipment. On any preference contract the certified firm "must own the equipment," or be buying it with financing or a capital lease "from a disinterested third-party entity" at market terms (§3.2(B)). Equipment leases, financing, staff sharing, revenue sharing and similar agreements with another company "may require submission to and approval by TERO" (§3.2(C)) confirmed.

The question to put to TERO in writing: at Concrete Warrior's current ownership, which tier and preference level does it qualify for, and does TERO treat ready-mix as manufacturing (which brings in SBA 8(a) standards under §4.2(D))? ask

The answer decides how much tribal work Concrete Warrior can win on preference, and so how many trucks it can keep busy. The TERO Expo, 19 to 21 October in New Town, opens with an "Update on TERO Regulations" session.

Separate federal rules apply on BIA work, and IHS has a parallel HHS rule (HHSAR 326.6). The Buy Indian Act test is 51% Indian ownership, 51% of the earnings on each contract and Indian control of daily operations, held from bid through the end of the contract confirmed. That is easier to meet than TERO's top level.

What a stack could look like

Five used rear-discharge mixers at about $110,000 each is roughly $550,000. That is an assumed price; get two or three dated dealer quotes for rear-discharge trucks that clear the plant (the only price on file is $110,000 a truck, quoted verbally by a Dodge Center, Minnesota seller on the 4 October call; nothing is in writing yet). One version: a bank lends 60% ($330,000) with a BIA or Bank of ND guarantee behind it, the Development Fund lends 30% ($165,000) at 2%, and Concrete Warrior puts in the remaining 10%. The BIA route needs closer to 20% equity, which the MHA loan could help cover. This is an illustration of how the pieces fit, not a quote.

At $330,000 the Bank of North Dakota guarantee drops to 50 percent. The Development Fund charges a 1 percent origination fee confirmed.

Three details a lender will raise

  • Winter. Ready-mix slows sharply in winter. Ask every lender for seasonal or skip payments; PNC Equipment Finance advertises both for mixers through dealer and manufacturer finance programs confirmed.
  • Title. To satisfy TERO's §3.2(B), the trucks should be titled to Concrete Warrior and financed by an unrelated lender at market terms, not leased from a partner or affiliate.
  • Tax. Property acquired after 19 January 2025, used trucks included, qualifies for 100% bonus depreciation confirmed. Used trucks qualify only if not bought from a related party. The older reservation-specific depreciation and employment credits expired after 2021.
