Concrete Warrior Supply · New Town, North Dakota

The 2027 case

A batch plant, a pit and a topsoil operation already standing on the Fort Berthold Reservation, with one competing plant in town and the next ones 80 to 90 miles away. 2027 is planned as a $20 million revenue year. The near-term constraint is delivery trucks.

01 · What is already in place

Built, not planned

The investment ask is for trucks and working capital. The production side is already on the ground.

  • Batch plant, erected in New Town, rated at 250 cubic yards an hour company. At that rate plant capacity is not the limit on 2027 volume; delivery trucks are.
  • Own sand and rock from the company's pit, so the plant is not buying aggregate from a competitor.
  • Screened topsoil operation with trommels on site and roughly 50,000 cubic yards on hand company, plus access to topsoil from a large landowner north of Williston, in the drilling area company.
  • Crushing and screening equipment from the affiliated Texas operation, Patriot Stone Supply, which runs north in season and south in winter.
  • Licensed to work on the reservation: Concrete Warrior LLC is in good standing with the North Dakota Secretary of State, and holds an MHA Nation TERO license company.
  • Findable and selling: live website and Google Business Profile, and a sales team working from a shared Salesforce account.

In progress: plant photos and drone footage; equipment list for the ND site specifically (the current list is the Texas fleet).

03 · The oilfield around the yard

Half the state's permits are inside 50 miles

Every new well permit in the state is published daily. Placing the last twelve months of them by location:

North Dakota912

On 7 October, 4 of the state's 33 drilling rigs were within 25 miles and another 12 within 50. Drilling runs all year: statewide permits never fell below 54 a month, even in February, while building construction drops to a few percent of the year. Oil traded at $102.54 in the state's September report, against $57 assumed in the state budget.

What a well uses in ready-mix is not published. The best available figures put it at roughly 10 to 35 cubic yards a well (conductor and cellar), or 40 to 200 per multi-well pad. Compressor stations and gas plants elsewhere have taken 2,400 to 5,000+ yards each. On that basis, wells alone within 50 miles support perhaps 70 to 250 yards a week. The oilfield is a steady base load, not the main volume. Downhole well cement is a separate product supplied by service companies and is not counted here.

The nearest active construction is the MHA Nation's Thunder Butte crude pipeline, Makoti to Stanley, approved by the Public Service Commission in May 2025 and about 60% built as of May 2026. No gas plant or compressor station within 50 miles has a 2024–2026 siting filing.

06 · Competition

One plant in town, then a long drive

  • Knife River (NYSE: KNF) runs the only other ready-mix plant in New Town. Concrete Warrior reports it is booked into next summer, with its large customers under contract company.
  • The next plants are in Minot, Williston and Dickinson, 80 to 90 miles and about an hour and a half away. Ready-mix has to be placed within about 90 minutes of batching, so a plant that far out cannot reliably serve New Town.
  • The former New Town ready-mix plant (West Dakota Ready Mix) is permanently closed.
  • Price benchmark: Knife River's 2025 average ready-mix price was $217.81 a yard across its Central segment (which includes North Dakota, Minnesota, Iowa, South Dakota and Texas), and $289.88 in its West segment sourced. Its 10-K credits North Dakota contracting with $109.5 million of revenue growth in 2025.
