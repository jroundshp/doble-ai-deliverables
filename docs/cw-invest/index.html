Financing routes to look at alongside equity

  • BIA Indian Loan Guarantee Program: guarantees up to 90% of a loan to a business at least 51% owned by enrolled tribal members sourced.
  • SBA HUBZone: reservation land qualifies; the firm needs its principal office in a HUBZone and 35% of employees living in one sourced.
  • MHA Business Loan Program: enrolled members only, up to $100,000.
  • Mountain|Plains Regional Native CDFI Coalition: nine Native community lenders across North Dakota, South Dakota, Montana and Wyoming.

Which of these apply depends on how Concrete Warrior is owned (section 07, first risk).

02 · What is already in place

Built, not planned

The production side is already on the ground. The ask is for what moves the product.

  • Batch plant, erected at the yard west of New Town, rated at 250 cubic yards an hour company. At that rate plant capacity is not the limit on 2027 volume; delivery trucks are.
  • Own sand and rock from the company's pit, so the plant is not buying aggregate from a competitor.
  • Screened topsoil operation with trommels on site and roughly 50,000 cubic yards on hand company, plus access to topsoil from a large landowner north of Williston, in the drilling area company.

Unique permit numbers issued 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2026. Eighteen of the 450 inside 50 miles were later cancelled.

On 7 October, 4 of the state's 33 drilling rigs were within 25 miles and another 12 within 50. Drilling runs all year: statewide permits never fell below 54 a month, even in February, while building construction drops to a few percent of the year. West Texas Intermediate was $102.54 on 21 September 2026; North Dakota's market oil price averaged $75.56 in July, against $57 in the state revenue forecast.

What a well uses in ready-mix is not published. The best available figures put it at roughly 10 to 35 cubic yards a well (conductor and cellar), or 40 to 200 per multi-well pad. Compressor stations and gas plants elsewhere have taken 2,400 to 5,000+ yards each. On that basis, wells alone within 50 miles support perhaps 70 to 250 yards a week. The oilfield is a steady base load, not the main volume. Downhole well cement is a separate product supplied by service companies and is not counted here.

The nearest permitted oilfield project is the MHA Nation's Thunder Butte crude pipeline, Makoti to Stanley, approved by the Public Service Commission in May 2025. Its May 2026 report put the project at 60% overall and the pipeline itself at 22%, with no pipeline work between 11 February and 30 May. No gas plant or compressor station within 50 miles has a 2024–2026 siting filing.

05 · Competition

One plant in town, three at the edge

  • Strata, a division of Knife River (NYSE: KNF), runs the only other ready-mix plant in New Town. Concrete Warrior reports it is booked into next summer, with its large customers under contract company. The "West Dakota Ready Mix" listing that shows as closed uses the same address and phone, so it is a former name, not a closed rival.
  • At the edge of the circle, in road miles from the yard: Knife River and Strata in Watford City (42), Dickinson Ready Mix in Killdeer (53), and Ruach Resources in Tioga (56) sourced. Ruach advertises that its plants pour year-round, with heated water. Minot (82), Williston (78) and Dickinson (88) are farther out.
  • What distance does and does not protect. Ready-mix has to be placed within about 90 minutes of batching. Watford City is about an hour from New Town and 52 minutes from the yard, so it can reach town jobs at a haul cost; the plants at the edge compete hardest for work between them and the yard, not inside New Town.
  • Price and margin benchmarks: Knife River's 2025 average ready-mix price was $199.17 a yard company-wide, including delivery; it does not publish prices by region. Its ready-mix gross margin was 17.1%, and crushed stone and sand and gravel averaged $21.55 and $14.39 a ton sourced.
  • Knife River bought Strata, the New Town plant's owner, in March 2025 for $454 million, adding 24 ready-mix plants. It now owns the New Town plant and both Watford City plants. Its 10-K says contracting work in its northern states fell 6% in 2025, partly because of the timing of North Dakota DOT lettings, and quotes NDDOT's estimate of $745 to $810 million of 2026 lettings against $345 million in 2025 sourced.

Downside and break-even

  • Downside case: half the ready-mix volume (20,000 yards) at Knife River's $199 average is $3.98 million of concrete revenue instead of $11.4 million. With topsoil and rock unchanged, the year is about $12.7 million.

09 · The MHA Nation

A large buyer, reached through its contractors

The Mandan, Hidatsa and Arikara Nation received $172.1 million as its share of state oil taxes in the current budget period through July 2026, and it has been putting that money into water plants, clinics, housing and community buildings across the reservation sourced.

That was 13% below the state's forecast of $197.8 million for the period, though July alone ran 42% above. Fort Berthold produces about 13% of North Dakota's oil, and in May the tribal chairman named data centers, natural gas processing and crude oil storage as development priorities.

What the tribe has already built

The spending pattern is the strongest evidence of what comes next. Recent work includes the Mandaree community, senior-living and clinic campus (about $150 million, 196,000 square feet), the Bruce A. Fox water treatment plant in Mandaree (opened August 2026), Fort Berthold Rural Water's New Town office and shop (2025), a family entertainment center in New Town (December 2025), the Community Health Resource Center in New Town (opened 4 June 2026), a casino in White Shield, and a community center and rental housing in Four Bears.

How the tribe buys

  • TERO covers everyone working on the reservation, not only the tribe. The ordinance applies to any entity awarding contracts of $5,000 or more for work mainly on the reservation. Competitive bids are limited to firms TERO certifies as more than 50% Indian-owned; a firm owned by an MHA member that bids within 2% of the low bid wins if it is qualified. Under the regulations, the work can go to open competition only if no certified firm is qualified to do it, and only with the TERO director's written approval sourced.
  • Fees. Construction contracts of $1,000 or more carry a 2.5% TERO fee. For businesses based on the reservation, the ordinance sets a 2.5% quarterly payroll fee and the regulations approved 12 August 2026 set one half of one percent, so that number needs confirming with TERO.
  • Most material is bought by the builders. The general contractors on tribal work are the ready-mix customers: Woodstone (White Shield casino), Rolac Contracting (staff housing), Consolidated Construction (Parshall clinic), Adolfson & Peterson (Mandaree campus), Meyer Contracting and Keller Paving (contracts let March 2025) and Storms Construction. A licensed supplier sells to them directly.
  • Partnership routes exist. TERO provides mentorship and broker agreements for a non-tribal firm working with a tribal one. How Concrete Warrior is owned decides which route applies (section 07).
  • Leadership changes in November. The chairman is not running again; the general election is 3 November 2026. New approvals may slow around the handover.

The public record is a floor. Much of what the tribe has approved is visible only as a resolution title, and the tribe's road program list is not public. The fuller picture has to come from the tribe directly. Questions for those meetings:

  • What construction is approved or funded for the 2027 season, by segment?
  • Which general contractors hold those jobs, and when are 2027 packages released?
  • Do material purchases go out as posted bids, or through the contractors?
  • What is the scope of the short-term-rental and segment housing contracts approved this year?
  • What is on the tribal road program for 2027, and is any of it concrete?

The offices to ask are the tribe's Construction Management office and TERO in New Town, Fort Berthold Rural Water, and the Fort Berthold Housing Authority.
