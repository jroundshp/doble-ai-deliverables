The job

Our owner runs a family of businesses that don't look much alike. There's a restaurant and sports bar in Cooperstown, plus an events venue that reopens for summer. In Ellicottville, a four-season ski town, there's a bistro, a bake shop and a coffee brand. Then there's a fiberglass and waterpark-equipment manufacturer, a construction materials company with yards in Texas and North Dakota, and a youth baseball destination being built in Cooperstown.

Each of them needs the same things: websites that are right, Google listings that are right, a clear message and someone who follows through. Right now that work is spread across too many people and too many inboxes. This job pulls it into one seat.

You'll start where the calendar is busiest: the hospitality businesses, which run on a written winter plan from November to March. Our marketing consultant built that plan and the AI system that runs it, and will train you on both. You'll learn the work first-hand, take it over, then carry the same approach into the other companies. The right person becomes the one the owner counts on for marketing across all of them.

What a week can look like

  • Monday: holiday hours change at two restaurants. Before lunch you've updated both websites, both Google profiles and the counter signs, and checked that nothing anywhere still shows the old hours.
  • Tuesday: you photograph a new lunch item at the bake shop, schedule a week of posts for three businesses and send the gift card email.
  • Wednesday: the manufacturer has a trade show coming. You build a one-page handout and a landing page with a QR code.
  • Thursday: you run the monthly site check across every company's website and fix what it finds.
  • Friday: après in Ellicottville. You're on site for photos and to see what guests actually order.

No two weeks match. If that sounds tiring, this isn't your job. If it sounds like fun, keep reading.

What you'll own

Starting this winter: the hospitality businesses

  • The monthly calendar. One message per property per month, out the same week everywhere a guest looks.
  • Social media, planned and scheduled ahead, built on photos you take on site of the real food, drinks and rooms.
  • Email to each guest list: holiday hours, events, gift cards, the specials worth driving for.
  • In-store marketing: TV menu screens, window menus, table cards and QR codes.
  • Staff briefings, so the people at the counter know what's on this month and what the place down the street offers when we're closed.
  • Events, groups and catering. You promote them and our sales lead books them. You'll work side by side.

Across every company

  • Websites and Google Business Profiles. When anything changes, from an opening time to a phone number or a product, you find every place that still shows the old version and fix it the same day.
  • Sales materials: one-pagers, trade show handouts, landing pages, partner and investor overviews.
  • Search and AI visibility, so each business turns up when someone asks Google or ChatGPT about it.
  • A short monthly report on what ran, what it brought in and what changes next month.

Why AI experience matters here

One person covering this many businesses only works with the right systems. We already run on them: AI drafts, a site sweeper that catches wrong hours, scheduled calendars, audits that score each website. Our stack is Claude and Claude Code working in VS Code, with Grok alongside. You don't need to know all of it on day one. You do need to already use some of it, or be the kind of person who has been itching to.

  • You use AI tools every working day to draft, edit, plan and research, and you can show us how. Claude, Grok and ChatGPT all count.
  • You've opened VS Code, or you want to. Having an AI agent edit a website, run a check or build a report shouldn't scare you off. It should be the part you're most excited to learn.
  • You've built at least one thing that repeats: a prompt library, a custom GPT or Claude project that knows a brand's voice, a calendar template, an automation that sends a form to the right inbox.
  • You check before you publish. Hours, prices, dates and specs get verified against the source, because a wrong hour on Google sends a family to a locked door.
  • You crop, light and resize photos, and you never generate a plate we don't serve or a product we don't make.
  • You can spot an AI-written post and fix it before it goes up.

Tools you'll use or learn: Claude and Claude Code, VS Code, Grok, WordPress, Google Business Profile, Meta Business Suite (Facebook and Instagram), Canva, an email platform, Toast and Square point of sale, Google Analytics and Search Console.
Good to have: GitHub, basic HTML, Zapier or Make, short-form video editing.

Who we're looking for

We'll take curiosity and drive over a long résumé. You might be two years out of school or ten years into marketing. What matters is that you want to learn this and own it.

  • Some marketing or social media experience. Hospitality is a plus, and so is anything B2B.
  • You write clean, warm copy and take a good photo with a phone.
  • You're organized enough to run several calendars at once and to say "not this month" to the next idea.

  • You can be in Ellicottville and Cooperstown every month. They're about four hours apart.
  • You'll take direction while you learn, then tell us when you see a better way.
