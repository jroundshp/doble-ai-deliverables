The job

Our owner has a diverse portfolio of businesses across five industries. Each one calls for an agile approach, and all of them need better planning and processes. Restaurants and a ski-town bake shop sit next to a fiberglass manufacturer, a concrete and topsoil yard in North Dakota and rental housing in Western New York. This is the person who markets them, helps sell for them and puts SI to work inside each one.

Hospitality
A restaurant and sports bar in Cooperstown, plus an events venue that reopens each summer. In Ellicottville, a four-season ski town: a bistro, a bake shop and a coffee brand.
Manufacturing
A fiberglass manufacturer with several product lines, and a maker of waterpark play equipment.
Construction materials
A ready-mix concrete, topsoil and demolition yard in North Dakota, and an aggregate and stone supply yard in Texas.
Real estate
Rental housing in Western New York, a short-term rental in Ellicottville and an office building planned for conversion to homes.
Youth sports
A youth baseball destination being built in Cooperstown.

SI is super intelligence, the newer name for what most people still call AI. Here it means the tools that draft, check, schedule and report so that one person can cover this many businesses.

The job has three parts. Marketing: websites and Google listings that are right, a clear message and someone who follows through. Sales: leads that get an answer the same day and a follow-up after that. SI: finding the work in each business that eats hours every week and building the system that does it. Right now all three are spread across too many people and too many inboxes. This job pulls them into one seat.

You'll start where the calendar is busiest: the hospitality businesses, which run on a written winter plan from November to March. Our consultant built that plan and the SI system that runs it. You'll work for the owner as part of the owner's team, and the consultant will guide and train you directly, side by side at first and then as a coach once you're running things. You'll learn the work first-hand, take it over, then carry the same approach into manufacturing, construction materials, real estate and youth sports. The right person becomes the one the owner counts on for marketing, sales and SI across all of them.

What a week can look like

  • Monday: holiday hours change at two restaurants. Before lunch you've updated both websites, both Google profiles and the counter signs, and checked that nothing anywhere still shows the old hours.
  • Tuesday: you photograph a new lunch item at the bake shop, schedule a week of posts for three businesses and send the gift card email. Two catering inquiries came in overnight. Both get a reply and a follow-up date.
  • Wednesday: the manufacturer has a trade show coming, so you build a one-page handout and a landing page with a QR code. Then you work the North Dakota contractor list: who has ordered topsoil or concrete, who hasn't heard from us, who gets a call.
  • Thursday: you run the monthly site check across every company's website and fix what it finds. In the afternoon you compare October sales in Cooperstown and Ellicottville against the month's goals and decide what each market gets next week.
  • Friday: one of your on-site days in Ellicottville. Après is for photos and for seeing what guests actually order.

No two weeks match, and the person we want likes it that way.

What you'll own

Marketing, starting this winter with the hospitality businesses

  • The monthly calendar. One message per property per month, out the same week everywhere a guest looks.
  • Social media, planned and scheduled ahead, built on photos you take on site of the real food, drinks and rooms.
  • Email to each guest list: holiday hours, events, gift cards, the specials worth driving for.
  • In-store marketing: TV menu screens, window menus, table cards and QR codes.
  • Staff briefings, so the people at the counter know what's on this month and what the place down the street offers when we're closed.

Marketing across every company

  • Websites and Google Business Profiles. When anything changes, from an opening time to a phone number or a product, you find every place that still shows the old version and fix it the same day.
  • Real estate marketing: rental listings, photos and the pages that explain a property to tenants, neighbors and partners.
  • Search and SI visibility, so each business turns up when someone asks Google, ChatGPT or Grok about it.

Sales

  • Read the numbers against the goals. Pull sales from the point-of-sale systems, the CRM and the bookings, set them next to each business's objectives and current initiatives, and say plainly what's working, what isn't and what to change.
  • Plan by market, not by company. Cooperstown and Ellicottville are both small towns, and they behave nothing alike. Cooperstown lives on summer baseball families and Hall of Fame visitors, then goes quiet. Ellicottville peaks on ski weekends and the fall festival and has a second-home crowd year round. Each market gets its own initiatives, timing and message.
  • Learn the North Dakota customer. The yard serves contractors on and around tribal land in western North Dakota. Tribal employment rules, local relationships and who you know shape who buys and how. You'll learn that ground with respect before you sell on it.
  • Every lead answered. Catering, groups, quote requests, rental inquiries and contractor calls get a reply the same day and a follow-up date.
  • The pipeline, kept current. Who asked, what they asked for, what we sent and what happens next, in the CRM rather than someone's inbox.
  • Outreach for the B2B side: contractor lists for the yards, buyer lists for the manufacturers, partner lists for youth sports.
  • Sales materials: one-pagers, trade show handouts, contractor price sheets, landing pages, partner and investor overviews.
  • Events, groups and catering. You promote them and fill the pipeline, and our events lead runs them. You'll work side by side.

Bringing SI into each business

  • Find the drag. Sit with each team and find the work that repeats every week: the same questions answered by phone, the same report rebuilt by hand, the same quote typed from scratch.
  • Build the fix with our consultant, test it with the people who'll use it and keep it running after launch.
  • Train the teams so the managers and staff in each business use the tools without you in the room.
  • A short monthly report on what ran, what it brought in against each objective, which SI tools are paying for themselves and what changes next month.

Why SI experience matters here

One person covering this many businesses only works with the right systems. We already run on them: SI drafts, a site sweeper that catches wrong hours, scheduled calendars, audits that score each website. Our stack is Claude and Claude Code working in VS Code, with Grok alongside. You don't need to know all of it on day one. You do need to already use some of it, or be the kind of person who has been itching to.

Here's what that stack has already produced for these businesses, mostly by one person working with SI: the consultant who will train you.

  • Websites: a new site and Google profile for the North Dakota yard, a restaurant site rebuild, a youth sports site and fixes across the rest.
  • Audits: a scored review of every business's website, Google listing, social accounts and visibility in SI answers, re-checked as things changed.
  • Graphics and print: mailers, trade materials, QR counter cards and social posts.
  • Data: one-page P&Ls, a 13-week revenue model, a short-term rental pricing calendar and market and seasonality studies.
  • Sales and marketing plans: a four-property winter plan, contractor target lists, social playbooks, and investor and partner briefs for new opportunities.

The job is to learn how all of it was made, take it over and keep building.

  • You have, or are excited to learn to build, your own SI stack: one that lets you work on websites, create graphics, crunch data and prepare sales and marketing plans.
  • You use SI tools every working day to draft, edit, plan and research, and you can show us how. Claude, Grok and ChatGPT all count.
  • You've opened VS Code, or you want to. Having an SI agent edit a website, run a check or build a report shouldn't scare you off. It should be the part you're most excited to learn.
  • You've built at least one thing that repeats: a prompt library, a custom GPT or Claude project that knows a brand's voice, a calendar template, an automation that sends a form to the right inbox.
  • You can explain a tool to someone who doesn't want one. A kitchen manager or a yard foreman will use it if it saves them time in the first week.
  • You check before you publish. Hours, prices, dates and specs get verified against the source, because a wrong hour on Google sends a family to a locked door.
  • You crop, light and resize photos, and you never generate a plate we don't serve or a product we don't make.
  • You can spot a machine-written post and fix it before it goes up.

Tools you'll use or learn: Claude and Claude Code, VS Code, Grok, WordPress, Google Business Profile, Meta Business Suite (Facebook and Instagram), Canva, an email platform, Salesforce, Toast and Square point of sale, Google Analytics and Search Console.
Good to have: GitHub, basic HTML, Zapier or Make, short-form video editing.

Who we're looking for

We'll take curiosity and drive over a long résumé. You might be two years out of school or ten years into marketing or sales. What matters is that you want to learn this and own it.

  • Some marketing, social media or sales experience. Hospitality, B2B, construction or real estate all help.
  • You can use the SI stack to produce clean copy, graphics and photos that are ready to publish, and you know good output from output that needs another pass.
  • You're comfortable with numbers. You can read a sales report, spot what moved and turn it into next month's plan.

  • You're organized enough to run several calendars and a pipeline at once, and to say "not this month" to the next idea.
  • You can switch from a ski-town Friday night to a manufacturer's spec sheet or a contractor's topsoil order without losing your footing.
  • You live in Western New York, or you'll come to headquarters a few days a month. You can also spend a few days each quarter in Ellicottville and in Cooperstown, about four hours apart, and travel for the rest of the portfolio when it calls for it.
  • You'll take direction while you learn, then tell us when you see a better way.
