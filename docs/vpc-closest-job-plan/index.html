VPC company AI system: the master plan

One company account, holding VPC's own data, that new workflows get added to over time. Engineering goes first. Other departments come in behind it.

First workflow: the closest-job index for metering manholes and flumes, built with Larry and written up in the submittal library concept. The website work order is a separate project and neither one waits on the other.

What we are trying to do

Three objectives, in the order they matter. Everything further down is a means to one of them.

  • Objective 01Streamline quotingPut AI on the part of quoting and submittal work that is retrieval and retyping rather than engineering. That is where the hours sit, and it is the part a machine can actually take.
  • Objective 02Build a structure, not a toolOne company account and one master plan that other departments and other staff move onto as it proves out, so the second workflow costs a fraction of the first.
  • Objective 03Turn it into an assetSomething worth more to VPC every month it runs. Each job that passes through makes the next search better, and the knowledge stays with the company as people come and go.

The third objective is the one that outlives the other two, and it is the reason the ownership section sits where it does rather than at the back.

03

What Doble AI is for

Fair question, and worth answering before the plan rather than after it.

Corey could set up the account himself this afternoon. Buying a corporate Grok Bot organisation and issuing seats takes about an hour and does not need us. If that were the whole plan, we would say so and stop.

The account is not the system. What does not exist until somebody builds it:

The indexEvery past job read, parsed and fingerprinted, with the misspellings normalised so a search finds all seventeen jobs instead of thirteen. This is software, and it does not come with a subscription.
The joinThe Salesforce order and the server drawing brought together, which is the thing that currently only happens inside Larry's head.
The scoringDeciding what makes two jobs similar, then changing it when Larry says the ranking is wrong. Judgement, tested against his, not a setting in a menu.
The handoverCompany accounts, a second seat once the tool goes daily, a runbook, and a re-index anybody at VPC can run. The unglamorous part, and the part that decides whether VPC owns an asset or rents a habit.
The word noTelling VPC when to stop. Do not buy CPQ software, do not chase CAD generation, do not dump the PDF library into a chatbot. A vendor paid to expand scope does not say that.

Where this sits commercially. The first workflow is inside the current arrangement, not a new line item. Beyond it, each additional department is its own build with its own decision, and the honest version of that is: if there is no next workflow worth building, there is nothing to bill for. VPC keeps everything built up to that point either way.

What the account costs. A per-seat monthly subscription billed to VPC, plus our build time. The seat count starts at one, Larry's, and grows only as the system earns it: a second when the tool goes daily, and somewhere between two and four seats by the six-month mark if other people are getting value from it. Nobody pays for a seat before there is something on the other end of it.

We are not putting a seat rate on this page. The published figures for these platforms and the figures on an actual invoice have a habit of disagreeing, and a number Corey plans around should come from the vendor rather than from us. We will confirm the current rate in writing before anything is signed.

And if Corey would rather have his own people run this, that is a real option and we would rather he took it than paid for something he can do internally. The index, the scoring and the runbook are documented as they are built, precisely so that route stays open.

04

The problem it solves

Turnaround is not slow because anybody is inventing a new product. Five to ten submittals a week, three to five hours each on a metering manhole, and roughly sixty to seventy percent of one already sitting in a job finished months ago. The time goes into finding that job.

The search fails quietly. Ask the drive for ultra bkt and thirteen of the seventeen jobs come back. Four are typed ulta bkt or ultra kkt and stay invisible, with nothing on screen to say they are missing. An index that normalises as it reads finds all seventeen, and nobody renames a file.

Hiring another engineer buys drafting hours. It does not buy retrieval, because a new person has less of the history in their head than Larry does.
