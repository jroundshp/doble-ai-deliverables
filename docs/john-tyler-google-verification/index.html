That is the only requirement flagged. Nothing about your name not being on the building, nothing about whether a broker is allowed to have a profile, nothing about the brokerage. Read the flagged line closely, because it is narrower than it sounds. It does not say you failed to film outside. It names three specific things: street signs, neighboring businesses, recognizable landmarks. Filming the outside of your own building, even well, satisfies none of the three, because none of them prove where that building is. A reviewer looking at the Slifer sign and a facade has no way to place it on the map. That is the gap, and it is a narrow one.

Lionshead makes this harder than it would be on a normal street. If that is the case at your door, the fix is not to film more of the building. It is to start the take further out, at the nearest point where a street name sign and a couple of neighboring business names are actually legible, and then walk in from there without stopping the recording. It is one continuous take, so the walk costs you nothing except starting sooner than feels natural.

If it comes back refused again, stop filming

A fourth video will not fix a problem the video is not causing. At that point it becomes a paperwork exercise, and both routes have limits worth knowing in advance.

  1. Open a support case through the verification tool

    Run the verification flow to the end. When no verification option is offered, it lets you create a case, describe the situation and attach evidence. That reaches a person instead of an automated check, which is what you want after three automated refusals.

  2. Use the appeals tool, with the documents ready beforehand

    There is a trap in this one. After you submit the appeal, Google opens a separate evidence form, and anything not submitted inside sixty minutes never gets attached. Have the files on your desktop before you start. You also cannot file a second appeal for the same issue while one is pending, and appeals are limited, so the first one has to be the good one.

    • Your Colorado real estate broker license
    • A letter on Slifer letterhead confirming your role at that address, worded with the same title your Slifer profile uses
    • A phone, internet or utility bill showing the same name and address as the profile
    • Photographs of the exterior signage and your workspace

    Every document has to carry the same address as the profile, which is why the address section comes first.

  3. Last resort, escalate in the Business Profile community forum

    Post the details and a Google Product Expert can push it to the internal team for a final look. Slower, but it works on cases the automated system keeps refusing.
