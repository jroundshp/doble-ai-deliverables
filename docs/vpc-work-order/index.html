VPC Website Work Order

What needs doing on the three WordPress sites, what has to be true before anyone touches anything, and two ways to run it.

This hour is the website only. The quoting and submittal work with AI is a separate session with a separate document, and nothing here depends on it.

The objective

When a buyer goes looking, VPC's own pages should be what comes back, and what they say should be right.

Where they look Google ChatGPT Perplexity Gemini Claude Grok Copilot
  • FindableThe pages exist, they get crawled, and nothing points where it should not.
  • ReadableA machine can tell what VPC makes and who it is for. The spec sheets are good. The markup is not, so the information sits on the page and stays invisible to whatever is reading it.
  • AccurateWhere the site contradicts itself, or the best description of a VPC product sits on somebody else's platform, the record VPC owns should be the right one.

This is a qualification play, not a traffic play. Generic visitors are worth nothing to VPC. The aim is that the few people searching for an FRP equipment enclosure or an ADA pool stair land on VPC.

04

Is a rebuild coming?

I have heard the site described as six or seven years old and probably near the end of its life. If that is right it changes what we should be doing, and it is much better to know in September than in November.

A rebuild is the one moment where findability is either designed in or thrown away, and thrown away is the default. URLs change, redirects get missed, and years of accumulated search history goes with them. It is rarely carelessness. A builder is thinking about how the new site looks and reads, because that is what a builder is hired to think about.

If a replacement is coming in the next few months, the list below stops being markup and becomes the requirements handed to whoever builds it.

05

Before anything changes

  • A full backup, files and database, stored somewhere other than the server itself. A host-only snapshot dies with the host account.
  • Confirmation it can be restored or at least downloaded, the name of the tool taking it, and a name against who checks that the restore actually works. We would rather settle the rollback path before we need it than during.
  • Retention held for the length of the work, rather than a rolling seven days that ages out halfway through.
  • A child theme, if there is not one already. The og:image, the Twitter card and the schema all live in template files, and a direct theme edit gets wiped by the next theme update. Clear WP Rocket after each change.
  • If a staging site gets spun up for any of this, it needs noindex and a password. See above for why I am fussy about that one.

On our side, before we touch anything we take a full render and crawl baseline of all three WordPress sites, every page, hashed. That is the honest answer to what happens if something breaks.

One flag on item 9. A few of the play product pages disagree with themselves. One addition to item 9 that costs nothing. All 24 play product pages already carry a visible FAQ block, the "Got Questions? We've Got Answers!" panel, and none of it is marked up. Everything on that list that does not require a decision from VPC is about a day of work.

07

What waits on an answer from VPC

  • Any FAQ answer with a number in it. NEMA ratings, lead time on a custom shelter. We do not publish a spec nobody gave us.
  • Product page copy about how VPC actually sells. Who each line is for in your words, and whether a buyer is configuring from standard sizes or commissioning to a drawing.
  • Two doors in the navigation. Plant and municipal FRP on one side, EasyRec and InterActive Play on the other. This moves live menus and URLs, which is precisely where indexing gets dropped, so it needs agreement before it needs work.
  • The blog. As of 10 September there is nothing in it at all, and the page tells a visitor so in those words. Whether that gets restored, redirected or retired is a VPC decision, and it is now a bigger one than the approval we asked for a week ago.

Three more are not website credentials at all and are not for today: Lockport's Google profile publishes an 888 number that appears nowhere on the site, Amherst returns no profile in Maps, and Medina is sitting on three or four reviews after a decade of municipal customers.

08

On Thomas, and what I am not saying

Three things cut against the obvious reading, and they belong on the table before anyone forms a view.

VPC sits sixth on page one of the FRP Shelters category on ThomasNet, out of 609 suppliers, below two sponsored listings, and without paying for placement. The two paying for the slots above it are Engineered Fiberglass Composites and ArmorCore by Waco Composites. Confirmed 9 September. The listing is a working asset and nothing here argues against it.

The catalog is not blank. Its product pages carry structured data as microdata, between 3 and 20 attributes per product. What is wrong is the quality: the product name picks up breadcrumb text, and pricing markup appeared on one of the five items I sampled. Cleanup, not construction.

The ThomasNet listing carries better product copy than vpcfiberglass.com does. Verbatim from the profile: enclosures available in 29 x 29 to 72 x 72 in. sizes, suitable for samplers, switchgear transformers, radio repeaters, chlorination and emergency spill equipment, well heads, flow measurement tools and instrumentation. Dimensioned, twelve named applications, and nothing like it sits on the site VPC owns.

So this stops being a writing project. It is bringing that copy home.

One structural question rather than a conclusion I have already reached. One company builds the site, hosts the catalog and sells the placement. Worth asking what happens to the catalog and the content inside it if that arrangement ever changes, and getting the answer in writing while the relationship is a good one.

09

Two ways to run it

Not a rebuild. Not a change of vendor. Thomas built the best site in this portfolio and our own audit says so in writing. The only real question is who does the work.

Option A

We hand Amanda the work order

Every item written as a task with the exact markup, the file it goes in, and what to check afterward. Nobody outside VPC touches the site.

What it needs Roughly a day of Amanda's time across the three sites.

Option B

Amanda hands us a login

A named admin account on the three WordPress sites, revocable in one click. We do the work, validate every block against what the page actually says, screenshot before and after, and re-crawl to prove it landed rather than tell you it did.

What it needs The backup above, an account, and about an hour of review at the end.

There is a middle version if that suits better. We stage everything, Amanda reviews it, Amanda presses the button.

Whichever way it goes, the thing I actually need from Corey is whether VPC is where those hours go over the next few weeks. If it is not, that is a good answer too, and I would rather have it than a maybe.
