01 Objective
The objective, stated plainly

The website, the Google Business Profile and every other digital asset built here exist for one purpose: to be a sales tool.

A place a decision maker can go to understand who this company is, see exactly what it sells and to what specification, see the certifications and credentials that prove it can be trusted with the work, and come away confident enough to buy. Every requirement in this document is downstream of that. If something on the site does not help a buyer understand the company, evaluate the material, or gain confidence, it does not belong in the build.

Two things follow from stating it that way. First, this is not a brochure and it is not a branding exercise — it is the thing that has to do the selling when nobody is on the phone, which in this trade is most of the time. Second, confidence is the actual product of the site. This is a new operation with no track record, no reviews and no history, competing against companies that have been in the basin for decades. Nothing on the site can manufacture years of operating history, so it has to substitute the things that genuinely stand in for it: published specifications, published prices, named credentials, real photographs of real equipment doing real work, and answers with enough depth that a reader concludes these people know the trade. Depth reads as substantial to this buyer. Breadth reads as scattered.

Concrete Warrior Supply operates a ready mix concrete batch plant and an aggregate operation at 9702 ND-23, New Town, North Dakota, on the Fort Berthold Reservation. The legal entity is Concrete Warrior LLC, a licensed contractor with the MHA Nation. It sells ready mix concrete, crushed concrete, road gravel and topsoil into both tribal and non-tribal markets.

Who the buyer is
Not homeowners. Project managers, dispatchers, excavation and site contractors, oil field service companies, tribal programs and public works. Often standing on a job site, on a phone, with poor signal.
What they want to know
Three things, in this order: do you have the material, can you get it to my site this week, and what does it cost. Every layout decision should serve that sequence.
The lead product
Ready mix concrete. There is no other batch plant in New Town, and concrete's roughly ninety minute placement window makes proximity decisive. Aggregate, scoria, crushing, recycling and topsoil follow behind it.
Definition of success
A contractor within delivery range who needs concrete or aggregate finds this business, gets a real answer including a price range, believes it, and calls. Measured by calls and quote requests, not by traffic.
The single most important constraint

This is a market where almost no competitor publishes anything useful. The advantage available here is specificity: real material specs, real price brackets, real photographs, real answers to real questions. A handsome site with vague copy loses to a plain site with numbers on it.

Bidders should read that as licence to spend effort on content structure rather than on visual novelty.

02 Name And Domain — Resolved
Decided August 13, 2026

Trading name: Concrete Warrior Supply.
Domain: concretewarriorsupply.com, verified available at the registry on August 13, 2026.

Legal entity remains Concrete Warrior LLC. The trading name adds one word; it does not change the filing, and the entity name is what goes on any TERO application.

Why Supply, and not the shorter name

  • The business sells four material lines and three of them are not concrete — crushed concrete, road gravel and topsoil alongside ready mix. A concrete-only name undersells three quarters of the catalogue, and it works hardest against topsoil, which is the least contested product off the reservation. Nobody searching for reclamation topsoil clicks a company called Concrete Warrior.
  • Nobody else in this market puts a material in the name. Ruach Resources, Knife River, Fisher Industries, Strata, Triple C. Aggregate businesses expand across materials and the name has to outlive the product mix.
  • It passes the truck door test. Three plain words, no spelling required on a phone call. A domain carrying a state abbreviation has to be spelled out every time it is spoken, and every spelling-out is a chance to lose the call.
  • The brand equity survives. Everyone still says Concrete Warrior. Supply signals a materials yard rather than a concrete contractor.

Two prerequisites that sit ahead of the build

1. Entity standing. The North Dakota Secretary of State registry currently returns Concrete Warrior LLC as Inactive – Involuntary, Not Good Standing, and the state's own portal shows the record eligible for reinstatement. In North Dakota that status almost always means a missed annual report rather than anything substantive. We could not retrieve the registered agent on the record to confirm it is this entity, because the Secretary of State's detail view is currently erroring for every record on the site, so this is worth one call to 701-328-2900 rather than an assumption. A dissolved entity complicates verifying a Google listing, signing contracts and holding a licence, so it wants settling first.

2. The Google Business Profile is the slowest item in the project. It asks for a website address during setup and verification on rural tribal land can take weeks and may need a second attempt. Register the domain and stand up a holding page with name, phone, address and service area before the site is built, so the listing can be filed against something. Primary category: Sand and gravel supplier. Never append keywords or the town to the listing name — it is a leading cause of suspension.

Standing rules

  • Registrant is the client. The domain is registered in the client's name, on the client's account, with the client holding the credentials. Never in a vendor's account.
  • Buy the obvious misspellings and redirect them. A few dollars a year, and it stops somebody else parking on them later.
  • One brand, not two. Rhino Ready Mix, LLC also exists. Trading under two names means two sites, two Google listings and two sets of local signals, roughly doubling the ongoing effort for no ranking benefit. This spec assumes one.
  • Name overlap to be aware of. A separate company, Warrior LLC, is an established contractor on Fort Berthold. It is not a competitor — it holds no aggregate or concrete categories — but the two names will sit near each other in front of the same people who award work. Worth raising directly with them rather than discovering later, and worth mentioning to TERO. It is a reason to keep the full three-word name in use consistently.
03 Site Structure

Roughly 24 pages at launch. The rule throughout: one page per thing somebody searches for. A single page trying to cover nine materials ranks for none of them.

Product lines confirmed August 12, 2026 — and topsoil splits in two

The business sells ready mix concrete, crushed concrete, road gravel and topsoil, into both tribal and non-tribal markets.

Topsoil needs two pages, not one. It is the same material out of the same pit, but it is two different sales. An oil and gas operator is buying proof the soil is not brine damaged and wants lab numbers: electrical conductivity, sodium adsorption ratio, pH, organic matter. A tribal, federal or public bid buyer is buying a county inspector's weed free certificate under N.D.C.C. 4.1-47. Different numbers, different vocabulary, different search terms. One page attempting both does neither.

Crushed concrete is not road gravel and gets its own page and its own argument. The batch plant generates returned loads and washout, which are feedstock, so the material comes out of the operation's own waste stream. It is cheaper than virgin aggregate for the buyer and nobody else in this market is marketing it.

Rules that apply to every page

  • Tappable phone number in the header, on every page, above the fold. Half this traffic is someone standing in dirt.
  • Unique title tag and meta description per page. No templated duplicates, no blanks.
  • A quote request path reachable from every page in one tap.
  • Minimum five FAQ questions per commercial page, marked up as FAQ structured data. This is the primary driver of being quoted by AI assistants and it is where the whole market is currently at zero.
  • No page ships with placeholder copy. If the client input is missing, the page does not launch.

Material and service pages, required contents

  • What it is, in plain language, and what it is normally used for
  • Gradation or spec, including the NDDOT class where one applies
  • Price bracket, per ton or per cubic yard [TBC]
  • Typical quantity guidance, for example tons per truckload and coverage
  • At least one real photograph of that material at this yard
  • Five FAQs with structured data
  • On the reclamation topsoil page specifically: the lab numbers, published. Electrical conductivity, sodium adsorption ratio, pH, organic matter and coarse fragments. Bakken reclamation is a salt problem rather than a weed problem, so an operator is checking the material is not saline or sodic. Published figures of the form "EC 1.2, SAR 1.8, pH 7.4, 4% organic matter, weed free certified under NDCC 4.1-47" are the single most citable thing this site can carry, and no competitor in the region publishes anything like it. [TBC — client to supply, and to confirm the numbers may be published]
Why concrete leads, and why that is structural rather than a marketing choice

West Dakota Ready Mix in New Town is permanently closed. The nearest operating plants are Tioga at roughly sixty miles and Dickinson and Bowman at 150 to 200. Concrete has a placement window of about ninety minutes, which makes distance decisive in a way it never is for gravel. A plant at New Town is therefore the only practical option for most of Fort Berthold. That is a position built on geography, and no competitor answers it without building a plant.

The same reading holds from a second direction: on the published MHA TERO preference lists there is no concrete or ready mix category at any level. Aggregate, reclamation, trucking, erosion control and road and pad construction are all represented. Concrete is absent entirely.

On service area pages

Build them only for towns genuinely served, and write each one differently. Thin near duplicate pages generated for every town within a hundred miles are treated as doorway pages, get filtered, and can drag down the rest of the site. Four real pages beat thirty generated ones.

Concrete and aggregate have different radii and the pages should say so. Concrete is limited by the placement window; aggregate is limited by freight cost.

Non negotiable: the business name, address and phone

The exact same business name, street address and phone number string must appear identically in the site footer, in the structured data, on the contact page and on the Google Business Profile. Byte for byte identical.

This sounds pedantic and it is the single most common cause of a local business failing to rank in its own town. Inconsistency here will undo a great deal of otherwise good work, so it is called out as an acceptance criterion rather than left to care.

05 Content, Photography And Tone

Photography

Real photographs only. No stock imagery and no AI generated images. This is a firm requirement, not a preference.

The client will supply roughly thirty daylight phone photographs of the plant, the pit, stockpiles, trucks, loading and finished pours. Slightly rough, real photographs of this yard outperform polished stock photography in this trade, because they are the one thing a competitor cannot copy and a template cannot fake. The builder is responsible for cropping, compressing and sizing them properly, not for replacing them.

Shot list, in priority order

  • Wide establishing shot of the yard — the homepage image
  • The crusher running, with material moving. A crusher sitting idle is worth a fraction of one working
  • The batch plant, and a mixer truck discharging on a pour
  • Separate stockpiles, one clearly identifiable per product. Scoria especially, since it is red and instantly distinguishable
  • The trommel screening topsoil, and screened material placed on a reclamation site if one exists
  • The feedstock pile of broken slab and returns — this is the recycling argument in a single image
  • A loader filling a truck, and trucks arriving at a site. Delivery is most of what is actually being bought
  • Crews working, with their consent
  • Something with a person or a truck in frame for scale. Stockpiles photograph as beige lumps without it
Three checks before any photograph is published

1. Location. Every image on this site must be this operation. Photographs from the affiliated Texas yard do not go on this site, however good they are. They misrepresent the yard and they undercut the point of the site being unambiguously about one place.

2. Branding on the equipment. Check truck doors, hard hats and signage in every frame. Equipment that arrived from the affiliated operation and still carries its livery puts another company's brand on this one's website. Reframe, retake or leave it out.

3. Safety. Anything showing a person without a hard hat or in an unsafe position does not go up. Buyers running ISNetworld and MSHA Part 46 compliance read photographs as evidence of how a site is operated.

Every image needs its location, approximate date and photographer recorded when it is handed over. That context cannot be reconstructed afterwards, and nothing gets captioned by guesswork.

Tone

  • Plain, specific, trade literate. Numbers and specs over adjectives.
  • No filler superlatives. "Premier provider of quality solutions" is worse than "Class 5, $14 a ton, delivered within 40 miles."
  • Do not overstate capability or capacity. Everything published must be defensible to a contractor who will notice.
Two copy rules that are not stylistic, and are not negotiable

1. Describe experience, not corporate structure. There is an affiliated aggregate and recycling operation in Texas, and the experience its crews bring north is real and worth stating. State it as capability: "our crews have been crushing, recycling and hauling aggregate in Texas since [year]." Do not state it as structure: "the North Dakota division of a Texas company." Those describe the same underlying fact, and only the second one causes a problem. The relationship is a joint venture between separate companies and should be described that way, in prose, on the About page. No shared navigation, no combined "our locations" page, no shared logo or brand system, and no visual twinning between the two sites. If the two sites look like siblings, the design has made the structural claim that the copy carefully avoided.

2. Make no claim about ownership or certification status until it is granted in writing. Certification questions with MHA TERO are open at the time of writing. Nothing on the site, in the Google listing, or in any profile asserts a preference status, a certification or an ownership characteristic that has not been formally issued. When something is granted, the wording is the exact wording of the credential and nothing broader. Overstating this is the one error on this project that would be genuinely hard to recover from, because the audience for the claim is also the body that verifies it.

06 Scope Boundaries
In scope
The ~24 pages. Six answer articles at launch. Structured data, metadata, sitemap, analytics, Search Console. Quote form. Photo processing. Handover docs and training.
Quote separately
Google Business Profile creation and verification. Trade directory listings. The four later articles. Ongoing content. Logo and brand identity, if none exists.
Explicitly out
Online ordering or payment. Customer portal or account logins. A Spanish version. Live dispatch or scheduling integration. Anything requiring a back office system that does not exist yet.
Why no Spanish version

We build bilingual sites routinely and it is the right call in many markets. It is not right here. The buyer for concrete and aggregate on Fort Berthold is English speaking and the local community is the MHA Nation. A Spanish version would be effort spent on nobody. If a bidder proposes one, treat it as padding.

Questions worth asking every bidder

Will you publish prices? Many builders will resist. In this market it is one of the strongest advantages available, and reluctance here signals a template rather than a strategy.

How do you handle FAQ structured data? If the answer is vague, the site will not get quoted by AI assistants, which is where a meaningful share of this market is heading.

Who writes the material specs? Whoever it is needs to be comfortable being corrected by Richard. Wrong specs in front of a spec buyer are worse than no specs.

08 Acceptance Criteria

The build is complete when all of the following are true and demonstrable. This list exists so that "done" is not a matter of opinion.

  • Every page has a unique title tag and meta description
  • Structured data passes Google's Rich Results Test with zero errors
  • Home page and one material page hit LCP under 2.5s on simulated mobile 4G
  • Pinch to zoom works
  • Business name, address and phone are byte identical across footer, contact page, structured data and Google listing
  • A test quote submission arrives in the monitored inbox and triggers the autoresponder
  • A test call from a mobile registers as a conversion in GA4
  • Sitemap submitted in Search Console with no coverage errors
  • No stock or AI generated imagery anywhere on the site
  • All six launch articles published, each with five FAQs marked up
  • No placeholder or lorem ipsum text on any live page
  • Client holds admin access to domain, hosting, analytics and Search Console

Before sending this to anyone, please read this

This document, and especially the competitive landscape document that accompanies it, contain original research into this market: where the gaps are, which competitors are weak and exactly how, which categories are empty, and where the openings sit. That research took real work and it is currently an advantage because nobody else has it.

Handing it to three web agencies means handing it to three companies who work for other clients, possibly one day in this industry. It is not confidential in a legal sense and there is nothing scandalous in it. But it is a competitive asset, and it stops being one the moment it is widely distributed.

Our suggestion: send the build specification to bidders, which is sections 02 through 08 and contains what a builder actually needs. Keep the competitive landscape document inside the group. A builder does not need to know why the market is open in order to quote the pages, and the reasoning is the part worth protecting.
